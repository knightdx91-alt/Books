# PRESENT (working title)
*Horror — time-loop school / the dead who don't know*

Seed: author's dream, 2026-09-13. Captured raw + developed. Not yet a book folder.

Alt titles: *Attendance* · *Nobody Is Absent* · *The Long Thursday* · *Substitute*

---

## The dream (author's words, preserved)

> It is a school that does the same thing like in a time loop everyday. A teacher tries to
> replace a teacher that had a child, but there is a twist. It is like an unbreakable spell
> on everyone and nothing changes in the school or the people in it every day. They go home
> and go back, but it is like the same day every day.
>
> The twist is everyone is dead. The adults are vampires but never feed or do anything but
> the same day. The kids in the school are dead but don't know it — they still act normal.
>
> The human woman who tries to get in the school by replacing a teacher later has to leave,
> as her reason for being there is to replace a teacher who is out for the day — but she
> shows up like normal, and it becomes a big thing as people start trying to figure out what
> is going on in the school and with the people who seem to have stopped living normal lives
> outside.
>
> She was shocked when the woman appeared, when she had sent the woman she replaced away to
> find her son that was alive outside the place.

Author note: woke before the protagonist found much out. No ending yet.

## The engine

The story's spine is the dream's own last image: **she sends the replaced teacher away to find
her living son, and the next morning that teacher is back at her desk with her roll sheet,
with no memory of having left.**

That establishes the core rule: **the loop does not repeat time. It repeats people.** Going home
is part of the performance. The loop extends exactly as far as it must to be convincing and no
further. The road out of town bends back.

## Why a substitute can get in

A substitute is the only living person the day can admit. A permanent hire would require a
tomorrow. A substitute exists for one day only — she is precisely the shape of the hole.

The loop lets her in, and then cannot heal over her.

## The twist under the twist

Vampires who never feed. Children who don't know they're dead. The *why* is the real story:

**The loop is an act of love, and that is what makes it unforgivable.**

The faculty were already what they are. Something killed the children (fire / gas / bus /
flood — choose late, keep it mundane). The adults could not save them, so they built a single
day and refused to let it end. Feeding requires a tomorrow; the abstinence is not virtue, it is
the price of the ritual. Nobody grows, nobody graduates, nobody is buried.

They are not jailers. They are parents who would not sign the paperwork.

Which reframes the protagonist: she is not escaping monsters. She is the woman who came to
switch off the machine that keeps someone's child "alive."

## Rules of the day

- **Nobody is ever absent.** Every child answers roll, every day, forever — except one desk.
  That desk is the thread.
- **Blood ends it.** Living blood on the floor is the one thing the day cannot process. A paper
  cut, a nosebleed — the building *notices*.
- **Her changes persist, and they should not.** Day 1 she moves a chair; day 3 it is still where
  she left it. The loop is degrading around her, and the faculty register it before she does.
  This is the escalation clock — the story has a ticking element, not just dread.
- **Seams, not gore.** A boy laughs a beat before the joke. A girl writes the same sentence all
  period. The lockers are empty. Lunch is served; nobody eats. Follow a child home: the house is
  right, the dinner is right, the parents are right — and at 9pm every house on the street goes
  dark at once, like a switch.

## Protagonist

Give her a loss of her own — a child, a marriage, a year she would live again if offered. It
makes the temptation to stay a live wire rather than a gimmick, and it is what makes Ending 2
land if chosen.

Her stated reason for being there is the substitute job. Her real reason should be a photograph,
a record, a name — something that proves a school the town has a soft blank about.

## Candidate endings

**1 — The mercy massacre.** She finds the anchor (roll book / bell / a body still in the
building) and breaks it. The children take the news *well* — they are relieved. The adults do
not: released into time, they are hungry for the first time in eighty years, and there is one
living woman in the room. She survives because the children stand between her and the faculty.
*Most commercial.*

**2 — She takes its place.** The loop did not admit her; it recruited her. It is running down and
needs a living heart. She volunteers. Last image: a new substitute arriving, handed the same roll
sheet. *Most quotable, most bleak. Requires her own loss planted early.*

**3 — She simply leaves.** She gets out. Nothing happens. No one believes her, the school is a
field, the town is fine — and every year on that date she wakes at 6:40 with the taste of chalk.
The horror is the indifference. *Most literary, hardest sell.*

**4 — She empties the school.** She cannot break the loop, but she can do what the faculty would
not: tell the children the truth, one at a time. Every child who believes stops. The adults watch
eighty years of work drain out. The last is the child whose body is still in the building — she
has to go find it. They let her walk out, because there is nothing left to guard.

**5 — The son.** *(Straight from the dream's own detail.)* The replaced teacher's boy is alive
outside — and seventy years old. The loop has run that long. She brings him back through the front
doors, and a mother sees her son with an old man's face. Time enters the building through a face,
and the day comes apart.

**Recommended combination: 5 as the climax mechanism, 4 as the resolution.** The old man is the
crowbar; emptying the school is the ending. Ending 1 is the commercial alternative; Ending 2 is
the alternative that gets quoted.

## Open questions

- What killed the children? (Keep mundane. Decide late — the reveal is emotional, not clever.)
- Whose desk is the empty one?
- How long has it run? (Eighty years is proposed — it must be long enough that the son is elderly.)
- Does the town outside know, refuse to know, or have a hole where the knowing goes?
- Does she have a child of her own, or did she have one?
