> **Sample lines are illustrations, not script.** Never paste one into the manuscript. The n-gram gates
> will flag it, and a line that appears here and on the page has become a catchphrase.

## How distinctness works here (read before adding a character)

Distinctness is **NOT** "give everyone a verbal tic." Characters are separated on **four axes**, in this
order of power:

1. **Attention / worldview:** what they NOTICE and reduce the world to. The deepest differentiator.
2. **Syntax / register:** sentence shape, clause habits, formality, questions against statements.
3. **Evasion:** what they will NOT say.
4. **Physicality / silence:** the body or the pause that stands in for speech.

There is no fifth thing. **Nobody has a verbal tic.** Each card also carries a **bleed guard**: what this
character must never borrow, usually from the narrator. When a narrator's habits leak onto the cast,
everyone starts to sound alike.

**Language on the page.** All speech is rendered in English, and the register signals the language.
Ancient speakers use no modern idiom and no modern units. Only Fenn, and Mara when she speaks English,
use modern idiom. See `voice-dna.md` §1, Dialogue conventions.

## PRINCIPALS

#### MARA REYES — narrator and protagonist; objects conservator, Philadelphia, b. 1965 (Ch.1–44)
- **Attention / worldview:** what a thing is made of, how it was joined, where it will fail and how it
  was mended. She reads people the same way: their hands, their teeth, the repaired places in them. She
  trusts what stays put (made things, the dead) and keeps a bag packed against what moves. **Only Mara
  notices materials and joins.**
- **Vocabulary band:** educated American, late-1990s frame of reference (answering machine, Pentax,
  Discman, payphones, SEPTA, Rite Aid, film sent away to develop), with nothing after spring 1998. Lab
  nouns leak in sparingly (squeeze-out, consolidant, chain lines, cold join, birch tar), never as a
  report form. She reads Attic Greek and Latin from books. Her spoken Greek begins as a classroom
  reconstruction that 1st-century ears find comic.
- **Syntax fingerprint:** **Narration:** long, clause-stacking sentences when interested, linked by
  semicolons and "and," with one sideways clause that comes back; plain short declaratives when afraid.
  **Speech:** quick and qualified, often answering a question with a question. In early Greek (Ch.4–10)
  her lines are short, careful and wrong. In Latin (Part Two) she is competent and accented. In English
  with Fenn she is fast, contracted and 1998. **As written in Ch.6 (synced 2026-10-19):** her Greek carries no
  contractions at all ("I shall not need to come down", "Had I any silver, you would have had silver", "Latin
  is not learned on this road"), so contractions mark her English ("They're fine."; the cliff speech; the
  plea to the rock). Her first Greek aloud in a month is to Valens in the lane.
- **Rhythm:** set chapter by chapter by the ledger's gear. Fragments are a pressure signal, so she
  rarely uses them in calm narration.
- **Evasion — never says:** the word *love* in her own narration until Part Two (first use no earlier
  than Ch.19, and rare after that); her father's death stated plainly (she circles it through his
  objects); any claim to be brave; any request to be looked after. After Ch.25 she will not put
  **Fenn's date** in the future tense (no "it will open in seventy-nine") until Ch.36. Hope for an
  earlier opening (Ch.30) and the argument over whether the door would take the children (Ch.33) are

- **Physicality / silence:** steady, competent hands that betray her only at thresholds. She **touches
  surfaces to read them** (a thumbnail along a seam, a fingertip on a glaze, her palm on painted
  plaster). When she cannot speak she *inspects* something. Under pressure her hands act neatly by
  themselves (sample-05).
- **Verbal tic:** none — distinguished by attention (materials and joins) and syntax (spiral in
  interest, clip in fear)
- **Bleed guard:** her dry asides and her eye for materials must never migrate into anyone else's
  dialogue. Avoid "the conservator in me" as a recurring phrase (at-risk list).

#### VALENS ("Lupus") — muleteer, former murmillo, freedman; b. c. AD 38 in Thrace (Ch.6–44)
- **Attention / worldview:** footing, exits, weather, and the moods of animals and men, which are one
  skill to him. He reads bodies before faces and asks who saw. His worldview: whoever is close enough
  to know you is close enough to sell you, so be useful and be nobody.
- **Vocabulary band:** Latin-plain, arena and road register; concrete nouns (ground, rope, hoof, gate,
  bread, ship, weight, rain). **No abstractions in his mouth:** never honor, destiny, justice or fate.
  In Greek (Part One) he is flatly literal, without idiom, using a tool he never learned to enjoy. In
  Latin (Part Two) he is a shade looser and allows himself a dry understatement. His Thracian childhood
  surfaces only as the onion memory, never as words.
- **Syntax fingerprint:** short, level declaratives. He rarely subordinates, and **never uses a simile
  or comparison in speech.** His questions are practical (where, how far, how heavy, who saw). He asks
  *why* only of plans, never of feelings. He understates danger to almost nothing.
- **Rhythm:** statements in ones and twos with silence between. His longest speeches are evidence given
  in strict chronological order (the telling at the stream, Ch.12; the testimony, Ch.22): a witness, not
  a reflective man. **In Ch.34 alone he talks freely and at length.** That is how ease sounds in him.
- **Evasion — never says:** anything about the men he killed; that he is afraid; the word *love* until
  it is said once, plainly, in Ch.43; any grievance about the frame; any request that Mara stay.
- **Physicality / silence:** stillness. When a conversation comes too close he finds maintenance work
  for his hands, and **it is always a different object** (a hoof, a girth strap, a lamp wick, a knife
  edge, the potted fig, a child's sandal strap). It is never a fixed gesture and never ends a scene. He
  is enormously careful with small objects (the watch) and gentle with animals and children. He hands the
  chicken to someone else.
- **Verbal tic:** none — distinguished by attention (footing, bodies, animals) and syntax (short, level,
  unsubordinated, no comparisons)
- **Bleed guard:** must never notice materials or workmanship the way Mara does. When he admires the
  watch, it is as a well-made *working* thing ("It's alive"), not as craft. No wry asides.
- **Chaos on the page (subjects, never phrases):** **figs** (Ch.11, 20, 34; spent after that).
  **Mind-reading threat** (Ch.6, 11, 19, 21, 28, 37: he is wrong in mundane ways). **Onion memory**
  (Ch.12, 22, 23, 32, 41; never explained). **The mime** (Ch.29: he weeps at a farce).
- **DISTINCTNESS GUARANTEE:** the only speaker who never compares one thing to another and never asks why
  about a feeling, against Mara, whose every other sentence qualifies, compares or asks.
- **Sample line:** "He's lame in the off hind. Not your doing. Hold his head and keep talking to him; he
  likes your voice."
