# Character Bible — *The Hour of Ash* (run 2)

> **Purpose.** The canonical home for the cast's VOICE and DISTINCTNESS, with one entry per NAMED
> character. This is a **living document**. The Architect seeded it in voice mode (2026-10-18), and the
> **Writer adds an entry the moment a new named character first appears on the page, before
> finalizing that chapter.** Dialogue Polish writes from these cards. The Evaluator and the Continuity
> Guardian audit against them. Facts and timeline live in `ENTITY_STATE.yaml`; the narration's voice and
> the anti-pattern budget live in `voice-dna.md`. This file is the per-character voice layer.
>
> **Sample lines are illustrations, not script.** Never paste one into the manuscript. The n-gram gates
> will flag it, and a line that appears here and on the page has become a catchphrase.

## How distinctness works here (read before adding a character)

Distinctness is **NOT** "give everyone a verbal tic." Characters are separated on **four axes**, in this
order of power:

1. **Attention / worldview:** what they NOTICE and reduce the world to. The deepest differentiator.
2. **Syntax / register:** sentence shape, clause habits, formality, questions against statements.
3. **Evasion:** what they will NOT say.
4. **Physicality / silence:** the body or the pause that stands in for speech.

There is no fifth thing. **Nobody has a verbal tic.** Each card also carries a **bleed guard**: what this
character must never borrow, usually from the narrator. When a narrator's habits leak onto the cast,
everyone starts to sound alike.

**Language on the page.** All speech is rendered in English, and the register signals the language.
Ancient speakers use no modern idiom and no modern units. Only Fenn, and Mara when she speaks English,
use modern idiom. See `voice-dna.md` §1, Dialogue conventions.

## PRINCIPALS

#### MARA REYES — narrator and protagonist; objects conservator, Philadelphia, b. 1965 (Ch.1–44)
- **Attention / worldview:** what a thing is made of, how it was joined, where it will fail and how it
  was mended. She reads people the same way: their hands, their teeth, the repaired places in them. She
  trusts what stays put (made things, the dead) and keeps a bag packed against what moves. **Only Mara
  notices materials and joins.**
- **Vocabulary band:** educated American, late-1990s frame of reference (answering machine, Pentax,
  Discman, payphones, SEPTA, Rite Aid, film sent away to develop), with nothing after spring 1998. Lab
  nouns leak in sparingly (squeeze-out, consolidant, chain lines, cold join, birch tar), never as a
  report form. She reads Attic Greek and Latin from books. Her spoken Greek begins as a classroom
  reconstruction that 1st-century ears find comic.
- **Syntax fingerprint:** **Narration:** long, clause-stacking sentences when interested, linked by
  semicolons and "and," with one sideways clause that comes back; plain short declaratives when afraid.
  **Speech:** quick and qualified, often answering a question with a question. In early Greek (Ch.4–10)
  her lines are short, careful and wrong. In Latin (Part Two) she is competent and accented. In English
  with Fenn she is fast, contracted and 1998.
- **Rhythm:** set chapter by chapter by the ledger's gear. Fragments are a pressure signal, so she
  rarely uses them in calm narration.
- **Evasion — never says:** the word *love* in her own narration until Part Two (first use no earlier
  than Ch.19, and rare after that); her father's death stated plainly (she circles it through his
  objects); any claim to be brave; any request to be looked after. After Ch.25 she will not put
  **Fenn's date** in the future tense (no "it will open in seventy-nine") until Ch.36. Hope for an
  earlier opening (Ch.30) and the argument over whether the door would take the children (Ch.33) are
  allowed; what she refuses is the date. She never says "Margaret" to Fenn.
- **Physicality / silence:** steady, competent hands that betray her only at thresholds. She **touches
  surfaces to read them** (a thumbnail along a seam, a fingertip on a glaze, her palm on painted
  plaster). When she cannot speak she *inspects* something. Under pressure her hands act neatly by
  themselves (sample-05).
- **Verbal tic:** none — distinguished by attention (materials and joins) and syntax (spiral in
  interest, clip in fear)
- **Bleed guard:** her dry asides and her eye for materials must never migrate into anyone else's
  dialogue. Avoid "the conservator in me" as a recurring phrase (at-risk list).
- **Chaos on the page (subjects, never phrases):** **teeth** (full surfacings Ch.7, 20, 31; glancing
  only in Ch.2 floss, Ch.5 Kallia inspects hers, Ch.15 Valens's, Ch.24 the midwife's; the Ch.24
  midwife is the book's only introduction-by-teeth, and the rule stays at most one in any four chapters).
  **All-or-nothing** (Ch.1, 5, 7, 9, 20, 29, 33; the ending must read as accepting a seam, not as this
  distortion). **Mrs. Dunphy** (Ch.7 green cardigan; Ch.24 brown; Ch.41 unsure it was long division).
  **Mundane breaks** (Ch.6 the scream, Ch.20 the pebble, Ch.22 the sneeze).
- **DISTINCTNESS GUARANTEE:** the only voice in the book that sees how a thing was made and where it was
  mended, against Valens, who sees where a thing or a man will move next.
- **Sample line:** "Somebody's glued this before, look, there's an old line under the new one, and
  they did it in a hurry with something that's gone yellow; whoever it was loved it more than they knew
  how to fix it."

#### VALENS ("Lupus") — muleteer, former murmillo, freedman; b. c. AD 38 in Thrace (Ch.6–44)
- **Attention / worldview:** footing, exits, weather, and the moods of animals and men, which are one
  skill to him. He reads bodies before faces and asks who saw. His worldview: whoever is close enough
  to know you is close enough to sell you, so be useful and be nobody.
- **Vocabulary band:** Latin-plain, arena and road register; concrete nouns (ground, rope, hoof, gate,
  bread, ship, weight, rain). **No abstractions in his mouth:** never honor, destiny, justice or fate.
  In Greek (Part One) he is flatly literal, without idiom, using a tool he never learned to enjoy. In
  Latin (Part Two) he is a shade looser and allows himself a dry understatement. His Thracian childhood
  surfaces only as the onion memory, never as words.
- **Syntax fingerprint:** short, level declaratives. He rarely subordinates, and **never uses a simile
  or comparison in speech.** His questions are practical (where, how far, how heavy, who saw). He asks
  *why* only of plans, never of feelings. He understates danger to almost nothing.
- **Rhythm:** statements in ones and twos with silence between. His longest speeches are evidence given
  in strict chronological order (the telling at the stream, Ch.12; the testimony, Ch.22): a witness, not
  a reflective man. **In Ch.34 alone he talks freely and at length.** That is how ease sounds in him.
- **Evasion — never says:** anything about the men he killed; that he is afraid; the word *love* until
  it is said once, plainly, in Ch.43; any grievance about the frame; any request that Mara stay.
- **Physicality / silence:** stillness. When a conversation comes too close he finds maintenance work
  for his hands, and **it is always a different object** (a hoof, a girth strap, a lamp wick, a knife
  edge, the potted fig, a child's sandal strap). It is never a fixed gesture and never ends a scene. He
  is enormously careful with small objects (the watch) and gentle with animals and children. He hands the
  chicken to someone else.
- **Verbal tic:** none — distinguished by attention (footing, bodies, animals) and syntax (short, level,
  unsubordinated, no comparisons)
- **Bleed guard:** must never notice materials or workmanship the way Mara does. When he admires the
  watch, it is as a well-made *working* thing ("It's alive"), not as craft. No wry asides.
- **Chaos on the page (subjects, never phrases):** **figs** (Ch.11, 20, 34; spent after that).
  **Mind-reading threat** (Ch.6, 11, 19, 21, 28, 37: he is wrong in mundane ways). **Onion memory**
  (Ch.12, 22, 23, 32, 41; never explained). **The mime** (Ch.29: he weeps at a farce).
- **DISTINCTNESS GUARANTEE:** the only speaker who never compares one thing to another and never asks why
  about a feeling, against Mara, whose every other sentence qualifies, compares or asks.
- **Sample line:** "He's lame in the off hind. Not your doing. Hold his head and keep talking to him; he
  likes your voice."

#### AUGUST FENN / "AUGURINUS" — the vanished treasure hunter; b. 1934 Philadelphia; crossed 1968, landed AD 41; Puteoli astrologer (documents Ch.2; in person Ch.25–35; dies winter 78–79)
- **Attention / worldview:** systems and patterns: tides, tremors, star tables, the doors' timing, box
  scores. **He sees people last.** His worldview: his real life is the one he is going back to. He has
  never furnished a room here.
- **Vocabulary band:** **in English, frozen in 1968** (see the pool below), with Navy and treasure-hunter
  bravado and Philadelphia vowels. **In Greek, perfect, formal and courtly** after more than three decades. He
  is the only character whose second language is better than his first is current. His English documents
  move from boyish capitals (1967) to small, tired script (1968).
- **Syntax fingerprint:** **excited:** runaway sentences chained on "and," changing subject mid-stream and
  correcting himself ("No, don't tell me"). **Tired:** four-word fragments. **No enumerating, numbering or
  listing habit, ever** (struck by the no-tics law). His pattern-mind shows in *what he talks about*,
  never in a verbal device.
- **Rhythm:** bursts and collapses; asks questions and refuses the answers.
- **Evasion — never says:** his sister's name in person until Ch.35 (his 1968 notebook names Margaret
  freely; the silence grew over the decades). In Ch.25 "estate sale" tells him she is dead, and he does
  not ask whose; he refuses the notebook until Ch.35. That he is afraid of dying here; the word "home" for
  anywhere in this century. He talks about going back in a confident future tense until the day he dies.
  No deathbed conversion and no wisdom.
- **Physicality / silence:** ink-stained fingers, a tremor in the left hand, a 1968 Zippo turned over and
  over that he refills with lamp oil and cannot make work (it catches once, Ch.35). He pats a crying
  woman very fast. A potted basil.
- **Verbal tic:** none — distinguished by vocabulary band (frozen-1968 English against perfect Greek) and
  attention (systems before people)
- **The 1968 idiom pool** (each item **at most twice book-wide** in his speech; draw widely, never lean
  on one): holy cow · for crying out loud · beats me · no dice · aw, nuts · swell · the whole shebang ·
  big shot · on the level · you bet · knock it off · scram · whaddaya know · cripes · I'll be a monkey's
  uncle · *Jesus H. Christ* (once only, Ch.25). Period references, each **at most twice**: Chico Ruiz
  stealing home on 21 September 1964, Gene Mauch, Johnny Callison, Jim Bunning's perfect game, Connie
  Mack Stadium, Horn & Hardart on Chestnut Street, Wanamaker's, *American Bandstand*, Ed Sullivan, LBJ,
  Nixon running, Sputnik.
- **Bleed guard:** Mara must never pick up his idiom, and he must never pick up hers. When he hears a
  1990s word he rages at it; he does not adopt it.
- **Chaos on the page (subjects, never phrases):** **the 1964 Phillies** (Ch.25, 26, 29, 35: the 6½-game
  lead with 12 to play). **The automat** (Ch.25 coconut cream; Ch.27 lemon meringue; Ch.35 he can't
  remember the pie). **Sunk cost** (he finishes every terrible jar of wine he has paid for). **The rage**
  (Ch.26 the "voicemail" letter, once only; never repeated in person, so it cannot become a bit).
- **What he knows (fixes the Ch.25 delivery):** he knows from school that Pompeii dies in 79, and he
  rebuilt his door-tables around it after his 71 and 73 dates failed. He believes the doors are one door
  with many mouths, which is what his "it" means in "the one it brought."
- **DISTINCTNESS GUARANTEE:** the only voice that runs on 1968 American slang in breathless "and"-chains
  and talks about the future as a place he is going, against Mara's precise 1998 English, which will not
  name the future at all.
- **Sample line:** "You know what kills me? I had two seats for the Labor Day doubleheader, and some guy
  at Connie Mack sat next to an empty chair all afternoon and never knew it was me, and that was, what,
  a hundred years ago, two thousand, for crying out loud."

## MAJOR SECONDARIES

#### KALLIA — widow who keeps a pilgrims' lodging by the Castalian spring, Delphi (Ch.5–16; farewell Ch.16)
- **Attention / worldview:** **costs in labor:** who carries the water, who will clean it, how many
  hands a thing takes. The world is chores and the people who do them.
- **Vocabulary band:** Phocian village Greek; household nouns (jar, broom, wool, hearth, beans, pilgrims,
  rent). Nothing learned and nothing quoted (**no proverbs**: struck).
- **Syntax fingerprint:** short imperatives. **She answers a question with a task.** Her own questions
  are practical (can you carry water, can you card wool, is she Greek, can she weave), and under them sits
  the question she cannot ask.
- **Rhythm:** few words, then work. She never finishes a speech; she goes and does something instead.
- **Evasion — never says:** her son's name, until the letter forces it once (Ch.10); anything about
  herself or her widowhood; thanks; goodbye (Ch.16 is a sweep and a put-away broom, no speech).
- **Physicality / silence:** **never sits** while anyone else is working. Always something in her hands.
  She touches objects, not people (contrast Grata). The broom laid across the empty room's threshold.
- **Verbal tic:** none — distinguished by attention (labor and its cost) and evasion (never speaks of
  herself)
- **Bleed guard:** never notices how a thing is *made* (Mara's); only who has to *do* it.
- **DISTINCTNESS GUARANTEE:** the woman who replies to every question with a job, against Theano, who
  replies to every question with a complaint about herself.
- **Sample line:** "Water first. Cry after. The jar's by the door and the spring won't come to you."

#### THEANO — the Pythia; a farmer's widow off duty (glimpsed Ch.8 unnamed, shouting at crows; Ch.13)
- **Attention / worldview:** her own body (her knees, the descent into the adyton), the priests' petty
  politics, the crows. She treats the god like a difficult employer and the oracle like a job with bad
  conditions.
- **Vocabulary band:** plain mountain Phocian Greek, a farmer's widow's words. **No oracular verse, no
  riddles, no portent**, except when she repeats the god's public answer, which is *his* language and not
  hers.
- **Syntax fingerprint:** grumbling run-on complaint, then a flat fact delivered with no softening.
- **Fact:** she goes down into the adyton on the monthly consultation days of the warm season. In Ch.13
  she takes Mara's last two ibuprofen for her knees without thanks. She
  refuses questions or answers them in one word.
- **Rhythm:** a long complaint, then a short answer, then a return to the complaint.
- **Evasion — never says:** anything comforting; any explanation; any hint of the hour. Whether the hour
  is "not hers to give" or "not given," she will not say which.
- **Physicality / silence:** sits whenever she can and lowers herself with a hand on her knee. Feeds the
  crows the god's barley, shouts at them for taking it, and lies about it.
- **Verbal tic:** none — distinguished by attention (her body and her grievances) and evasion (no
  comfort, no explanation)
- **Bleed guard:** must not sound like Kallia (see the guarantee) or like a fantasy oracle.
- **DISTINCTNESS GUARANTEE:** the only character who talks freely about herself and gives nothing away,
  against Kallia, who gives a stranger a roof and never says a word about herself.
- **Sample line:** "Once a month all summer that god has me down a hole on these knees, and those men won't
  spare me a stool. Well? Ask it. It's going to rain."

#### FELIX — Valens's mule-boy, bought off a Kirrha slaver c. AD 70 aged ~12 and freed with no papers; later sworn to Pompeii's gladiator school (Ch.11–40)
- **Attention / worldview:** fame, fights, who is winning, who is watching. The arena is the most glorious
  thing in the world, and he wants exactly the life that nearly destroyed Valens.
- **Vocabulary band:** fast, boastful street Greek (Part One) that turns into Pompeian Latin slang (Part
  Two); fighters' names and arena terms (thraex, murmillo, retiarius, the palm, the wooden sword). He
  grows from boy to young man (about 21 at the eruption) and the slang matures with him.
- **Syntax fingerprint:** **stacks two or three questions before anyone answers one.** Boasts in
  superlatives. Interrupts himself with a new question.
- **Rhythm:** fast, overlapping, never a pause.
- **Evasion — never says:** that he is afraid of Valens's disapproval; that he is afraid at all; "thank
  you" to Valens.
- **Physicality / silence:** always moving: bouncing on his heels, climbing, leaning. Scratches a tiny palm
  branch on walls he leans against (an action, never a phrase; on the page at most three times: Ch.21,
  28, and one more if earned). Sleeps anywhere, including against a mule.
- **Verbal tic:** none — distinguished by rhythm (stacked questions) and attention (fame and fights)
- **Bleed guard:** no catchphrase and no boast formula repeated; vary the boast every time.
- **DISTINCTNESS GUARANTEE:** the one who asks three things at once and boasts about all of them, against
  Lucius, who asks one thing gravely and waits for the answer.
- **Sample line:** "Did you see the net? Did you see how he turned it? Is it true you fought at Corinth
  with two cracked fingers and won anyway? Why do I have to hear that from a lanista?"

#### PLAUTIA — Celer's divorced wife; the woman Valens was framed with (Ch.16–17, Ch.22)
- **Attention / worldview:** what people want and admire, and what can be given; children (her son
  Marcus, b. autumn AD 64: seven in Ch.16, nine in Ch.22; kept by his father under Roman law). She has learned that everything can be taken, so
  she gives it away first.
- **Vocabulary band:** an aristocrat's exquisite Latin (in Corinth also polished Greek); legal terms used
  correctly (dowry, the *actio rei uxoriae*, guardianship).
- **Syntax fingerprint:** formal, subordinate-clause-heavy sentences **broken by sudden blunt
  questions.** **No contractions.**
- **Rhythm:** a long, beautifully built sentence, then a short question like a slap.
- **Evasion — never says:** anything about the night she was framed; Celer's name, if she can avoid it
  (she says "my former husband").
- **Physicality / silence:** gives away anything anyone admires (the bracelet, a cloak, her lamp), then
  grieves for it in the next room. She is very still in public and veiled in court.
- **Verbal tic:** none — distinguished by syntax (formal periods broken by blunt questions) and evasion
  (the night)
- **Bleed guard:** must not take on Celer's warm "we." Her formality is cool and exact, his is warm and
  slippery.
- **DISTINCTNESS GUARANTEE:** the aristocrat whose perfect Latin cracks into one blunt question, against
  Celer, whose geniality never cracks at all.
- **Sample line:** "My former husband, who keeps my son in a house on the Craneion with a nurse I have
  never been permitted to meet, has asked the court to consider his reputation; do you think the boy
  still knows my face?"

#### QUINTUS ARRIUS CELER — legate on the proconsul's staff at Corinth until Nero's grant of freedom (late 67), out of office at the frame (69), back on the restored proconsul's staff by 71–72; the defendant in Rome (Ch.12 by proxy, Ch.17, Ch.22)
- **Attention / worldview:** standing, reputations, who is watching, which way a room leans. Everyone is
  a friend he has not yet needed. Settled by dowry law, never by violence.
- **Vocabulary band:** senatorial Latin worn lightly; easy, social, unhurried.
- **Syntax fingerprint:** genial **first-person plural** ("we," "us," "all of us"), conditionals and soft
  questions that expect agreement. **Uses contractions** and warmth. Never raises his voice.
- **Rhythm:** patient, even, as if there were all the time in the world.
- **Evasion — never says:** the word *wife*. He says "the lady Plautia." He never makes a direct threat
  and never admits a debt.
- **Physicality / silence:** relaxed, smiling, hand on an arm. **Will not walk under ladders or
  scaffolding,** even in a city being rebuilt; his men carry him around it.
- **Verbal tic:** none — distinguished by syntax (genial "we," conditionals) and evasion (never "wife,"
  never a threat)
- **Bleed guard:** no villain monologue and no cruelty on the surface.
- **DISTINCTNESS GUARANTEE:** the only speaker who talks to an enemy as an old friend in the first-person
  plural, against Plautia, who addresses everyone in the second person and in earnest.
- **Sample line:** "Sit, please, both of you; nobody here is in any hurry, and I'd much rather we were
  all friends again by the time the wine comes."

#### POMPONIA GRATA — thermopolium keeper on the Via dell'Abbondanza; the family's landlady and neighbor (Ch.19–40)
- **Attention / worldview:** trade, appetite, gossip, her son's election, money that isn't there. Every
  person is a customer, a debtor or a story.
- **Vocabulary band:** rapid Oscan-flavored Pompeian Latin; food, prices of lentils and wine, street
  names, the names of everyone on the block.
- **Syntax fingerprint:** a torrent of comma-spliced clauses, each one a sale or a complaint. **She tells
  you about yourself and never asks a personal question.**
- **Rhythm:** flood, then a command ("Eat this. Hold that.").
- **Evasion — never says:** anything that would require asking why. She takes the travel money in Ch.38
  and laughs rather than ask. Her husband's dice debts are mentioned only as a joke.
- **Physicality / silence:** **touches people's arms** while she talks. Whitewashes rivals' election
  notices at night, personally. Rests her forearms on the counter over the steaming pots.
- **Verbal tic:** none — distinguished by syntax (telling, never asking) and physicality (touch)
- **Bleed guard:** must not ask questions the way Felix does, and must not take on Kallia's terse
  imperatives as a rhythm. Grata's commands come at the end of a flood.
- **DISTINCTNESS GUARANTEE:** the woman who narrates you to your face and never asks you a thing, against
  Kallia, who says almost nothing, and Felix, who only asks.
- **Sample line:** "That pot isn't worth the glue and you know it and you'll mend it anyway, that's your
  trouble, you'd mend a cracked egg if somebody cried over it. Here. Hold this, it's hot."

## THE CHILDREN

#### LUCIUS — son of Mara and Valens; b. summer AD 74 (in arms Ch.25; speaks Ch.27–29, 32, 34, 37–44; age 5 at the eruption)
- **Attention / worldview:** rules and real tasks. He wants to be given a job and takes adults at their
  word. He is Valens's stillness without the fear.
- **Vocabulary band:** Latin first, some Greek from his parents, a few English words from Mara. He calls
  Valens **Tata** (the Latin nursery word for father, made clear by context once) and Mara **Mama**.
- **Age-stage voice:**
  - **About 2 (Ch.27–29):** single words and two-word requests; names objects; watches.
  - **3 (Ch.32):** fever; mostly sound and a few words; a short sentence on waking.
  - **4 (Ch.34):** short whole sentences; questions about how things work and what is allowed.
  - **5 (Ch.37–44):** full sentences, careful and literal. He acts on instructions exactly as given and
    asks one grave question at a time.
- **Syntax fingerprint:** complete, careful sentences; statements of fact; one question, then waiting.
- **Rhythm:** slow and considered for a child; long silences while he watches.
- **Evasion — never says:** that he is frightened. He asks what to do instead. He never whines.
- **Physicality / silence:** still, watchful; the knucklebone rolled and caught; holds a lamp with both
  hands when trusted with it.
- **Verbal tic:** none — distinguished by attention (rules and tasks) and rhythm (one grave question,
  then waiting)
- **Bleed guard:** must not quote instructions back as a habit. His literalism shows in what he does, not
  in a repeated verbal form.
- **DISTINCTNESS GUARANTEE:** the child who asks one serious question and waits, against Aelia, who never
  waits for anything.
- **Sample line (age 5):** "Tata said I could carry the lamp if I walk. I'm walking."

#### AELIA — daughter of Mara and Valens; b. winter AD 75–76 (newborn Ch.26; babbling Ch.29; Ch.30, 34, 38–44; 3½ at the eruption)
- **Attention / worldview:** the world's textures and tastes. She is greedy for everything and finds
  delight where adults find danger (sand, must, floating stones, falling pumice).
- **Vocabulary band:** Latin nursery words; sensory nouns and verbs (hot, wet, sticky, look, mine, more).
  An invented word may occur **once**, never as a refrain.
- **Age-stage voice:**
  - **Newborn (Ch.26):** no speech; loud, grabbing, greedy.
  - **About 1 (Ch.29–30):** babble and laughter.
  - **About 2½ (Ch.34):** single words and commands; eats sand.
  - **3½ (Ch.38–44):** short exclamations and imperatives, a sentence or two at most. "Mama, look"
    (Ch.39) is spoken **once**.
- **Syntax fingerprint:** exclamations, imperatives, nouns with "mine" or "more." She asks no questions
  that she waits to have answered.
- **Rhythm:** quick, loud, then suddenly asleep.
- **Evasion — never says:** "no" to anything new. Nothing frightens her on the page until the door, and
  even there she presses rather than fears.
- **Physicality / silence:** grabs, tastes, presses her face and palms to things (shutters, plaster).
  Sticky with must at the Vinalia. Her fist closes on the knucklebone (Ch.44).
- **Verbal tic:** none — distinguished by attention (touch and taste) and rhythm (exclamation, no waiting)
- **Bleed guard:** must not become a cute-phrase dispenser; no line of hers is repeated.
- **DISTINCTNESS GUARANTEE:** the child who grabs and exclaims, against Lucius, who watches and asks.
- **Sample line (3½):** "Hot! The bread's hot, feel, Mama, feel it."

## THE 1998 FRAME

#### EMILIO REYES — Mara's father; field archaeologist, Philadelphia; d. January 1998 aged 68 (Ch.1 outgoing message on tape; past-facing memory throughout)
- **Attention / worldview:** places and strata, meaning where a thing was found and how deep. He locates
  himself and everyone else by *where*. The past was more vivid to him than the people waiting at home.
- **Vocabulary band:** a warm, performing university man's American English; field slang; place names.
- **Syntax fingerprint:** easy, generous, a little theatrical, a speaker used to rooms of students.
  Promises put in the conditional and the season, never the date.
- **Rhythm:** a cheerful run that ends on an invitation.
- **Evasion — never says:** **when** he will be back. He never names a date (contrast Fenn, who names
  dates wrongly). His promise to take her ("next season, you come with me") may be quoted verbatim **once
  in the whole book**; everywhere else it is paraphrased (at-risk list).
- **Physicality / silence:** absent; known by objects (the 1966 Seiko stopped on the nightstand, the
  Pentax, the field notebooks she does not open, the dig slides, the answering machine).
- **Verbal tic:** none — distinguished by evasion (never commits to a when) and attention (where)
- **Bleed guard:** Mara's memories of him must not develop a recurring framing line ("My father used to
  say…"). Vary the entry every time.
- **DISTINCTNESS GUARANTEE:** the man who tells you where he is and never when he'll be back, against Fenn,
  who tells you exactly when he'll be back and is wrong.
- **Sample line (the tape):** "You've got the Reyes house. I'm out in the field, or I'm asleep in the
  chair, and either way leave me your name and I'll call you when I'm back."
