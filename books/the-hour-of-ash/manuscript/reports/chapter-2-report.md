## Structure
- **Structural approach:** COLLAGE, with the internal shape *notebook against days*. Fenn's entries (1967 → Feb 1968 → Delphi spring 1968 → June → July 21, 1968) are read "in rations" and interleaved with Mara's days from March to May 1998. This is a different approach from Ch.1, which was chronological.
- **Function:** Accumulation, layered with a quiet decision. The reader finishes carrying Fenn as a person, the cold air, the slip and the bag.
- **Hook type:** dramatic irony. The reader knows from Ch.1 that he never came back; the last line is his plan to. There is no narrator comment after it and no button. The type differs from Ch.1's CONTRAST/QUESTION-PLANT.

## Anchor and surprise
- **Anchor:** the handwriting. It starts as block capitals a quarter-inch high, retraced, pressing a raised copy through every page "like the dots beside an elevator button" (L7). The capitals stand upright through April, then shrink and join, and "When I turned these over, the backs were smooth" (L43). The June entries need the magnifier, and the last is "Tired today." (L103). The final entry is "in the small leaning hand, and the pen had barely marked the page" (L143). The anchor is carried by her conservator's attention to pen pressure, never by a statement.
- **Surprise:** the laugh at *firma mancante*, the Soprintendenza returning the cession because the outgoing director had not signed it (L65–73). She slides down the cabinet to the linoleum; the cereal pours across the floor. Halloran: "Is that—are you crying?" / "I'm laughing." / "Oh, thank God. I couldn't tell which."
- **Beat subversion:** nothing is cryptic. The coordinates are "as plainly as a street address… nothing to work out". The only obstacle is her decision, and she makes it with floss in her teeth.

- **Teeth (glancing, per schedule):** the decision made mid-floss (L107); afterward she "finished the upper left… and did the lowers properly, since the floss was out anyway" (L111); floss packed before the passport (L113). There is no introduction-by-teeth.
