## How the watch, the wrist and CH16-8 are handled
- **The watch:** it is handed back already wound, with no speech. She notices it running (the seconds hand) and fully wound (the crown will not turn). It reads **twenty to one**, and the mooring post's shadow is "shorter than my sandal, tipped a little east", which fits Ch.17's "a little past its height" plus the run. One past-facing sentence recalls the Ch.11 reading, which was fast ("ten past nine … while the spout's shadow still slanted long over the trough"). The reader infers that he set it by the shadow. The page never says he set it, never says he kept it wound, and never counts the months.
- **The wrist (decided on the page):** the watch goes on her **RIGHT** wrist. She tries the left first, and the steel comes down on Plautia's gold: "Gold that soft took a scratch from anything, and it had a child's teeth in it already." That is the conservator's reason, given in one sentence. Valens closes the clasp. His thumb is too broad for the catch, so he uses his nail. Then: "The watch sat on the wrong wrist and ticked." The bracelet stays on the left, "made for the left". Ch.24's winding has to be on the right wrist.
- **CH16-8:** the household's freedman booked passage at sunrise "for a muleteer and a wife and a boy" (the Corinth cover, in a stranger's mouth). Felix, asked by bored sailors how long his master had been married, told the truth: "Nobody ever told me she was. Who married her? When was it? Wouldn't I have been there?" After the consent the master says: "A muleteer, then, with a wife and a boy. As I was told at sunrise, and nobody on my ship has lied to anybody." Nothing in the narration explains that the cover is now true. Valens never says "My wife" to the master (that gatekeeper line is CLOSED after Ch.16). The freedman has gone back for the men's race, so no member of the Corinth household sees the wedding. The planning exchange from Ch.15 ("That's for the shipmaster, at the gangway") is not quoted; the reader carries it.

The ten words are: "Will you be my wife, Mara?" (6) / "Yes." (1) / "Will you?" (2) / "Yes," (1). There are no vows, and no narrator counts the words or comments on them.
- **Felix cries** (l.80), "open-mouthed, as a far younger boy cries", and says "It's the sun."

## Canon decisions
1. **The ship's master is named PACUVIUS**, a Campanian nomen, heard once in the plank sailor's mouth. He is an Italian trading Lechaion to Puteoli and is carrying his daughter, newly married by proxy, to her husband's mother at Puteoli. Bible card added.
2. **The watch's custody:** returned to Mara on the Lechaion quay, spring AD 72. It is on her **right** wrist, closed by Valens, running and fully wound, and set near local sun time (it read 12:40 against the post's shadow). Plautia's bracelet stays on her left.
3. **The ship:** a merchantman with the goose carved from its sternpost; the sail is brailed through lead rings; a tow boat takes her out; she is moored stern-to with a cleated gangplank. The only other ship for Italy sails at the Ides, carries marble and takes no mules.
4. **The freedman** paid the passage at sunrise as "a muleteer and a wife and a boy" and went back to Corinth for the men's race. No one from the Corinth household witnessed the wedding.
5. **All four mules and the fig** are aboard. Felix ends the chapter shirtless (his tunic is the dun's blindfold) and crowned.
