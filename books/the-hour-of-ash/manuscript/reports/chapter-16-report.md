## CH12-2 and CH12-8: how they're handled
- **CH12-2 (the record's form): settled on the page, consistent with "the magistrate's book".** Plautia (l.518–536): "It is the book of the magistrate who sat at those games, the whole of his year's business, and it is boards, not a roll". It is bound by "A cord. Through holes at the back edge, and knotted, and the knot sealed.", with the seals "His, and his colleague's." The leaf with his name is "two boards that close on each other with the writing inside; once the cord is cut they come away together and go into a writing case like any other pair." So the outline's Ch.17 "bronze-and-wax diptych" is a leaf of the magistrate's book, not a separate document. Mara's one recognition moment goes into how it is joined ("A cord can be cut … and tied again. A seal is wax." / "So that it slips past a drinker … A sober clerk who is looking would find it."). That is one beat, not a competence set piece. Ch.17 may call the leaf a diptych. Bronze is not on the page, so Ch.17 should drop "bronze" or give it a reason (corner fittings, say).
- **CH12-8 (whether "Valens" is his own name): settled. It is.** The leaf reads "Gaius Iulius Valens, freedman of Gaius, set free by his master before the magistrate of the games and before the emperor, with the witnesses' names under it, and the day." (l.536). "Iulius," / "The lanista's," … "I had it with the sword." (l.542). Plautia heard him give all of it when he was sworn to her, "your hand on the household altar" ("You said the Gaius as though somebody might take it back." / "It was new,"). She has always called him Valens, and the benches called him **Lupus** ("tell each other they have seen Lupus"). It follows that Ch.12's "They asked my name and I gave them Valens." was his real name given on the quay. Ch.22's "his own name" is **Gaius Iulius Valens**.

## Canon decisions made on the page (for ENTITY_STATE and the orchestrator)
1. **CH12-2 resolved:** the record is the magistrate's book of the AD 67 games, a book of waxed boards on a sealed cord kept in the proconsul's office. Valens's leaf is two boards near the front.
2. **CH12-8 resolved:** his name is **Gaius Iulius Valens, freedman of Gaius** (the lanista's praenomen and nomen). "Valens" is his own; "Lupus" is the arena's.
3. **Plautia:** about thirty, left earlobe torn and healed notched, no earrings; she nursed Marcus herself. Marcus's grandfather (Celer's father) was a suffect consul "two months in a bad year". Her brother is pressing a Sicyon remarriage. The suit is heard **in Rome** ("two years from this spring" by the advocate, "one" by her brother). Her dowry instrument is in the colony's books, now in the proconsul's office.
4. **The bracelet:** a plain hinged gold band with a hidden seam clasp, made for the left wrist, with Marcus's teething marks near the clasp. It is now Mara's, worn on her left wrist. (New object for the tracker.)
5. **The record office:** at the end of the long colonnade across the forum from the old temple. Two doors: one onto the colonnade, with the proconsul's porter; one at the back onto a lane, by a baker's oven.
6. **The himation:** now Mara's outright. Pin eye worked, hem bound. (objects.borrowed-himation: paid, Ch.16.)
7. **Film:** one frame, Kallia's lamp on her sill at first light (the named Ch.16 frame). The counter is not stated.
8. **Flashlight:** seen by Felix (the boat). Nearly exhausted ("not much. Some.").
9. **The string:** two mules sold at the foot of the gorge before the crossing. The rest crossed with the dun, the black and the potted fig. They lodge "in the rooms over the stable" of the brother's house. Mara is "his wife" to the porter and the cook.
10. **Kallia's open thread (ariston-reply):** the line is written at Corinth in C2 on papyrus bought "at the top of the Lechaion road", to go east from Kenchreai. Sending it is not yet shown.
11. **Timeline:** they came down "on the last fair day" of the grazing, in autumn AD 71 before the rains. Kirrha to Lechaion took a day and a night at sea. C1 is midwinter, after the Saturnalia; C3 is "the end of that month"; C4 is late winter, before the Isthmia of spring AD 72.
