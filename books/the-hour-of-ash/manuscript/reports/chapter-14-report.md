# Writer Report: Chapter 14 — "What Pompeii Means"

- **Word count:** 3,106 (shape_check prose) / 3,397 `wc -w` including header comments, against a target of 3,400 (brief band 3,000–3,800). It lands in band, about 9% under target, and is not padded. The first draft came in at 2,341. It was brought up by adding real beats (the fountain overflow; the midnight court with Kallia; what the plan does to the man and to Felix; Saint Paul's grain ship; Pliny's letters; the hand over Valens's shoulder), never by stretching finished prose. Every outline beat is on the page.
- **Shape:** Fragmented, one night in shards. 10 shards and 9 breaks. Opens `I-punch` ("I knew the year.", inside a longer paragraph as the ledger asks); closes `long-fade` (48-word final sentence, the night thinning, no aphorism). Dialogue 5% (band 0–10). Median sentence 12 (gear low). Shape audit clean.
- **Hook as written:** "I knew the year." This is a flat confession/voice hook (it is not SENSORY IMMERSION, which was Ch.13's). The whole outcome of the town arrives in the first paragraph as history, so the question the reader carries is what she will do with it.
- **Pull as written:** a DECISION POINT carried as a fade (it is not REVELATION, which was Ch.13's). She has decided to see the cave shut before she commits to Italy. Valens has gone for the mules and Kallia sends her to the bottom of the climb. The final image is the cliffs coming clear of the night, "with the path to the plateau scratched up the rock in its long turns and nobody on it yet." The question it loads is sharper than the one the chapter resolved: if the cave is shut, the only door in the world is in Pompeii, and she has just made a plan that needs a man she means to leave there.
- **Emotional anchor:** hit at l.80–92. The word sits "behind my teeth" a yard from the man who carried it out of the temple and "had no more idea what it was than the cat on Theano's hearth." She tells him about the Amphissa woman's well instead. Her hand goes out over his shoulder and is drawn back. Then: "I said the word once with no sound in it, toward the jars. To everybody under that roof it was a town in Italy where they made fish sauce."
- **Emotional surprise (the laugh, NON-NEGOTIABLE):** l.58–68. The trigger runs Neapolis → Halloran's offered room ("I had said Greece, perhaps.") → her father's summers ("Every year there was next year. Next year there was never room.") → "He had not troubled with a permit. He had picked the year." Then "I laughed." The shape is new: aloud, through a wrist, "with a squeak in the top of it", dry eyes, the muscles under her ribs. None of the earlier laugh shapes is reused (Ch.2 bark/sink, Ch.7 silent into wool, Ch.8 group). It wakes Valens, which turns the laugh into the not-telling.
- **Non-negotiables:** the laugh (l.66–68); the plan of money, a man, the sea in spring and Italy (l.124–136, written as reasoning and not as a list); Valens asleep across her doorway, put there by Kallia (l.24–26, the floor shown and the decision not repeated); she does not tell him (l.80–92); she tells no one (Valens twice, Kallia once, each with a different true or borrowed cover).
- **No-foreshadowing audit:** gate 0. Her knowledge of AD 79 appears only as present thought and history: the year and month, Pliny the Younger's letters (unnamed; "a boy of nine now"), the town from a plan, the tombs. No outcome of hers is narrated. Her future appears only as intention ("I meant to…"), plan language and conditionals. The 1988 casts are told as what she saw then. I deliberately avoided the pine cloud (Ch.39), pumice (Ch.40) and Pliny the Elder's boats at Stabiae, because knowing that boats could not land would undercut the Ch.37 backup boat.
- **CH13-7 ("Tomorrow", the cave):** handled on the page in its own shard (l.140–146) and the close. She argues whether the cave still matters ("There was no need of the cave now…"; "That door went home with no sea in the way, and no town."). She decides it does: "I wanted it shut… After that the one door left in the world would be the door the plan ended at, and I could stop listening for the other." / "I meant to go up as soon as it was light and find out which." The night ends heading for that errand: Valens has gone for the mules and Kallia sends her to wait at the bottom of the climb. **For the Ch.15 writer:** account for the visit in a clause or a short backfill (they went up; the crack was shut), in fresh words. The cave descriptors from Ch.3/6/8 are SPENT, and so are Ch.14's own ("given me back my own arm, cold"). Recommend ENTITY CH13-7 move to "carried as an errand; Ch.15 to close".

- **Impulse deviations (kept):**
