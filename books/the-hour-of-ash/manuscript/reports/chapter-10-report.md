- **Emotional anchor: hit.** Kallia sweeps boards that were already swept at dawn, in the middle of the afternoon, and lays the broom back across the threshold ("the head against one post and the handle against the other"). Then she says the room's name for the first and only time, renting it: "He can have Ariston's room, at the three obols." She goes out. "Up at the last door the broom still lay over the threshold, from post to post." (¶196). Three plain paragraphs of the afternoon follow, so the broom is not the closing image.
- **Emotional surprise:** the good news lands as the loss. Kallia says "He's living." flat to Nikaso, who takes it as joy ("A child … And you out here spinning."). The neighbors spread "Syria, and a wife, and a child" gate by gate. Beat subversion: the soldier's letter does not bring a death. He is alive and content, and that is the bereavement. Read silently once Kallia has gone, "To myself it was a cheerful letter" (¶198).
- **Non-negotiables:**

She reads every line, with nothing cut or softened. ¶160 shows she could have edited it and did not.
  - **Kallia says his name once:** "Ariston's room" (¶190). The name is also on the page once in Mara's reading of the letter's formula. The narration calls him "Kallia's son" once.

The outline's ledger legend allows a one-sentence opening paragraph ONLY in Ch.4, 24, 30, 36 and 42. I followed the outline: the first *sentence* is the ≤8-word object line, inside a longer first paragraph. The sig computes as `short-punch` either way.   4. **No kin in Syracuse is mentioned.** "Aimilios of Syracuse" is untouched. No one comes for Mara. Valens is absent.
