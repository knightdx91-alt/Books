- **Beats delivered:**
  1. **The bout begins.** Thraex against murmillo. Felix is the thraex: griffin-crested brimmed helmet, punched visor, two high greaves over quilted wraps, curved sword, small shield. He is thin and quick, so he gets the thraex kit. He had asked for Valens's armature, "the big board and the fish on my head", and Nicanor laughed. Rusticus is the murmillo.
  2. **Backfill: Felix's absence arrives as news on the return from Puteoli.** Grata tells it in the street in one flood. Then the bare floor where his blanket was, the hare bowl left behind, the sherd gone, and the midwinter split eyebrow explained (the posts, not the dun). **Different route from Ch.21:** nobody goes to the barracks gate. The news comes from Grata, then Prima at the vats, then the lanista's room behind a wine shop, then Nicanor bringing Felix home at dusk to tell it himself.
  3. **Auctoratus:** sworn to the lanista Albucius for three years, before the duumvir's freedman, with Nicanor as witness. 400 down, the same per fight, less forty for greaves. Albucius did not ask for a paper. The contract cannot be bought out before the first fight because the pair is sold to the duumvir and the list is made. Albucius offers to sell him back cheaper afterward.
  4. **Felix's pride:** "I never stumbled once. There. It's told." / "They're made to my legs." / "Say so, then. Say I can't." He refuses the wish aloud: "I'd sooner you stayed away … the whole place would know who'd taught me."
  5. **The girl at the fullery: Prima,** the fuller's wife's niece, who treads the vats. He swore to impress her because she once said at the fountain that the fighters "had arms". She holds his ΦΗΛΙΞ sherd. Her verdict on him: "That fat's better than burned."
  6. **NON-NEGOTIABLE, Valens decides to watch, not intervene.** "I can't go down there. I'd be one more pair of feet on that sand." … "I'll watch." He sits in the top ring with the women and the poor, so that no herald can make it "Lupus's boy on the sand". He takes Lucius, and the reason is never said. He never steps onto the sand and nothing sets up a bout for him. Mara is sure all week that he will do something (offer himself to the duumvir, vault the parapet). He doesn't.
  7. **NON-NEGOTIABLE, the bout.** It is clumsy, cautious and short. Felix circles wide, hits the rim and backs off. Rusticus ties his loose greave lacing in full view, and Felix does not strike. The whips come out and the crowd chants kill/whip/burn. One exchange cuts Felix on the upper arm with the shield rim. The duumvir sends both off standing (*stantes missi*, rendered in English). The crowd jeers and throws nut shells, crusts and a sandal. Felix is alive. The next pair's horns sound before the sandal is cleared.
  8. **Felix's line to Valens ends the chapter,** in the north tunnel under the seats.
- **Emotional anchor: hit.** "Valens sat. Lucius sat on his knee." The whole top ring is on its feet ("every soul but one was up"). "Valens had an arm round the boy's middle. The other rested open on the stone at his side. His eyes were on the sand." The peak carries no comparison.
- **Emotional surprise (the outline's): the fight is short and dull, Felix survives, and the crowd hated it.** All on the page. A second surprise: Felix's best moment is a refusal (not striking a man bent over his laces), and the crowd jeers it.
- **Beat subversion:** the champion does not step in. His courage is sitting still. The protégé's bout is dull and both men walk off. The crowd is angry.
