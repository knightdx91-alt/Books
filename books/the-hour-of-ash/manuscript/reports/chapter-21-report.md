## Beats (outline + checkpoint-4 rulings)
1. **Summons, early (by about 1,400 words), via the Puteoli board (AUTHOR-CP4-2).** The knowledge path takes one line: "The lime carter. At the crossing. … He'd been to Puteoli. He looks at the board for me." The letter is from Plautia's brother in a scribe's hand. The suit will be heard in Rome at the Basilica Julia in April. His freedman waits at the wine-seller's at Puteoli from the Kalends of March to the Ides with road money "for you and yours". Plautia adds a line in her own hand: *Four lines since Corinth. Is she well? Bring her, and I will see for myself.* (This gives Ch.22 its reason for Mara being in Rome.)
2. **Mara tells Valens before the street does.** He does not grin in the doorway. He stays on the sill, keeps his eyes on her face, and asks **"Who knows?"** (his fear of being traceable, even inside joy). Then come "Since when?" and "When?". He leaves the tablet unopened, takes the lamp, and spends the evening cutting out the worn top tread of the roof stair, the one Felix slipped on in Ch.20. He does not know that Grata predicted Mara would "curse that stair". Mara laughs helplessly at the old tread on the hearth. His only comment: "It's a stair." In the dark he says: "There's no step on that stair now will turn your foot." Next morning the gossip reaches Felix ("the baker's boy says the carter says Grata says"), and Valens's "You did." confirms she got there first.
3. **The amphitheater, at the Saturnalia show** given by Gaius Fabius Rufinus, candidate for aedile. Valens's string carted the sand, and the muleteers were paid in bone seat tokens.
   - The old trainer **Nicanor** recognizes him by the heel-test on the sand (Valens's footing habit, seen since Ch.6, now traced to his training).
   - Felix's face: his hands stop moving, he slides off the wall without a word, and his face goes "the color of the sand".
   - Mara watches from the women's rows. The herald names Lupus to the bowl. Valens's hand goes up to the benches, then grips Felix's tunic and sits him down.
   - The editor's freedman offers 2,000 sesterces for an exhibition bout at the Parilia.
   - The "Lupus" graffito (a fish-crested helmet and LVPVS) is on the pier at the foot of the outer stair. A second, neater LVPVS with a wreath is on the barracks gatepost in S4.
4. **Flat refusal:** "No," said Valens, then "Lupus died in Asia … Or drowned off Cenchreae. Ask Nicanor." Rome stays undecided. The emotional surprise (the fame flatters him and he hates that it does) is carried by objects and actions:
   - the hand that goes up;
   - "The fish faces the wrong way," three paces past the graffito;
   - his back straightening all the way home;
   - washing that one hand at the basin, again and again;
   - "My hand went up to them. I hadn't told it to."
   - his thumbnail pressed to the wreath, which he leaves whole.
5. **The argument (S3).** Mara, pregnant, argues: say it once, have it written, be believed, come home. Valens argues: name in a clerk's book, Celer knows where I sleep, a child is coming, "Since the ship I've been nobody here, and I've slept all those nights through." The subtext stays under the words:
   - his question appears only as "his mouth began a word and gave it up";
   - hers surfaces as "You have not stood still since Corinth … You sat me by the stair at the show." / "There's always a way out. I find it first." There are no speeches about honor.
   - **Added turn:** Valens points out that Celer's advocate will ask about *her*: whose daughter, which house in Syracuse. Mara has no people anywhere, and says nothing. This makes her ask for him to stand in the open cost her something.
6. **Mind-reading goes wrong.** Grata brushes sand off his shoulders and leaves her hand there. She wears the pearl earrings she keeps for the baths. She pours him the street's Falernian, and tells Mara she'd "sit him on my bench in a clean tunic and let the custom come to look". Valens reads all of it as a woman building up to evict them: "She was looking for the place to start." / "She watches me across that counter all day long." / the earrings are "so he'd see she has gold to lose". Mara has to turn her face to the lamp.
7. **Close (S4, late winter).** Felix has skipped the string for three days to sit at Nicanor's gate. The **palm branch** is scratched in the gatepost plaster, alone, with no name. It ties back to his Ch.20 line about a name on every wall but his, without quoting it. Mara: "He cannot write his name." Valens keeps his own literacy from Felix even now ("Then he'd know I'd let him think it four years"). The open question ends the chapter.
