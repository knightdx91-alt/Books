## Where the NON-NEGOTIABLES landed

The **empty statue bases** come with the guide still reciting the absent Kroton runner over one of them. Mara kneels and reads the **fresh pry-marks**: a crowbar spall with raw crystal and no dirt or lichen, square chisel bites still sharp, a crumb of chopped lead soft and bright under the gray skin, copper-stain streaks with no bronze above them, a sawn heel left in a socket. She dates the damage to "two or three years of weather at the very most." The guide names "the singer" and five hundred bronzes; Mara corrects his "ditch" to Nero's death in a freedman's villa, June 68, so "it was at least that late." This is her first real wonder: "This was the first time I had stood where the taking happened while the marks were still sharp." She spends a frame on the base.

## Anchor and surprise
- **Anchor:** the temple *painted* (stucco columns; frieze triglyphs blue-black and metopes red; cornice in red, blue and gold; gilded shields flashing in the altar smoke; tinted pediment figures; gilt acroteria), and to her it is ugly: "The Mummers had got at it." She goes to the exact spot where the 1998 path stood and waits "for it to turn into my temple. It stayed red." The peak line: "I had come the whole way back to see it alive, and I preferred it dead."
- **Surprise:** disappointment, with the shame shown by an action instead of named: she thinks "ugly" in English "and in exactly those words," then "checked the faces of the pilgrims going up the ramp straight away, in case one of them had heard." The shame then *drives the plot*: she goes up to give the temple its due, and that is how she gets beaten.
- **Beat subversion:** there is no wonder-struck first look. The living sanctuary is loud, commercial and indifferent, and her first wonder is the looted bases.
