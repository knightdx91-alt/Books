# Voice Bank — *The Hour of Ash* (run 2)

Benchmark passages the Writer calibrates against before every chapter, and the Evaluator and Editor
measure against. They are **targets for register, not text to reuse.** No sentence, image or line in
these samples may be copied into the manuscript. The echo scan (`tic_report.py --echoes N`, step 5c of
`check_chapter.sh`) lists any 5+-word phrase a chapter shares with a sample, tagged "voice-bank".

## The voice in one paragraph
Mara Reyes, first person, past tense, told in order. A dry, curious, late-1990s American intelligence
inside a precisely rendered 1st-century surface, in the Outlander register: **median ~15-word sentences**
(gear varies by chapter per the ledger), **~15% short (≤6 words), ~7% long (≥35)**, **semicolons where an
em-dash would tempt (em-dash ≤4 per chapter; these samples use none)**, **characterizing simile only**
(it makes you see or smile; it never interprets a feeling), and **one or two specific period materials
per scene** (red-slip ware, birch tar, laid paper, burnt-horn tooth powder, lead fixings). Her attention
goes to what things are made of and where they were joined.

## The narrating stance (foundation §6a): read before writing a word
The telling walks beside the told. **Mara's horizon is the moment she is narrating.** She can look back
(1998, her father, Mrs. Dunphy, last winter), and she can know history, including AD 79, as a present
thought in a present scene (sample-13). She never reaches forward: no "I did not know yet", "the last
time I would", "it would prove", "in the years since", "the man I would marry", and no narrating present
("even now I can see"). **Zero forward-gestures across all thirteen samples.** Weight goes into objects and
repeated situations.

## Voice under pressure (sample-05, 06, 07)
In order of severity: (1) the dry asides die; (2) she narrates only what bodies and hands do, in plain
verbs and short sentences; (3) at the extreme, Latin goes unglossed, speech goes untagged, and the senses
narrow to one, usually sound. **It is never a list or an inventory.** The voice deflates rather than soars.

## Tic budget: ZERO (author ruling 2026-10-18)
No character, Mara included, has a verbal tic, catchphrase, refrain or signature device. Distinctness
lives in attention/worldview, syntax, evasion and physicality (see sample-08 for Valens against Mara, and
sample-09 for Fenn's frozen 1960s idiom, which has **no enumerating habit**). Motifs are objects and
situations: the watch, the film, the microcassette, the knucklebone, a hand at a threshold. They are never
phrases.

## Retired reflexes (never)
Significance-tagging · define-by-negation openers · anaphora triads · performed candour ("I want to be
exact") · an object or device used as a scene button · closing aphorisms · proverb-as-button.

## How the Writer uses this
Before drafting chapter N: read this README, then the sample matching the chapter's ledger gear and
approach (low gear → 05–07; high gear → 01, 13; dialogue-heavy → 08–09; a no-pull row → 12). After
drafting, check the chapter's median sentence length and em-dash count against the ledger row, and
re-read sample-13 if the chapter mentions the mountain.
