"Lupus," the man said again, friendly, and the one behind him drew.

Valens put the goat's rope in my hand. Then he was not beside me. I heard the first man's breath go out of him. I saw a knife on the road. The third man came round the cart at me and I swung the camera by its strap because it was the thing in my hand.

It hit him on the side of the head. He sat down. The lens cap came off and rolled away through the dust in a wide slow curve and lay still.

The goat pulled. I held on.

Valens had the second man against the wheel. I looked at the lens cap. I looked at the man sitting in the road holding his ear. He got up and ran, and the other one ran after him, and the one under the wheel did not get up.

I picked up the lens cap. I wiped it on my skirt and put it back on. My hands did that by themselves, neatly, and it was only when Valens came and stood in front of me that I saw his hands were shaking and mine were not.

I took them. They were wet. I held them until they stopped.
