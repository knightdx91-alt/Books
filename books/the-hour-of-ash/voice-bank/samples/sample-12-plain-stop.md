Kallia took the letter out of my hands and folded it along its old creases, into eighths, until it was small enough to put inside her belt. She did not ask me to read it again. She stood for a while looking at the hearth, where the fire had gone down to a red seam under the ash, and then she said that the man from Elis had been asking about a room since the morning, and that she supposed he could have the one at the back.

I said that I would air it.

"I'll do it," she said.

She took the broom from where it lay across the threshold and went in. I heard her open the shutters. I heard the broom on the floor, a long stroke and a short one, and the creak of the bed frame when she pulled it out from the wall. The goat in the yard began complaining about its water. I got up and filled the trough, and when I came back in she was in the street already, talking to the man from Elis about the price, and I sat down at the table and finished shelling the beans.
