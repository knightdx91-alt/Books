The cold came first.

It came out of the painted door the way cold comes off a cellar step in August, and the lamp flame leaned toward it and the hiss of the falling stones went thin and far away.

I put my hand out. My hand went in.

Air. Night air, cool on my knuckles. It smelled of pine and diesel. Somewhere very close a cicada was going, and under the cicada there was a hum I had not heard in nine years and knew at once: a streetlamp. Orange light on a wire fence.

"Mama."

Aelia put her hand where mine had gone. Her palm lay flat against the plaster. I watched her press, the way she pressed her face to the shutters to see the street, and the plaster was plaster, red and yellow, a painted door on a dead man's tomb.

Lucius tried. Valens tried. Valens laid his whole broad hand on it, gently, and then his shoulder, and nothing gave.

My arm was in the night to the elbow.
