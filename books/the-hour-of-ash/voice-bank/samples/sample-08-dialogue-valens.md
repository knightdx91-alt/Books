"There's nothing up there in this," he said. "Snow on the plateau by noon. The mules don't like the cave."

"Mules have opinions about caves?"

"Mules have opinions about everything." He was rubbing tallow into a girth strap that did not look as if it needed it. "Why the cave?"

"I left something there."

"In the cave of the nymphs. In November." He did not look up. "What?"

I could have said a hundred things. I could have told him the truth, which would have got me shut in a room by the sanctuary with the other mad women who came to Delphi to hear voices. I unbuckled my father's watch instead and held it out across the yard, and the low sun came off the steel case and went across his face, and he stopped working the strap.

He came and took it from my palm without touching my fingers. He turned it over. He held it to his ear and his eyebrows went up a very little.

"It's alive," he said.

"It needs winding. Every day. That little crown on the side."

He tried it. He turned it the wrong way, carefully, with a thumb that was too big for it, and I made a noise, and he stopped.

"Show me," he said. "Then we go at first light, and you pay me this, and I keep it."
