The mountain came up out of the haze while we were still off Misenum, and I did not know it at first. I had seen it a hundred times in photographs, a broken gray cone with a smaller one inside it like a stone in a split fruit. This was a single broad summit, green to the shoulder, terraced with vines all the way up the southern face, and there was a small white villa halfway up with something red drying on its roof. Smoke from somebody's kitchen went up beside it in a thin straight line and leaned over at the top.

I took the camera out of my bag. I had twenty-two frames left. I put my eye to the viewfinder and framed the summit and the villa and the smoke, and I held it there until my arms ached.

Seventy-nine, I thought. That was all; the number, sitting in my head like a coin under the tongue.

"Fine mountain for vines," Valens said, beside me at the rail.

I lowered the camera. "Yes," I said. "It is."

I put the cap back on the lens and the camera back in the bag, and I watched the villa with the red roof until the headland took it.
