## Opening (Hook)

> I reached the quay from the garden road with the taste of blood in my mouth from running, and the whole harbor reeked of pitch
> going soft in the sun. The stone was dry and glaring underfoot, and the wind blew straight in off the water into our faces; across
> the basin somebody was hammering a barrel hoop, two blows and a rest, two blows and a rest, as if nothing in Achaea were in a hurry.
>
> What I saw first, and clearly, was the goose, rising above the stern of a ship halfway along the quay. It was carved out of the
> sternpost itself, the whole neck one unbroken curve that came back over the deck, so that the head eyed the water. […]

- **Type: SENSORY IMMERSION (arrival, body-first).** This agrees with the writer's typing.
  - The reader is in Mara's mouth (blood), nose (pitch) and feet (glare) before learning anything about the quay.
  - The field holds the one wrong detail the type requires: the cooper's unhurried two-blows-and-a-rest. It runs against a woman
    who can taste her own lungs. The harbor's indifference is the tension.
  - The first sentence is an I-statement of 28 words and an in-moment fact of arrival, which meets the ledger.
  - It does not recap the run. The run is present only as what it left in her body.
- **Why not IN MEDIAS RES (barred).** The hook window does not drop the reader into an action already under way. The run is
  over ("I reached"), and the first five sentences are a sensory field followed by the goose. The live action (Felix riding the
  plank) enters at sentence six, after the hook has done its work. The hook works through sense and contrast, not through catching up
  on events.
- **Why not CONTRAST.** The hammer against the hurry is a contrast, but it sits inside the sensory field as its wrong note. It is not a
  two-term collision that carries the hook alone. One clean type.
- **Ch.17's sail is answered, not waved away.** The goose identifies the ship (Ch.17: "the one with the goose carved on her stern").
  In paragraph 4, "on the very ship that was not going to wait for us, they were hoisting the sail" shows whose sail it is through
  action. The pressure then becomes the master's deadline ("You have till the yard's at the top"). This pays Ch.17's cliffhanger in
  full, not in one line.
- **Score: 8/10.**
  - Information gap 2/3: is the goose ship still at the quay, and is it the sail? The answer is held back for three paragraphs.
  - Emotional pull 2/3: the blood taste against a world in no hurry. No emotion is named.
  - Voice 2/2: Mara's eye for how things are made (pitch going soft, the hoop's rhythm, the neck carved from the sternpost "one
    unbroken curve"), with no credential aside.
  - Connection 2/2: it picks up the run in the body without re-narrating it.
- **Rewritten:** no.
- **Alternatives considered (not applied):**
  - CONTRAST: open on the cooper's hammer (est. 7). This breaks the ledger's I-statement and moves the wrong note to the front
    before the reader has the body.
  - VOICE: a dry I-statement about running all that way to watch a boy ride a plank (est. 7). It re-narrates the run, which the
    outline forbids, and spends the plank image before it is seen.
  - QUESTION: "I reached the quay in time to see our ship hoisting her sail." (est. 7). This answers Ch.17's sail in the first line,
    the throwaway the Ch.17 ripple note warned against, and it reads as a summary.

## Ending (Pull)

> The fig was all that was left on the quay, in its jar by the ring, and me.
>
> Valens was on the plank again, coming for it, and took the jar's rim on his side, and I took mine, and the little tree shook all its
> leaves between us.
>
> "Tread on the cleats," he said. "It gives in the middle."
>
> We went up together under the goose, with the plank bouncing under the weight of us both and the jar between us.
>
> They drew the plank in after us.

- **Type: DECISION POINT (enacted, irrevocable).** The writer called it "action". By function it is the closing of a door behind
  a choice, which matches the type's model ("She put the envelope in the mailbox. The metal door clanged shut."). The choice is made
  in ten words mid-chapter. The last line is the moment it can no longer be undone. Mara, the last thing of hers left on Greek stone
  ("and me"), is carried aboard with the fig. Then the only way back is taken up by other hands. The Greek door she came through
  stays on the far side of that plank.
- **Why this is a pull at a hinge, with no foreshadowing.**
  - **The decision is debatable**, which is the type's test. The reader knows what Italy in AD 72 is carrying her toward, and knows
    that a marriage now gives her one more reason not to leave by any door. The line says none of this. The reader's own knowledge
    does the work, so foreshadowing stays at 0.
  - The next page is the PART TWO · WHEN title, and the withheld WHEN is the book's open question. The close does not need to
    point at it.
- **Anti-pattern checks.**
  - **Symmetrical ending (#5):** checked and kept. The crew spent the whole opening trying to draw this plank aboard while Felix held
    it down. The same act now happens with the opposite meaning: no longer the threat but the seal. The callback changes the meaning
    of the act; it is not structural neatness.
  - **Gentle landing (#10):** no. The run of sentences compresses from the fig line (17w) to the shared climb (21w) to the 7-word
    close, and the last sentence is the tightest.
  - **Aphorism or profound closing line:** none. "It gives in the middle" is Valens's literal instruction about the plank. It is not
    the button and not a proverb.
  - **No emotion named.**
  - **Threads left live:** Puteoli's scrutiny (the mother-in-law, "the magistrate's people"), Celer's bill of sale, the watch on the
    wrong wrist, and the door.
- **Score: 8/10.**
  - Forward pull 2/3: no suspense hangs on the line. The pull is irrevocability plus the part title.
  - Emotional charge 3/3: "and me" set beside the fig, then the plank taken up behind them. The residue is Greece closing.
  - Unpredictability 1/2: boarding is expected. The reversal of the plank's meaning is the surprise.
  - Promise keeping 2/2: it pays the run, the wedding, the watch and the mule, and opens Italy.
- **Midnight Test:** passes at the hinge. The reader turns over to see what "WHEN" means now that she is married and cannot go back.
- **Rewritten:** no.
- **Alternatives considered (not applied):**
  - EMOTIONAL GUT PUNCH: a backward look at the iron ring standing empty on the quay (est. 6). It is a camera pulling back, so it
    decompresses. "Ring" invites a wedding-ring irony that reads as narrator significance.
  - Forward motion: the sail let fall after the plank (est. 6–7). It spends Ch.19's departure, loses the plank's reversal, and a
    brailed sail "coming down" reads as lowering, which is ambiguous.
  - QUESTION PLANT: close on Felix's tunic line (est. 6). It is comic and already spent two paragraphs up as a spoken button. It
    would also make a line of dialogue the close, which breaks the `punch` signature.
