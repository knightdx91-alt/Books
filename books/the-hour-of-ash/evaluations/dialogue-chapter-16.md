## Language decision (made consistent)
- **Mara and Plautia share Latin.** Plautia opens in elaborate Greek, which Mara loses. On "When it runs, I lose it." she moves to slow book Latin ("each clause was mine before the next arrived"). Every Plautia scene after C1 is in that Latin. The C4 plan stays in it too, and "She gave his name its weight, every sound of it, as she gave every word" already marks her as slow even when periodic. The closing paragraph's "Latin plain enough for the stableboy" is plain against ornate, not slow against fast, so it agrees.
- **Mara's rule:** she follows plain, slow speech and loses fast or elaborate talk. On the page:
  - She catches the one plain clause of Plautia's Greek evasion ("Anything I have seen is a matter for the court,") and loses the rest at "the second bend of it".
  - She gets only "something about foreigners" from the master. That narration is deliberately kept as summary, as evidence of the rule.
  - She follows Myrrhine's short household Greek, Valens's plain Greek, and Felix's short stacked questions after a summer of them.
- **Myrrhine follows Latin only by the word:** "She had caught the cloak and the cook, and no more." (C1, new), and the unchanged "She had followed that much." (C4).
- **Valens speaks Latin to the mule** ("Up, now, you old fool. Up,"). Mara, who has Latin, renders it.
