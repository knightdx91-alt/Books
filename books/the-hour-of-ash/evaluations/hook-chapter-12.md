## Opening (Hook)

> "Lupus? By the Twins, it is. Lupus, walking behind mules."

- **Type: QUESTION.** This is the type the Ch.11 report recommended, and the honest reading.
  - The detail that does not fit is the name. The reader has seen "Lupus" exactly once, on the runaway
    notice in Ch.9: *answering to the name Lupus … whoever takes him should take him with others … five
    hundred drachmas.*
  - On this road, the name arrives in a friendly mouth with an oath and a joke. The question it plants is
    who out here knows him by that name, and what he wants with it.
  - Paragraph 2 answers the notice's "with others" without pointing at it ("two others at his heels").
  - The question is answered inside the chapter: he knows the name from the benches at Corinth, and the
    knife shows what he came for. No broken promise.
- **Why it is not IN MEDIAS RES.** The structural approach is in medias res, but the hook type is not.
  Nothing is under way in the first line. It is a greeting. What holds the reader is the wrong name in the
  right tone, not action already in motion. The barred type is avoided honestly, and the repeat does not need
  to be logged as ledger-forced.
- **Why it is not CONTRAST.** The friendly/lethal collision exists, but only across the chapter. Inside the
  first three sentences there is only the friendliness, so the stall comes from the name alone.
- **Score: 9/10.**
  - Information gap 3/3: a bounty name, spoken warmly, by a stranger.
  - Emotional pull 2/3: unease, not alarm. The alarm arrives in paragraph 2 ("gaining on us since the
    bend"). That is the right ceiling for a greeting.
  - Voice 2/2: "By the Twins" fixes him as a sociable Greek fan. "walking behind mules" is the fan's small
    insult (from the sand to the pack-string) and sets up his whole register.
  - Connection 2/2: Ch.11 closed on the Kirrha road, mules and daybreak. Ch.12 opens on that road behind
    those mules, with no recap.
- **Ledger signature:** `dialogue` ✓ (opens on a quote).
- **Rewritten:** no.
- **Alternatives considered (not applied):**
  - Lead with the narration ("The man had been gaining on us since the bend…") and move the line second
    (est. 6). This breaks the `dialogue` signature and spends the name's surprise on a description.
  - Trim to "Lupus? By the Twins, it is." (est. 7). This loses "behind mules", which is the insult that
    makes him a person and not a plot device.

## Ending (Pull)

> The whole plain lay under us, gray with olives and going blue. Out past it the gulf still held some light,
> and the boats were in at Kirrha with their sails furled. The road ran white from the shore to the foot of
> the hill and the first of the terrace walls. I knew the spot by the turn of the wall below the olives.
>
> A cart had stopped beside the ditch.

### Is the cart a concrete punch or an ominous promise?

**It is a concrete punch, and it is not an ominous promise.**

- **The cart is paid off, not promised.** The graybeard said at the ford, 76 lines earlier, "My son's run
  back to Kirrha for the headman and a cart." The cart is that cart, arriving. The sentence reports a
  completed present fact ("had stopped") in the scene, seen from the wall. It reaches forward to nothing.
- **It adds no new threat.** The threats are the two who ran and Celer. The cart is neither. Valens's
  back-watching earlier in the section is present-scene vigilance, already on the page. The last line does
  not add to it, point at it, or echo it.
- **It completes outline beat 4** ("They leave the body for the road's owners to find"). The road's owners
  have found him.
- **POV is honest.** From that height Mara cannot know whose cart it is, so "A cart" is right. The preceding
  sentence ("I knew the spot by the turn of the wall") does the locating. The reader supplies the
  graybeard's line, and the reader who does is rewarded.
- **Residual risk (minor).** A reader who has forgotten the graybeard could take a stopped cart at the ditch
  as discovery pending. Even that reading is closure (the body is being found) rather than dread of
  something coming. I do not judge the risk worth a word of explanation.
