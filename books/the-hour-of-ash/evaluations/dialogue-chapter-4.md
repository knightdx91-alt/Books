## Changes Made
### Subtext (surface: names, months, roads, food · real: "goddess or trouble?" against "what year is it?", and neither is answered)
1. Shard 2: the boy no longer just answers. After "Kleodamos" he goes up on his toes to see past her into the arch and asks "Monē? Monē ei?", which is his trouble question. Mara answers it and pushes her own in the same breath ("Yes. Alone. I am alone. And the month?"). He gives the month and pushes back: "Boathoos. Ti theleis?" These are two conversations running side by side. (Technique: overlapping topics.)
2. "Pothen?" he said again → "Pothen?" with no tag. The repetition now carries itself, so the child repeats himself without the narration pointing at it.
3. Shard 2 ending: Mara protests the rubbed-out word ("It is only a name. The name of a town."). His answer is the stick and "Nümfi". That answers a different question and keeps the uncanny reading alive. (Technique: deflection.)
4. Kaisar: "Késar. En Rhōmē." The boy hands her own words back as an obvious fact, which gives no date. Her "Which Kaisar? His name. Tell me his—" is cut off by a goat. **The year stays unanswered**, and the shrug is gone. (Techniques: non-answer, interruption.)
5. Thorn: Mara offers her hands in textbook Greek ("I will draw the thorn out. I am skilled at small work."). "He talked to it" → "He talked to the thorn instead." So the existing line "none of it was meant for me" now lands on her ignored offer. (Technique: silence as response.)

### Food and goats (the boy's concrete attention)
6. Hunger: he answers her "*Peinō*" by laying a hand flat over his bag: "Ti dōseis?" Her English answer is "Anything. Anything you want." This motivates the granola-for-bread offer in the next shard. Before, she simply opened the duffel.
7. Granola: "He looked at me, and bit." → "He did not wait for an answer; he bit." After he hands back the rest: "*Kopros*." This is a child's verdict and stays unglossed; context does the work.
8. Last shard: "Makran." is now followed by "And to the goats, in the same breath, 'Sitta! Sitta, oikade.'" This is a Theocritean herding call. He is done with her and turning for home when she stops him with the name question.

### Voice adjustments
9. Mara: "Keep it" (natural English) → "Take it." / "It is yours." (stiff Greek, which keeps the mismatch running). "Delphoi. Thank you" → "Delphoi. I thank you." "What is your name, boy?" → "What is your name?" ("boy" as an address was used twice; it stays only in "Tell me, boy", so no epithet forms).
