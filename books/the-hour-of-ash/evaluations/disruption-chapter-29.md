## Ruling on hook-craft's flag: "the bakers' shutter" vs "the baker's door"

**Changed to "the baker's shutter"** (apostrophe only; closing line otherwise word for word). Reasons:
- Everywhere else in the chapter "the baker" is the master: "I crossed to the baker", "the baker's man", and "went in at the baker's door" four lines earlier. The plural reads as a typo far more often than as the household.
- The singular is the sharper close. The shutter is his master's again, the festival is over, and the man feeding the oven goes back to being "somebody". The plural blurred who owns the shutter, and ownership is exactly what the chapter's shame is about.
- The header comment's quotation of the closing line was updated to match.

## Strongest Disruption

## Risk Assessment
- **The soot order (#4)** is new business in an already full S1. If the evaluator thinks the crowning runs long, cut the fuller's paragraph and keep the soot. That saves about 25 words, mostly narration, and dialogue stays above 36%.
- **"threw the dog"** relies on the reader taking "the dog" as a bad throw from the swearing. Grata's "Venus" is the payoff and nobody explains either. If it reads as a literal dog, change it to "threw badly".
- **"the Emperor two, and he's cheap at that"** is a joke about Vespasian's money-grubbing and is left unglossed by design.
- **Bible sync (for the evaluator's FINAL pass):** new walk-on lines for the fuller's lads, the figure seller and the fuller ("I've a vat in soak"). New Felix line ("Then for not laughing.") and Mara line ("I did no such thing."). Fenn line changed ("kept his English to himself" replaces "for once he let a thing be"). "the bakers' shutter" is now "the baker's shutter". Cerdo's coppers-in-palm beat is gone.
