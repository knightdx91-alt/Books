## Changes Made

### S2, the legal exposition (the writer's flagged weak spot)
The law now comes in three light touches and is never restated:
1. Our advocate: the retained sixth, "kept for misconduct". The narration and Plautia's roof line already say that the law allows it, so he no longer explains it to a judge who knows it.
2. Celer's advocate: "I need not remind this court that a slave is not heard against his master." This is a preterition, which a vain young orator would use. The law is clear and the line stays characterful.
3. The single iudex and his assessors are carried by the staging (unchanged), and there is still no jury.

Speech by speech:
- **Celer's advocate, property speech:** about 150 → 95 words. Cut "article of property" and the doubled "If… If…" period. It ends "Written fair, a mistake is still a mistake."
- **Our advocate, leaf speech:** about 95 → 60 words. Cut "boards five and six" and the semicolon lecture. It is now four plain sentences: the book, the knot, what the leaf carries.
- **Celer's advocate, after the witnesses:** about 70 → 30 words. "Neither of them can say whose man was written down. My client's bill can." He argues instead of re-explaining.
- **The iudex, uncle line:** de-relayed and tightened to "My friend's uncle was consul that year, for his two months… Your bill makes him consul in a month he spent at his villa at Tibur…"
- **Our advocate, dowry:** "One other person under that roof knew what was true. He is here. The court can hear him tomorrow." The last sentence now sets up Celer's advocate's "will not hear" directly.

### Subtext
- **S6, the gallery:** "the word of such a man against the word of a senator of Rome" became "As the court pleases. The court has heard the question. It knows my client's family, and it can see the witness." The gladiator-versus-senator weighing now sits under the line instead of in it. Our advocate's "consul's dates against a steward's" still turns the weighing back onto the documents.
- **S4:** the reported objection became direct speech, with the iudex's "I'll hear him first… and decide afterward what he's worth." This carries the same question obliquely, in his voice.
