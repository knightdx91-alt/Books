## Opening (Hook)
- **Type:** IN MEDIAS RES. The reader enters an encounter already under way: Ch.3 stopped on the step
  out, and Ch.4 starts with her already being looked at. No establishing shot, no recap, no wonder. The
  grounding detail lands in the next sentence (a few dozen goats, black, brown and dirty white, every
  head up). "First" carries the open question, since someone else is about to see her, and paragraph 3
  answers it with the boy coming up off the ground. Not CONTRAST (Ch.1), VOICE (Ch.2) or SENSORY
  IMMERSION (Ch.3).
- **Score: 8/10** (Information gap 2/3, Emotional pull 2/3, Voice 2/2, Connection 2/2)
  - Information gap 2: "first" implies a second witness, and the reader waits for it. It is resolved
    within two paragraphs, which is right for a fast, short-punch chapter, but it limits the gap.
  - Emotional pull 2: comedy and nerves together. The nanny forgets the sprig in her mouth, and the
    kid keeps clanking "because it was scratching its ear with a hind hoof and would not be hurried".
    Then the boy comes up "all at once and already facing me". A stranger has been seen and has not
    yet been judged.
  - Voice 2: dry, exact, unimpressed. This is the observer who in Ch.3 dated a season from its light.
    The one simile ("the way a cat leaves a sofa when a door slams") characterizes rather than
    explains, uses her 1998 frame of reference, and stays inside the §4 budget.
  - Connection 2: "I stepped out into October." → "The goats saw me first." The Ch.3 ending had
    already put bells and dung under the lip, so the white space carries her one step.
- **Sentences after the opening line (paras 2–3):** each one is concrete and none is wasted. No
  portent, no foreshadowing (families A–E clear), no define-by-negation, no anaphora triad, no
  em-dash. "Every head came up together" followed by "the bells went quiet a few at a time" is
  physically accurate (the bells settle after the heads stop), not a contradiction. Nothing to fix.
- **Ledger check:** "The goats saw me first." = 5 words, alone in its paragraph, does not start with
  "I", so `short-punch` ✓. It is verbatim from the outline's opening strategy.
- **Rewritten:** no.
- **Alternatives considered (for the follow-on sentences only; the first line is fixed):**
  - QUESTION, with the boy's spit moved up into paragraph 2 (6). It puts the uncanny reading before
    the reader has seen the boy, and it breaks the shard order the dialogue pass set.
  - TICKING CLOCK, with the low sun and her hunger pressed into paragraph 2 (5). The clock would be
    invented, and the outline holds the hunger back as the chapter's emotional surprise in shard 3.

## Ending (Pull)
- **Type:** REVELATION. When she asks his name, he points the stick at the cave and then at her
  breastbone. He is not telling her who he is; he is telling her what she is. The word "Nümfi", which
  slid past her twice, comes together one letter at a time and turns out to be the same ΝΥΜΦ- cut
  with red paint beside the arch. This changes how the whole chapter reads. The spit into his tunic,
  "Mē graphe" with the word rubbed out, his helpless laughter when she pointed "Nümfi?" back at him,
  and "Monē ei?": a child has met a woman who came out of the Nymphs' cave and has been asking whether
  she is a goddess or trouble (the outline's subtext layer). Not QUESTION PLANT (Ch.1), EMOTIONAL GUT
  PUNCH (Ch.2) or CLIFFHANGER (Ch.3).
- **Score: 8/10** (Forward pull 2/3, Emotional charge 2/3, Unpredictability 2/2, Promise 2/2)
  - Forward pull 2: a stranger in jeans with no name and no road has been named a nymph, which in
    Greek is also "bride" and "young woman". The reader needs to see what that word does to her down
    in Delphi. The road down was given a beat earlier ("Delphous. Katō, katō."), so the reveal comes
    on top of the forward direction rather than replacing it.
  - Emotional charge 2: the slow assembly is the payoff of the outline's emotional anchor. A woman who
    can spell the word cannot hear it until the third try, and the letters come out of the sound "one
    at a time". The boy is "loud and patient, for the deaf". The feeling is recognition mixed with
    comedy, held without comment.
  - Unpredictability 2: the reader expects the boy's name, as she does. What arrives is her own label.
  - Promise 2: it keeps the chapter's promise (the language barrier is real and her book Greek is a
    liability) and opens a new one: how she will be received.
- **Sentences before the closing line (paras 177–181):** an action (the stick sent up to the black
  cave mouth with one star over it and brought round onto her fleece), her request, then the phonetic
  shards (nu, upsilon "rounded tight, nothing like mine", mu, phi "the same strokes as the ones cut
  beside the arch", an eta "that was not the eta of any grammar I owned"). This is perception, not
  reflection. There is no narrator comment, no year, no aphorism and no em-dash, and the energy tightens
  toward the word instead of fading out (no Gentle Landing). The one star over the cave is something in
  her eyeline at dusk, not portent. Theme RECEDES ✓.
- **Ledger check:** the final paragraph is `"Nymphē."` alone in quotes and is the last line of the file
  with nothing after it, so `dialogue` ✓. It is verbatim from outline beat 5 (NON-NEGOTIABLE).
- **Midnight Test:** turns the page. The reader can spell the word too, and wants to watch Delphi meet
  it.
- **Rewritten:** no.
- **Alternatives considered (for the lead-in only; the last line is fixed):**
  - DECISION POINT: put her choice to follow the stick down ahead of the naming (6). The choice is
    obvious (she has nowhere else to go), so it carries no moral weight, and it would put action
    between the letters and the word.
  - OMINOUS PROMISE: have her register what a "nymph" walking into a sanctuary might mean (4).
    That is a narrator reaching forward (§0), a reflective turn the RECEDES row forbids, and it
    breaks "nothing after it".
- **Minor note, no action:** "He said it a third time" counts across the chapter (p17 "Nümfi?", p71

  "Say it" reads as "say what you just showed me". That is clear enough, and the line is outside the
  2–4 sentences this pass may touch only if a score falls below the floor.
