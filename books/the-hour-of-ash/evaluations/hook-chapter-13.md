## Opening (Hook)

> The morning on which Apollo would first answer anybody that year came in under a frost so hard that Kallia's jars
> rang on the paving at the spring, and the water from the lion-head spouts smoked in the dark.
>
> The priests were higher up, at the rock basin, and they were more noise than shape: … Down at the lions it was
> Kallia and me, and the cold, and the long noise of the water.
>
> I had not had her to myself in three days.

- **Type: SENSORY IMMERSION (date-framed).** This agrees with the writer's typing.
  - Sound and temperature come before any situation: jars that ring on frozen paving, water that smokes, priests heard
    rather than seen.
  - The tension the rule requires comes from the frame clause. This is the morning the god answers, and the reader
    has spent Ch.11–12 earning this question.
- **Why it is not QUESTION.** Nothing in the first lines is impossible or out of place. The reader already knows the
  day is coming (Ch.12: "It was three days to the seventh of Bysios."). What pulls is arrival, not a puzzle.
- **Why it is not TICKING CLOCK.** The date has already arrived, so nothing is counting down.
- **Weather-opening check (anti-pattern 2).** The frost passes because it is the threat. Praxias names it 50 lines
  later: "Even money she stands like a post … They do, in a frost." The goat test, which the whole consultation
  depends on, is at risk because of the cold. The opener's weather is load-bearing, not an establishing shot, and a
  person is acting in it (Kallia's jars).
- **Score: 8/10.**
  - Information gap 2/3: the god's answer is the gap. The sentence does not plant a new one, and as a scene-set it
    should not.
  - Emotional pull 2/3: the cold carries anticipation. A lowered charge is right after Ch.12's killing.
  - Voice 2/2: "first answer anybody" is Mara's dry deflation of an oracle. The jars and the spouts are her
    materials eye.
  - Connection 2/2: Ch.12 bridged to the date. The title spends "the seventh of Bysios" (allowlist use 2 of 3), and
    the opener paraphrases it freshly without the spent "god's day" family. Paragraph 3 covers the three-day gap
    without a recap ("I had not had her to myself in three days.").
- **Threshold:** 7 for commercial/historical romance. 8 clears it, so under the respect rule there is **no rewrite**.
- **Rewritten:** no.
- **Alternatives considered (not applied):**
  - Shorter scene-set, "The god's year opened under a frost so hard that Kallia's jars rang on the paving." (est. 7).
    It is tighter, but "the god's year" sits next to the CLOSED "god's day" family and loses "anybody".
  - Contrast, the frost against a god of light, as two sentences (est. 7). It would turn the date into a conceit and
    break the `scene-set` signature's plainness.
- **Noted, not changed:** "noise" is used twice in sentences 2–3 ("more noise than shape" / "the long noise of the
  water"). It reads as deliberate (the priests are noise; at the lions the only noise is water, so Mara has Kallia
  alone). It is not a cross-chapter echo, so it was left alone.

## Ending (Pull)

> "You've had your answer to that," said Theano, watching the crows. "Ask the other."
>
> I had one question left.
>
> "Which city?"
>
> Theano shifted on the stone, and grunted with it. "Pompeii."

- **Type: REVELATION.** This agrees with the writer's typing and is the type the Ch.12 report recommended.
  - New information changes the whole picture behind it. The priest's Syracuse gloss (Etna, which Mara has just shown
    faces the wrong sea) collapses.
  - "The mountain of vines by the western sea" turns into a mountain the reader can name. "Where the dead go out of
    the city" becomes a street of tombs outside a gate the reader can picture.
  - The reader (AD 71, eight years out) knows what the word means. Mara does too. The chapter does not say so.
- **Why it is not EMOTIONAL GUT PUNCH (barred).** The charge is information, delivered flat, with no reaction after
  it. No feeling is staged, and no detail does the grieving. The horror is held for Ch.14 by design (outline: "Here it
  is only the word"). Foreshadow stays at 0.
- **Score: 9/10.**
  - Forward pull 3/3: Midnight Test. Nobody turns off the light on "Pompeii." in AD 71.
  - Emotional charge 3/3: all of it is dramatic irony. One word lands on top of a grunt, a weight shift, and a widow
    whose pills have not yet worked.
  - Unpredictability 1/2: the city is the book's premise, so the word itself is expected. The surprise is the source
    and the register: a tired farmer's widow, mid-grumble, after the formal answer has misled everyone. That source is
    the outline's emotional surprise.
  - Promise keeping 2/2: the chapter's promise (the WHERE) is kept exactly, and the WHEN is refused twice (Valens's
    "No.", then Theano), so the next promise (the hour) is opened.
- **Compression:** 5 words → 2 → 9 → 1. Each line is tighter than the one before, and the last is the tightest.
  There is no gentle landing.

### The edit (penultimate sentence only)

- **Before:** `Theano put a hand down on her knee to shift her weight on the stone, and grunted with it. "Pompeii."`
- **After:** `Theano shifted on the stone, and grunted with it. "Pompeii."`
- **Why (NO TICS, gesture layer):** "hand … on his/her knee" is the chapter's most-used piece of stage business.
  - Valens has it 7 times in the court (l.192 lifted from his knee, l.196 back on his knee, l.204 spoon on his knee,
    l.316 hands on his knees, l.376 lifted an inch from his knee, l.380 the hand on his knee, l.392 forearm on his
    knee).
  - Theano has it 3 times in her scene (l.440 hands on her knees, l.450 pressed her knees, and the closer).
  - The tic gate's gesture classes do not count this pose, so the gate passed it. On the page, though, the book's
    turning-point line was arriving on the chapter's tenth hand-to-knee.
  - The closer is in hook-craft territory, so I cut the pose there and kept everything else the writer chose: the
    shift, the stone (her husband's), the grunt "with it" (effort, not an answer) and the word.
- **Echo guard:** "shifted her weight" was rejected because it echoes the SPENT Ch.11 anchor ("shifted her weight off
  the sore foot", l.266) and Ch.12 ("shifted his weight"). "shifted on the" / "on the stone, and" / "and grunted"
  have no prior uses in the book (grep), and the echo scan reports 0.
- **What is kept:** the dialogue-polish line "I had one question left." and the `dialogue` signature (the chapter ends
  on the quote). Nothing follows "Pompeii.".
- **Alternatives considered (not applied):**
  - Bare, `"Which city?" / "Pompeii."` (est. 8). It loses the tired body, which is the whole subversion (the truth
    from an old woman with bad knees, not a trance).
  - The crows, "A crow came down for a husk she had missed. 'Pompeii.'" (est. 6). It makes the crows a scene-button
    (a retired reflex) and risks the Ch.8 "eyes on the crows" echo.
  - The pre-edit text (est. 8). Same type and same beat, but it closes on the chapter's tenth knee touch.
