## Changes Made
### Subtext (surface: water, hands, teeth, chores · real: "will this runaway bring trouble to my door?", never said)
1. "Who's coming after you?" → **"Who saw you come down here?"** Kallia now asks a practical question about witnesses. Mara's outburst ("No one. There isn't a person on earth who knows where I am…") answers the hidden question without either woman naming it. (Technique: displacement.)
2. Sieve quarrel: "You'll mend it, or your wife will hear about it at the spring…" → "Mend it. Your wife and I fill our jars at the same spring." The task comes first and the threat is left implied. This also removes an "X, or Y" construction.
3. Kallia's question run is less orderly: "Grind?" / "No." / "Cook?" → "Grind? Cook?" She stops waiting for the noes.

### Voice adjustments
- **Press woman vs Kallia (bleed).** She had answered "I have no man" with silence and a pointed task, which is Kallia's move. She now says **"Lucky."** (her useless husband, her own life) and names a wage: "You eat when they're done." That makes her transactional and self-referring, where Kallia gives tasks with no talk of pay and never mentions herself. Her "up north" was cut because the guide uses it too. Her parting "You'll want more than bread" is now "take money", since "You'll want" appeared in three voices.
- **Herald:** stops answering. "Citizens only." was cut; "The rest of you can smell it cooking" moves to the man by the money-changer's table, who earlier called out *Kalōs!* (that replaces "said *good*, quite loudly"; unglossed, and clear because it greets the news).
- **Temple slave:** gets one command ("Down."), and his rod line is now imperatives with no pricing.
- **Clay-figure seller:** "the singer" ×2 → "Since the bronzes were carted off…". The at-risk cap on "the singer" is 2, and the guide has spent both.
- **Guide:** "some king up north" → "some king from past the Istros" (unglossed).
- **Procurer:** "Suit yourself" (modern-sounding) → "As you like"; "You'll want somewhere for the night" → "It gets cold in this square after dark".
- **Mara:** "Take your hand off it" (it echoed her own "Get your hands off me") → "No. I'll carry it." "A man with a stick. At the temple." → "…Over a paint job." (her dry aside, materials). "My father's watch" → "My father's Seiko" (that phrase was at its book-wide cap of 2 with Ch.3).
- **Kallia:** "Then wake her." → "Wake her." (removes a "Then…" pivot). "With her mouth all honey" (idiom, close to a proverb) → "borrowed it to strain her must" (a household noun).
