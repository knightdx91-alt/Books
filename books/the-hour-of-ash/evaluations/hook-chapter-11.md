## Opening (Hook)

**Original first sentence (75 words):**
> On the other side of Praxias's wall a mule yard was already at work in the half dark: a shovel went
> along cobbles in long scrapes, buckets were being set down on stone, a boy was telling some animal at
> the top of his voice that it was the worst mule between Parnassus and the sea, and once, very low, a
> man spoke, and the boy answered him with three questions before he had finished.

### Honest verdict on the writer's intent (IN MEDIAS RES or VOICE)

**It reads as plain SENSORY IMMERSION**, which repeats Ch.10.

- **The subject is the yard.** The sentence's grammatical subject is the yard, and its whole body is a
  catalogue of sounds. Mara is not in it, and nothing is under way for *her*.
- **"Already" belongs to the yard.** It puts the yard mid-action, not the reader, so it works as an
  establishing soundscape heard from outside.
- **The scene under way arrives late.** The actual situation (a price being named) only appears in
  sentence 3, "named me his price". By then the reader has already filed the paragraph as atmosphere.
- **Not VOICE either.** The liveliest phrase, "the worst mule between Parnassus and the sea", is Felix's,
  reported. Mara's own voice shows only in the precision of "three questions before he had finished".
  That is good observation, but it is not a personality carrying the hook.
- **It also lacks what SENSORY needs.** The type requires one wrong or ominous detail in the field, and
  there is none. The yard is cheerful.

**Score as found: 6/10** (Information gap 2/3, Emotional pull 1/3, Voice 2/2, Connection 1/2). That is
below the genre threshold of 7, and it is a barred repeat.

### Edit (one subordinate clause; sound stays the spine)

**New first sentence (85 words):**
> On the other side of Praxias's wall, **while he made up his mind what to charge me,** a mule yard was
> already at work in the half dark: a shovel went along cobbles in long scrapes, buckets were being set
> down on stone, a boy was telling some animal at the top of his voice that it was the worst mule between
> Parnassus and the sea, and once, very low, a man spoke, and the boy answered him with three questions
> before he had finished.

Sentences 2 and 3 are unchanged: "Praxias heard none of it. He sat on the bench outside his door with a
quail in the crook of his arm and named me his price."

- **Type: IN MEDIAS RES.** The reader now enters a transaction already under way by word 14. Someone is
  deciding what Mara will pay, and the reader catches up by inference. ¶3 ("He was Kallia's cousin…
  Kallia had sent me to him…") does the backfill that the type expects.
- **The sound is now the grounding.** The yard catalogue becomes the sensory ground the type calls for,
  where before it was the whole hook.
- **Sentence 2 sharpens.** "Praxias heard none of it" now marks a split. He is thinking about money, and
  she is listening over the wall to a low-voiced man and a boy with questions. That seeds the chapter's
  slow reveal of a person without pointing at it.
- **Sentence 3 closes the setup.** "Named me his price" now resolves the deliberation the first sentence
  opened, and "Five drachmas" lands as its answer.
- **Score: 7/10** (Information gap 2/3, Emotional pull 2/3, Voice 2/2, Connection 1/2).
  - Information gap 2: two questions open at once. What is she buying from Kallia's cousin? Who are the
    man and the boy? The chapter answers both.
  - Emotional pull 2: the stakes are small but real, because she is being priced. Her attention has
    already gone over the wall.
  - Voice 2: the inserted clause is in Mara's plain, uncontracted register, with no simile and no
    credential aside. "What to charge me" is dry about being charged by family, and nothing more.
  - Connection 1: Ch.10 ends as a plain stop, so there is nothing to bridge. Ch.11 moves on through
    Kallia's family (her cousin, sent by her) without returning to the letter or the obol.
  - Honest caveat: this is a *quiet* in-medias-res. about 70 of the 85 words are still sound, as the ledger
    asks. A 7 that keeps the "unhurried, working rhythm" register is right for this chapter.
- **Ledger check:**
  - Opens on a sound: unchanged. The sentence begins at the wall and its body is the yard heard.
  - `long-spiral`: shape_check confirms it. The first sentence is 85 words, and "On the other…" does
    not match TIME_OPENERS.
  - Adds no device: no new verbal pattern, no simile, no "and" (the and-chain count is still 3 against a
    cap of 9).
  - Book-wide check: "made up his mind" and "what to charge" appear nowhere else in the book.

### Alternatives considered (not applied)
- **CONTRAST:** "…while he priced the god's house for me by the drachma, …" (est. 6).
  - It pre-empts Praxias's own "on his feet in the god's own house" in ¶14, an in-chapter repeat.
  - It has the narrator editorialize the sacred-versus-money point before the scene earns it.
- **VOICE:** "…I listened to the wrong side of his wall…" (est. 6).
  - "Wrong side" is wit that points at where her interest lies. That is close to significance-tagging,
    and it tells the reader the romance before the chapter shows it.
  - An "I…" first word would flip the signature to `I-statement` and break the ledger.
- **Leave it and log the repeat as ledger-forced** (the fallback in the Ch.10 report). Rejected, because
  an honest non-sensory version fitted inside the ledger with a 10-word insertion.

## Ending (Pull)

**Last lines (unchanged):**
> He went to the gate to look at the road, as he did every night before he put the bar across. The dog
> got up off its side of the gutter and went with him.
>
> "We take the string to Kirrha for oil the day after tomorrow, at daybreak," he said. "Bring your
> drachmas, and wear something you can walk back up in behind a goat."

- **Type: OMINOUS PROMISE (character-voiced, practical, unpointed).**
  - The line is pure logistics: when, where, what to bring, what to wear.
  - The dread comes entirely from facts already on the page this chapter. The board in the square told
    whoever went to fetch him to bring friends. He has barred the gate by day. On the seventh morning he
    took the string by the olive path, though "all week he had used the road". In the sentence just
    before, he looks at the road "as he did every night before he put the bar across".
  - Now, unprompted, he names a day and an hour on that road, with her.
  - The narrator adds nothing, so this is present-scene dread, not foreshadowing. Gate: foreshadow 0.
  - A DECISION POINT reading is possible (his choice to take the road with her). But the decision that
    matters, to carry her question, was made about a dozen lines earlier. What pulls the reader is the coming
    road, not a debatable choice.
- **Score: 8/10** (Forward pull 2/3, Emotional charge 2/3, Unpredictability 2/2, Promise keeping 2/2).
  - Forward pull 2: the danger is implied, not stated. An attentive reader feels it, and a skimming one
    gets only an errand. That is the right ceiling under the no-foreshadowing law.
  - Emotional charge 2: after the refused kiss, being included in the errand is the trust the outline
    asks for ("the intimacy that grows is trust, not desire"). The dry "behind a goat" carries the warmth,
    so no romantic button is needed.
  - Unpredictability 2: after a near-kiss and a name exchange, the reader expects an explanation or a
    charged line. They get goat logistics, and a reversal of his week of caution.
  - Promise keeping 2:
    - The chapter delivers outline beat 5. He agrees with no reason given, and the watch stays in his
      pouch.
    - It opens Ch.12 exactly as the outline requires (the cheaper Kirrha goat).
- **Practical, not romantic: PASS.** The line is about oil, a goat and her drachmas.
- **Not his spent move.** The dialogue pass retired "a practical order in answer to closeness". This line
  answers the goat question two exchanges earlier, not the name moment. It is a plan, not a deflection.
- **No device as scene-button. No aphorism.**
- **Signature:** `dialogue` ✓ (the last sentence closes on a quote).
- **Midnight test:** turn the page. Everything the chapter promised is settled, and the one thing left
  open is the road he has been avoiding.
- **Rewritten:** no.
- **Alternatives considered (not applied):**
  - Cut to "Bring your drachmas." (est. 7). It is clipped enough to read as a button, and it loses the dry
    warmth and the goat, which is the practical content.
  - Add a narrated beat after the line, such as the bar going across (est. 6). That breaks the
    `dialogue` signature and turns the bar into a scene-button device.
  - Reverse the two sentences in the speech (est. 6). Ending on "at daybreak" leans on the time as a held
    ominous note, which is too pointed.
