## Opening (Hook)

> I knew the year. It came to me on Theano's doorstep, before her mouth had quite closed on the word, and it came
> whole. Seventy-nine, late in the summer, in August. Behind it the god's verse lay down flat and pointed one way.

- **Type: VOICE (confession).** This agrees with the writer's typing.
  - The hook is how she delivers it: a flat four-word admission, then a historian narrowing to the month and stopping
    there. There is no gasp, no named feeling and no "horror".
  - The reader has had "Pompeii." for one line of white space and already knows what Mara knows. The pull comes from
    hearing her hold it this quietly.
- **Why it is not SENSORY IMMERSION (barred).** Sentences 1–4 carry no sense data. The first physical detail is the
  crow on her foot, in sentence 7, outside the hook window.
- **Why it is not QUESTION.** Nothing in it is impossible or out of place. The gap is what she will do, not what
  something means.
- **Why it is not REVELATION-as-hook.** The information was revealed in Ch.13. This only confirms it in her head.
- **Score: 9/10.**
  - Information gap 2/3: the open question is her response, not a fact. That is enough here, because the fact belongs
    to the reader already.
  - Emotional pull 3/3: "and it came whole" and the date given flat do all the dread work, without one feeling word.
  - Voice 2/2: dry, precise and unemphatic. Mara dates things the way she would date a coin.
  - Connection 2/2: `"Pompeii."` → `I knew the year.` is the tightest pull-to-hook join in the book. The second
    sentence puts her back on the doorstep "before her mouth had quite closed on the word", so the two chapters meet
    in the same instant without a recap.
- **Allowlist guard:** "The dead of that town left it by its gates … and lay along the roads outside" paraphrases
  the god's verse. It does not spend "where the dead go out" (1 of 3 used in Ch.13) ✓.
- **Rewritten:** no.
- **Alternatives considered (the mandated first line kept; sentences 2–4 varied; not applied):**
  - CONTRAST, "I knew the year. Theano was still talking about her stool." (est. 7). The collision is sharper, but it
    spends the stool, which is Mara's cover to Kallia in S7, and the instant of knowing becomes a joke before the laugh
    has earned it.
  - TICKING CLOCK, "I knew the year. From here to it was eight years and a little over." (est. 7). It takes S4's
    arithmetic early and sets a clock the chapter then honestly takes apart ("could open on any day in eight years").
    That is a manufactured deadline.

## Ending (Pull)

> I woke to an empty doorway. Kallia's blanket lay on the threshold, folded in four. … Kallia stood at the gate with
> the bar in her hands.
>
> "He's gone down for the mules," she said. "You're to wait for him at the bottom of the climb. There's bread in the
> cloth."
>
> She took the bar down and let me into the lane. Above the end of it the two cliffs were coming clear of the night,
> gray at their feet first and then, very slowly, a paler gray along the rim, with the path to the plateau scratched
> up the rock in its long turns and nobody on it yet.

- **Type: DECISION POINT (enacted at a fade).** This agrees with the writer's typing.
  - The choices are made in S8 (the plan: go to Pompeii, knowing) and S9 ("I meant to go up as soon as it was light
    and find out which").
  - S10 shows the first step paid for: the bar comes down, the lane opens, and the path she chose is empty and waiting.
  - The reader has an opinion about the choice and needs to see whether it was right. If the cave is open, she goes
    home this morning, away from the man who has just gone for her mules. If it is shut, she has chosen to walk toward
    AD 79. Reasonable readers will disagree about both, and that disagreement is the pull.
- **Why it is not REVELATION (barred).** No new fact changes the picture. The empty doorway is a two-line jolt that
  Kallia resolves at once, and "gone down for the mules" is logistics.
  - What it shows instead: he has put himself into an errand she never asked him on. That is "I left him out of the
    plan. He kept being in it." made visible, but it is not a disclosure.
- **Why it is not QUESTION PLANT.** Open or shut is the consequence of the decision, not a new question. Ch.13's
  "Tomorrow." / "It's shut." already planted it.
- **The ledger's long fade with a pull, and no aphorism:**
  - The last sentence is about 55 words of concrete image: the cliffs, gray low then gray at the rim, the switchback
    path. No sentence states a meaning, and nothing reaches past the morning.
  - "nobody on it yet" refers to the next half hour, when she will be on it. It is same-morning future-in-past, not
    foreshadowing, and style_check reports foreshadow 0.
  - The energy is compressed, not released. Kallia's three short orders fall between the sleep sentence and the final
    image, so the shard rises on a command before it opens out.
- **Score: 8/10.**
  - Forward pull 2/3: Midnight Test passed. The reader wants to see the cave answer, but the pull is held quiet by
    design (a long fade, not a cliffhanger).
  - Emotional charge 2/3: the blanket folded in four, the hearth already done ("which was my work"), the man gone ahead
    for her. All of it is unpointed tenderness set against the plan that left him behind.
  - Unpredictability 2/2: she meant to go alone, and the empty doorway turns into his errand.
  - Promise keeping 2/2: the chapter's promises (the year, the plan, the "Tomorrow" errand) are all kept. The
    open-or-shut promise passes to Ch.15.
- **Rewritten:** no.
- **Alternatives considered (not applied):**
  - The same type with Mara as the agent, "She took the bar down, and I went out into the lane." (est. 8). It
    takes the gate away from Kallia, whose bar and gate are her guardianship and carry the chapter's doorway staging.
    "went out" also comes close to the exit-by-gait class that Ch.14 bans.
  - QUESTION PLANT, closing on Kallia's "There's bread in the cloth." (est. 7). It breaks the `long-fade` signature
    (it computes as `dialogue`) and gives the last word to bread.
  - OMINOUS PROMISE, cutting "and nobody on it yet" (est. 6). Without the empty path the image loses the waiting
    choice and reads as a dawn postcard. "coming clear of the night" alone also tips toward symbolic weather.
