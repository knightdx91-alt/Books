## Opening (Hook)

> "And a bear, a live one, in a cage on a cart. They had him in at the barracks gate with me right there by it, and he put his paw
> out through the bars at me in passing, and I never moved, the porter saw me not move. […]"
>
> Felix filled the shop opening with the morning behind him and a mule's nosebag in each fist. I had vomited into the night pot at
> cockcrow, quietly, while he slept on, and I sat on the cobbler's old last sipping water, waiting for the shop to hold still.

- **Type: IN MEDIAS RES.** We come in on the conjunction in the middle of Felix's burst ("And a bear…"), so the conversation was
  already going before the page began. The reader works out where they are (the shop, the morning, Felix back from the barracks)
  from inference.
  - **Why it is not VOICE:** what pulls here is the moment we land in: a bear, the barracks gate and a boy who stood his ground.
    Felix's personality does not carry it. The second paragraph moves the hook to Mara's nausea, which is the wrong detail inside
    the scene and the "gas in the cinnamon". The pull comes from the situation, not from listening to a voice.
- **50-word grab:** yes. A live bear, a paw through the bars and "I never moved" all land inside 45 words. Inside 80, the
  narrator's vomiting turns comic momentum into a private stake: she has not told him yet.
- **Score: 8/10.** Information gap 2/3 (what show, why the barracks; and then why she is sick, which Ch.20's last line answers
  for the reader but not for Valens). Emotional pull 2/3 (comic energy against a hidden secret). Voice 2/2. Connection 2/2: Ch.20
  closed on Grata's "It's a child you've got", and Ch.21 opens the next morning with the nausea, with no recap.

## Ending (Pull)

> hanging at his knee, looking at the dark plaster where they were, and I wanted him to say Rome, or say no, and he did neither of

    "would", no mountain and no portent, and the style gate's foreshadow count is 0.
