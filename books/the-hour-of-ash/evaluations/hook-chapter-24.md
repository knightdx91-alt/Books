## Opening (Hook)

> I hated the midwife.

- **Type: QUESTION.** One assertion that stops the reader short: why would a woman in labour hate the person delivering her
  child? It is not CONTRAST, because no second element is set against it in the sentence. It is not VOICE either, since the
  pull comes from the claim and not from how it is said. The chapter answers the question honestly. The teeth are introduced at
  once (l.24), at the crisis they are the thing she holds on to and hates "with the whole of me" (l.278), and Statia names that
  look herself at first light (l.456). The promise is kept.
- **Score: 9/10.** Information gap 3/3. Emotional pull 2/3 (blunt and wry rather than wrenching, which suits the low gear).
  Voice 2/2 (Mara's flat, unvarnished register). Connection 2/2 (Ch.23 ended on the child kicking, and "midwife" puts us in the
  birth without any recap).
- **Rewritten:** no. Four words, load-bearing, and the strong ninth-hour paragraph that follows lands Statia, the stool and the
  heat within three sentences.

  breaks the I-punch ledger). VOICE ("I had read two midwives' manuals and neither had teeth in it." est. 6; it is a credential
  aside, and it explains its own joke).

## Ending (Pull)

> It was Valens. The child lay asleep on his chest; my hand was in his right, palm down on his knee. Between his finger and his
> thumb he was turning the crown of the watch, forward, in small even turns, with the dry tick of it going under his fingers. He
> kept on until the crown would go no farther, and laid my hand back on the sheet beside his son.

- **Type: QUESTION PLANT.** It is not an emotional gut punch, because no feeling is pressed on the reader and the birth's
  emotional peak (the jar held out on locked arms) came two shards earlier. What the close plants is a question the reader
  feels without being able to put into words. Valens, unasked, keeps the one modern thing in the house running on the morning his
  son is born, and does it the way she did it in Ch.1 (to the stop). It sits beside the open hour-of-birth thread ("Did you get
  the hour?" / "All of them.") and the Chaldean rumour planted at lamp-lighting, which Ch.25 "Augurinus" collects. Neither is
  pointed at.
- **Score: 9/10.** Forward pull 2/3 (quiet by design, since the birth resolves the chapter's main tension). Emotional charge 3/3.
  Unpredictability 2/2 (a baby-face close is expected, and we get a man's thumb on a watch crown). Promise 2/2 (the night's
  promise is paid and his care of her time is left open).
