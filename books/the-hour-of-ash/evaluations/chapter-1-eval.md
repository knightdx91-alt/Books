### The book's own laws
- **NO FORESHADOWING (target 0): PASS, 0 forward gestures.** I read every reflective sentence for families A–E. "he had not asked to do it over, then or ever" is past-complete because he is dead. "One more winter in that drawer and you'll have two maps" is about the paper, said in dialogue. Halloran's "I'll try you again" is diegetic. Neither Pompeii nor 79 is named; "Campanian" is the reader's irony only. The ending is a pull toward the notebook, not a forward gesture by the narrator.
- **NO TICS: one construction-level risk.** No character carries a verbal tic, and Szabo's absence of contractions is syntax, not a catchphrase. Within-chapter repeats are all justified: "generous with his time" ×2 (the eulogy's own "he said again"), "Which one stops it?" ×2 (the same tape), "I got as far as" ×2, "the way it always had" ×2 in one sentence, "The machine waited" ×2 within the anchor. **The risk is the narrator's polysyndetic looking-inventory** (#8). ## Reader Reports

### The Devourer
Sentence 1 hooks them, but they hit a speed bump at once. ¶2–3 are dense furniture, and the pluperfect loop (eulogy → lid-lifter → "I had been holding the plate ever since") walks *backward* from a sentence that promised a walk-out. They pick up at "I got as far as the meatballs". The Doug exchange and the loading dock move fast. In the shop they love the buzzer, "You are dripping" and the haggle, and they skim the provenance interview (seven Mara questions). Gloria's scene is quick. They read every word at the machine. **Where they would stop:** around ¶199–215 if tired. **What keeps them:** the voice, and a genuinely funny rhythm of rebuffs. They continue to Ch.2, but they need the cave by Ch.3, and the outline delivers it.

### The Critic
They underline "Somewhere, I assumed, there was a pamphlet", "turned the tissue back the way you turn back a sheet from a face", "It weighed less than the cake had", the check with the father's number, and "he had not asked to do it over, then or ever". They admire the restraint at the machine (no named feeling at all) and Szabo's letting "It's a common name" stand. **Disappointments:** the and-chain cadence wears by the third scene. "*Come to Grief*" on the library tape is the author winking. The white projector square is a symbol placed to be noticed. Every walk-on gets a cute grievance (fire bill, mulch, Amman, a style, the barber), which reads as a workshop formula. "because you could see it from the street" explains a joke that had already landed.

### The Hostile (finds time-slip romance precious; distrusts a grieving-daughter opener)
They came to sneer and concede two things: nothing here is precious (there are no stones, storms or omens), and the grief opener is *irritated*, not lachrymose. **Challenges:** (1) Nets are not cut down on March 14, 1998, which was NCAA second-round day. (2) After the greeting plays, "The machine waited for me to speak." A playback check on a two-tape machine does not beep and wait to record. (3) The conservator out-knows the dealer on the holes, the restrike, the verdigris, *tre lune* and the date of Kastri ("Mary Sue in a black jacket"). (4) Quirky-walk-on syndrome. (5) Szabo edges toward the wise-immigrant role she was designed to refuse in "Everything burned. Then somebody comes with a pen and says, not everything, not this." (6) An objects conservator signs a $1,150 check she cannot cover with no hesitation. This one is defended, because it is rendered as grief-impulse ("before I had checked anything at all") and the wrong phone number is the price. Verdict: "Better than I expected; still a writer showing me her details."

### The Casual Reader (airport, 10 pages)
They smile at the pamphlet line and like her at once, because sneaking out with cake you don't want is something they have done. Pages 2–3 feel like "a lot of description of a room". They perk up with the dean ("He'd have hated it." He smiled at that, pleased with himself) and with "Your nose is peeling." By page 10 they are in the antique shop and enjoying the cranky Hungarian lady. **Would they buy after 3 pages?** Probably yes, but not urgently. The vibe is wry and sad and real; what holds them back is that pages 2–3 sit still. **8/10.** That is under the 8.5 gate, and **the shortfall is a VIBES PROBLEM, rated IMPORTANT.** The fix sits in the first 1,500 words, not in the anchor.

### The Devoted Reader (active: series-length immersive saga)
They clock the Phillies cigar box and file it. The coordinates check out against the Corycian Cave on a map. They note "somebody else's country", the *Campanian* permit, "where the strain had gone", and the stapled platter whose visible seam is the book's ethic. They would adopt this book and write about the greeting. The net-cutting slip will be the first Reddit nitpick. This report feeds CVI-Legacy and does not block revision.
