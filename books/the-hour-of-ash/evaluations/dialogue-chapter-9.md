### Valens's exchange (the focus)

They match his Ch.6 self:
- plain, contracted, short level declaratives with no subordination;
- questions only of the practical kind (what for, how long);
- attention on the animal and on who is watching (his look into the court before "That's the god's");
- no simile, no abstraction, no catchphrase.

**Cut: "Warm it first. It'll crack cold."** It was the Ch.6 order-plus-consequence shape ("Eat the crumbs. You'll want them on the stair." / "Hold that. She'll pull."), the reflex the bible SPENT until Ch.12. It also had a muleteer instructing a conservator in how a material behaves, which is a bleed into Mara's axis. Her own narration already shows her warming the pitch.

Checked and absent:
- romance signals in his lines or beats;
- closeness answered by an order;
- a bare "No.";
- anything that shows he can read. "That's the god's" is recognising a temple bronze in a priest's court, not reading.

The subtext is that neither of them mentions the mountain, the watch or the cave, and he gives credit to a woman with nothing. "He'll pay." is kept as the dispatch required.

## Changes Made

### Subtext additions and repairs
- **The priest's bargaining (outline subtext: whether a woman's skill can be worth money).**
  - The FLAT line "I'll pay a smith a smith's price. I'll not pay a woman a smith's price for doing it with wax." became **"A smith's price is for a smith's fire. I have wax from my own bees."** Technique: displacement. He values the work at the price of its material and never names her sex, so the insult travels underneath. The bee-man's own wax then becomes the fill, an irony left unpointed. Mara's reply "It is not a smith's work. That is why it will hold." now answers the displacement directly.
  - "Then you have the worst of it." (a "Then you…" shape) became **"The rest is only money."** It answers "I know about the man" and sets up her "Mend it for a stater." She turns her skill into the god's fee, and he treats a man as the thing that cannot be bought.
  - Still carried by the existing structure: the third-person talk past her, the slave as go-between, his direct "you" only for what she must pay, and the wiped hand at the payment.
- **The goat man.** "Come back with a man." became **"Send whoever's leading her up, with the rest of it. I'll keep her warm till then."** The requirement is now assumed rather than stated. That is the fifth voicing of "a man" in this chapter, and the bible already closes it. "with the rest of it" keeps the money pressure.
- **Kallia.** Her reply to "Onto it. From above." is the basin and clay, an act as planned. The scene had four pot beats in ~200 words: two chore deflections and a turned-back close. Now:
  - "She returned to her pot." is cut.
  - "The sand hissed round the belly of the pot." stays as her one chore beat.
  - The close "At the pot Kallia worked her sand … with her shoulders to me, and the scraping continued." became **"Kallia's sand had gone quiet while I tied it."** She stops work to watch the rag knotted: attention without a word, with no chore, no turn-away and no negation ending.
