## Changes Made

### Subtext and the brief's Kallia requirements
- **Her reply that is not a task.** Before, Mara asked "Have you asked him anything? The god?" and Kallia gave no answer, then turned the torch on another woman: "Hold that lower. You're blinding the girl." That is exactly the overused move: the silence is cashed out in a chore. Now she answers with the question back **"Who would I send in?"**, and the beat ends on the wind and the flame. Technique: deflection by question. The line echoes Mara's own "What man would go in for me?" and her answer "Whoever you can get." It lets the reader sense that she has a question and no man to carry it (widow; "not sons" in her rule) without explaining anything, so Ch.10's reveal is intact. It is present-scene only, so no foreshadowing.
- **One task-answer cut.** "Where they danced the last time." used to be followed by "Hitch that up, or you'll be on your face before the stair." The pin moved higher is now the whole reply. The cut also removes her second "…before the stair" threat in twenty lines.
- **Carrying belongs to Kallia.** "Nobody will carry you down." became **"I'll not lay you out on a stair."** It is still her labor axis (laying out the dead is women's work), and it frees "carry" for her two pen lines ("Who's to carry her?" and "Somebody has to carry him…").
- **Continuity fix in her speech.** "You tell them what you told the clerk. Then you talk about your feet." became **"You tell them what I told the clerk, and not a word over."** In Ch.7, Mara gave the clerk only her name and patronymic; Kallia supplied the Syracuse story ("You've no ears. I answer."). The old line also repeated "talk about your feet" from the first paragraph and carried a "Then" opener.

### Voice adjustments

  - "What have?" became "What has stopped?" (it was elliptical to the point of ungrammatical).

  - "Give it here," became "Let me have it," because "Give it" is Kallia's idiom ("Give it her, and hush").
  - "So you choose the shape for it first, before it chooses one for itself" became "So you choose the shape while it is still soft". The leather personified as choosing its own shape was the book's theme spoken aloud (rule 8). Now it is a repairer's instruction.
