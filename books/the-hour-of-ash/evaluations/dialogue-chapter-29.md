## Changes Made

### Subtext Additions
- **The two exiles (outline layer, kept and given more texture rather than replaced):**
  - On the road, Fenn's courtly "sir" now drops when he asks Valens about the roads. It returns only where his Greek is formal. Valens's "Sit at the back of it going home" stays, and the care goes unanswered.
  - The lesson loses its explainers ("Standing?" / "Sitting, sir…", and the pitcher line). What is left is a run of Valens's questions from his own life, ending in "His blood, mostly. They never get it." Valens then goes back to the pits with no comment from the narrator ("Valens had not finished with the pits.").
  - "I'll get olives in" moves onto Fenn's own beat and goes into his Greek register: "I shall get olives in."
  - Technique: displacement. The friendship moves entirely through stairs, olives and the stealing lesson, and nobody names it.
- **The reserve clause:** Felix's "Like me and Albucius" and Fenn's "Yours runs out, Majesty" are cut, because those two lines said what the reader had just seen. Valens's "And he plays." now carries it alone, and Fenn's "For that money he would play on his knees" misses the point in character (he sees people last).
- **Cerdo at the pour:** "Poured for by a Chaldean. Me." was a comment on the inversion, which his card's evasion forbids. It is replaced by Fenn's "I have had no guest to pour for in years, sir. Forgive me if I spill." / Cerdo: "It's in the cup."
  - Surface: a shaky hand and a full cup. Underneath: the lonely host, and a slave who makes nothing of a kindness.
  - Technique: over-specificity. Cerdo answers the spill and nothing else.
- **Cerdo's attention:** "It's the boys with the walnuts. Grown up." is replaced by his line on Fenn's night lights: "Somebody's up those poles all night, then, and sleeps when the rest of you are up." This is the night worker's life, a fact and its cost. Nobody answers it, and Valens's oil question follows.
- **Valens after the farce:** new line on the walk home: "The baker has bad knees," said Valens, at the theater steps, his first words since the crossing. "He won't drop on them like that for many more winters."
  - Surface: an actor's body, read the way Valens reads bodies. Underneath: the weeping, which nobody mentions.
  - Technique: deflection plus silence. Mara does not answer.
- **The fountain girl:** the opaque "What then?" / "Then there's no festival" becomes "And when it's over?" / "I carry yours". This removes "What then", which sat next to Ch.27's spent "What then, that morning?". Surface: who goes first at the spout. Underneath: the inversion runs out.
