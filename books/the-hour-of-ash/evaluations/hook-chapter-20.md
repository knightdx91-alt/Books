## Opening (Hook)

> *A bronze pot has walked off from this shop, the big one, with the handle I had riveted on new at the Saturnalia. Sixty-five
> sesterces if anybody brings it back, and twenty more for the thief, if he comes with it.*
>
> Somebody had lettered it in red on the pier of a cookshop above the bull's-head fountain. Under it, in charcoal and a smaller hand,
> another somebody had added that the thief was enjoying his soup. I had passed it every day for half a month […] before I stopped to
> read it.

- **Type: VOICE (the town's voice: an aggrieved shopkeeper in paint, answered by a wit in charcoal).** This agrees with the writer's
  typing.
  - The hook is the personality on the wall. "Walked off" makes the pot a deserter, and "the big one" and the handle "riveted on new at
    the Saturnalia" are an owner's private grievances made public. The thief clause is sly: twenty more if the thief comes with it.
    The charcoal reply ("the thief was enjoying his soup") makes the wall talk back. The reader leans in to listen to the town before
    anything has happened.
  - The voice carries information as well as charm, so it is not snark without stakes. It tells us this is a town that keeps and
    mends, which is the chapter's trade, and a man who prizes a repair. That is beat 1's reveal ("people here mend rather than
    replace"), and the narration never states it.
  - **Why it is not CONTRAST** (Ch.19 flag 1): nothing is juxtaposed against anything. Paying the thief's captor is a joke inside one
    grievance, not two incompatible things colliding. Ch.9's document hook (the manumission) and Ch.19's marriage-against-mule were the
    collisions. This one is a complaint.
  - **Why it is not QUESTION:** the lost pot is a mild gap ("where is it?"). It is honestly answered later, in the ditch outside the
    Sarno Gate with a frog in it, but the gap is not what pulls. The pull is the voice. One committed type, no blend.
  - **Why it is not SENSORY:** there is one color (red, then charcoal) and no immersion. The water jar on Mara's hip comes third and
    is texture.
- **Score: 8/10.**
  - Information gap 2/3: a theft, a reward and a heckler; and what is this notice to Mara? (The paint question and the board answer
    it within a page.) The gap is mild by design: it is a Collage opener, and documents read quick.
  - Emotional pull 2/3: comic and warm. It is the first light page after Ch.19's dormant door, and the right register for the
    domestic-year chapter.
  - Voice 2/2: no other book sounds like this. It is a real-style Pompeian notice in plain English. It does not quote CIL or use a
    gloss, and it has no credential aside.
  - Connection 2/2: Ch.19 closed on the cobbler's shop left unshuttered among the knocking boards. Ch.20 opens on another shop's loss,
    painted on its pier, and within two pages the cobbler's pier carries Mara's own board. The white space is crossed by the shop motif
    without recapping the door, the tomb or the evening.

## Ending (Pull)

> When the week was out I sat one evening on the bench before her counter, over a cup of her hot wine I had not drunk, and she lifted
> the lid to ladle some for a carter, and my hand went over my mouth.
>
> "That jar has gone off," I said, behind my hand.
>
> […] Grata put the lid down. She put her arm over the counter and rested two fingertips on the inside of my wrist, beside Plautia's
> gold, and kept them there while she talked.
>
> "[…] and you've not drunk my wine tonight, and you've not told him either, or he'd be standing in my door grinning at nothing the way
> husbands do. It'll come with the heat. You'll curse that stair every day of the last month, and him along with it, and he'll be no use
> to you, none of them are. It's not my fish. It's a child you've got."

- **Type: REVELATION (Grata clocks the pregnancy before Mara has said it).** This agrees with the writer's typing.
  - The new information changes the book's footing. The domestic year becomes a family, in the city whose date the reader knows.
  - The page never touches that knowledge. The reader supplies the weight, which is the book's no-foreshadowing method. Grata's
    forecasts ("It'll come with the heat", "the last month") are a midwife-neighbour's due-date reckoning in her own mouth, not
    narration reaching forward. They agree with Ch.24's dateline (*summer, AD 74*) ✓.
  - **There is backward ripple, which a revelation needs:** the week of going "round the long block" to avoid the jar; the late figs
    ("I wanted nothing else that whole day"); the hot wine untouched; Mara's own diagnosis ("The jar had turned, I decided."). Each
    re-reads on the turn. The fountain break is **not** recruited: it sits a season earlier, and the reveal never mentions it. The
    at-risk rule "never explained, by anyone, ever" holds ✓.
  - **Why it is not QUESTION PLANT** (Ch.19 flag 2): nothing is left unasked or half-seen. Grata states it. The forward thread (Valens
    does not know; a carter heard it at the counter) is a consequence of the revelation, not a germinating question.
  - **Why it is not EMOTIONAL GUT PUNCH:** Mara's reaction is withheld. The force is the information and Grata's comic certainty, not
    a precise detail of grief or joy.
- **The last line works as the reveal, not a button.** The flood discloses its evidence in order: the green mornings, then "the look
  about the eyes my sister had with each of hers", then the wine not drunk, then the husband not told. An alert reader is ahead of it by
  "my sister". The final two sentences still land because they reverse Mara's own line: she said "That jar has gone off"; Grata answers
  "It's not my fish." The plain naming comes last, after the flood. That is Grata's syntax (she tells; she never asks), and it is the
  first time the word *child* is on the page. Questions in her speech: 0 ✓. Teeth: 0 ✓.
- **Anti-pattern checks.**

  - No aphorism. "The way husbands do" / "none of them are" is Grata's character opinion, not a closing truth.
  - No summary, no sleep.
  - No gentle landing: the section compresses from a week of avoidance into one evening, then one speech, then one line.
  - Not symmetrical: the opening's pot is not called back by the fish jar in words.
  - No device as a button: the jar lid is business inside the scene, not the last image.
- **Score: 8/10.**
  - Forward pull 3/3: the reader has to see Valens told. Grata has already pictured it ("standing in my door grinning at nothing"),
    and a carter at her counter heard it, so the street will know before the husband does. The block ends, and the book does not let
    go.
  - Emotional charge 2/3: tender under comedy (two fingertips on the wrist "beside Plautia's gold"). The full weight is the reader's,
    by design.
  - Unpredictability 1/2: morning sickness at a smell is a familiar signal, and a reader may call it before Grata does. The
    surprise is who says it (the neighbour, over a carter's cup) and that Mara has diagnosed the fish.
  - Promise keeping 2/2: it delivers outline beat 8 (NON-NEGOTIABLE: Grata's spoken line ends the chapter), and it opens Ch.21's
    "a family coming".
- **Midnight Test / review checkpoint:** passes. Ch.20 closes review block 4, so the author reads Ch.21 after a pause. The pause lands
  on a fresh fact with an untold husband, which is the strongest carry the block has had since Ch.17's unfinished action.
