## Opening (Hook)

> By the twelfth morning of my marriage Pacuvius's goose ship was coming round the last cape into the bay, and I had slept all eleven
> nights before it on her deck, between my husband and a mule. The mules stood amidships inside a square of hurdles lashed to
> ring-bolts, […] Valens slept along the outside of the hurdles with his shoulders to them, and I slept along Valens. […] Nobody aboard
> that ship had a door to shut on anybody.

- **Type: CONTRAST (the honeymoon against the mule).** This agrees with the writer's typing.
  - The collision sits in the first sentence: "my marriage" against "between my husband and a mule". The paragraph's last line turns it
    into a conflict rather than a quirk: eleven nights married, and no door. The contrast implies desire against circumstance, which
    is what the type needs in a romance.
  - **Why it is not SENSORY.** Only one sentence (the mules' ears laid back at the sea) is image, and it is arrangement-comedy, not
    immersion: there is no smell, taste or body sense and no wrong note inside a sensory field. No weather acts on Mara's body
    (at-risk after Ch.18) ✓. The bay and the mountain, which would have typed as SENSORY, come later, at paragraph 6.
  - **Why it is not a blend with VOICE.** The dryness ("I slept along Valens") delivers the contrast; it does not carry the hook on its
    own.
  - It is a scene-set opener: "By the" is on the detector's list, and the writer avoided a third "By the time the" book-wide. It is a
    time-and-place fact in the moment, not a summary of the crossing.
  - The ship is "the goose ship", as the Ch.18 ripple asked: not a grain ship, and the goose is not described again. "Goose" also
    appears in Ch.18's next-to-last paragraph, but only as the ship's name. No phrasing or move from Ch.18's last two paragraphs is
    reused ✓.
  - Through-line, not a symmetry: the opening's "no door to shut" runs into Grata's rooms ("I woke alone in a room with a door"; "It
    sticks at the sill") and ends at the cobbler's boards left down. The closing paragraph does not repeat the opening's words.
- **Score: 8/10.**
  - Information gap 2/3: the wedding is known, so the gap is the first consequence of an irrevocable choice and the bay ahead. It is
    mild by design.
  - Emotional pull 2/3: comic-tender. The ankle held in the dark two paragraphs later pays it off.
  - Voice 2/2: Mara's level, exact syntax ("I slept along Valens"), with no credential aside.
  - Connection 2/2: "They drew the plank in after us" becomes eleven nights on that deck, with no recap and the wedding not
    re-narrated ✓.
- **Rewritten:** no.
- **Alternatives considered (not applied):**
  - VOICE: an I-statement about not having her husband to herself for eleven nights (est. 7). It loses the `scene-set` signature, sits
    close to performed candour, and states the conflict the mule implies.
  - QUESTION: open on the girl's sprung brooch ("It won't hold.") (est. 6–7). It breaks `scene-set`, makes a dialogue opener, and
    hands the hook to a walk-on.

- **Foreshadowing check:** nothing in the window points at the mountain, the year or the ending. "Door" is a literal ship fact ✓.

## Ending (Pull)

> I took the bench at the front of her counter with the bowl she gave me, and emptied it, and did not know what had been in it, and it
> was good. Everywhere in the street the shops were putting their shutters up for the night, each board let into the groove of its sill
> with a knock of wood on stone, and the knocking came toward us from the crossing one shopfront after another, passed the cobbler's,
> where our boards still leaned against the inside wall because nobody had thought to put them up, passed Grata's lamps, and carried on
> toward the gate, […] voices and feet and a girl on a balcony overhead singing the same two lines of a song to a baby, and then the
> same two lines, louder than the baby.

- **Type: QUESTION PLANT (the shop left open).** The writer called it "long fade". By function it is the type's own model ("She locked
  the door and put the key in the drawer… But she left the light on."):
  - Every shutter on the street goes up in sequence, and theirs alone does not. That is the one unclosed thing, set inside the
    knocking at the heart of the sentence, not as a button.
  - The question is felt, never asked: the door home is found, faintly cool and dormant; Mara has told no one; and the household has
    not yet closed up its own front for the night. Are they shutting in here, or not? The text never frames it as a choice, so it is
    not a DECISION POINT ✓.
  - The door's cold does carry (the writer's claim holds), but by weight, not by mention. The cassette hub that "turned" two paragraphs
    up and the palm's coolness "gone by the gate" sit just behind the warm bowl and the street. Bringing the cold back here would be a
    device as a button, and would contradict the SPENT wording, so it stays out.
- **Long fade is a designed fade, not a gentle landing (#10).** The energy holds because the sentence travels: the knocking moves along
  the street, passes us, passes the lamps and goes on. The last clause tightens to a comic sting ("louder than the baby"), a concrete
  sound, not an abstraction. It is the chapter's one designed held sentence (and-chains in the tic gate: clean).
- **Anti-pattern checks.**
  - No emotion named ("it was good" is about the food).
  - No aphorism and no profound closing line.
  - No summary of the day.
  - No sleep as an ending: the sleep is two paragraphs back and comes before the cassette.
  - Not symmetrical: the opening's door is not called back in words.
  - **The baby** is street texture. It does not reach toward Ch.20's pregnancy or the approved ending, and the narration claims nothing
    for it ✓.
  - The double "put … up" in the sentence is the contrast itself (theirs against everyone's). Kept.
  - Threads left live: the cool door (Mara alone knows it), the tape, the letter board at Puteoli, Valens's certainty about the clerk,
    Grata's whitewashed patch, the unshuttered shop.
- **Score: 8/10.**
  - Forward pull 2/3: quiet. The pull is the found, dormant door set against a household that has not yet closed its doors.
  - Emotional charge 2/3: warmth (the bowl, the street going home to eat) laid over the cold the reader still holds.
  - Unpredictability 2/2: after the tomb the reader expects the door to be the last image. It ends instead in other people's suppers and a
    girl out-singing a baby.
  - Promise keeping 2/2: it delivers the outline's beat 7 (a long fade into the city's evening noise). The unshuttered shop opens
    straight onto Ch.20, *The Mender's Shop* ("they need a living").
- **Midnight Test:** passes at the quiet register (genre threshold 7; the quiet-chapter rule applies). The reader turns over to see what
  becomes of the open shop and the door Mara has told no one about.
- **Rewritten:** no.
- **Alternatives considered (not applied):**
  - EMOTIONAL GUT PUNCH: end on the cassette hub, "and it turned" (est. 7). That is a device as the scene's button (a retired reflex).
    It breaks `long-fade` and spends the touch as a sting.
  - OMINOUS PROMISE: the coolness returning to her palm in the dark (est. 6). That is foreshadowing-adjacent, contradicts the spent "gone
    by the gate", and is barred by the brief.
  - REVELATION: none is available that the chapter has earned, and inventing one would break the outline.
