## Opening (Hook)
- **Type:** SENSORY IMMERSION. Smell (thyme pressed out by the wind), sound (bells "without any tune
  at all"), sight (the road "like a dropped bootlace"), and the one off-note the type requires: a truck
  so far below that it "made no sound." The modern world is present and already muted. That is
  physical fact at altitude, not portent; nothing in the sentence waits, looms or knows. Not CONTRAST
  (Ch.1) and not VOICE (Ch.2), though the voice is fully present.
- **Score: 7/10** (Information gap 2/3, Emotional pull 1/3, Voice 2/2, Connection 2/2)
  - Information gap 2: Ch.2 ended on Fenn "going in tomorrow first thing" and Mara's flight. The
    reader lands on a mountain with an unnamed "me" climbing alone and knows at once which mountain
    it is. The question is what she will do up there, not where she is.
  - Emotional pull 1: the sentence is vast, clean and a little lonely. It is deliberately calm (the
    ledger forbids portent, and the outline's reading speed is "slow and sensory all the way up"),
    so the feeling it makes is altitude and smallness, not unease. This is the honest ceiling for
    a no-portent spiral, and the right register for the climb.
  - Voice 2: plain diction, one characterizing simile ("a dropped bootlace") of the kind §4 allows,
    no analytical extension, and the eye of a woman who photographs sites from above before a spade
    goes in (paid off two paragraphs later with her father's "only honest picture of a season").
  - Connection 2: Ch.2 ends in Fenn's 1968 voice; Ch.3 begins in Mara's 1998 body on the same
    slope. There is no recap and the white space does the travelling.
- **Ledger check:** first sentence "The thyme grew in low gray cushions up the whole slope, … so far
  below me that a truck crawling along it made no sound." = **79 words**, one sentence, sensory,
  plateau-facing, all four required elements (thyme, bells, wind, road). `long-spiral` ✓. The opening
  paragraph is that single sentence; Ch.3 is not on the one-sentence-opener list, but the sig is
  computed on the sentence and the outline's opening strategy asks for exactly this. No em-dash.
- **Banned-opener scan:** no weather report (the wind is a carrier of smell, not a forecast), no
  waking, no recap, no competence line, no metacognition, no define-by-negation, no foreshadowing
  (families A–E clear).
- **Rewritten:** no. It meets the floor, fits the chapter's register exactly, and the outline
  requires the type and the shape. Rule 7: leave functional work alone.
- **Alternatives considered:**
  - IN MEDIAS RES: open on the duffel strap working at the bone of her shoulder (6). Breaks the
    ledger (`long-spiral` is the plateau, not the body), and that material is already paragraph 2.
  - QUESTION: open on Fenn having slept up here (5). Any version strong enough to stall the reader
    reaches forward toward the cave, which is portent under §0 and the writer warnings.
- **Bootlace echo:** "dropped bootlace" (opener) and the frayed bootlace she reties at a red square
  (para 10) are two different objects in two different sentences. Not a phrase repeat. Leave.

## Ending (Pull)
- **Type:** CLIFFHANGER, in its quietest form: action unfinished. She crosses the lip of the cave
  into a world she has read only from light, air and stone, and the chapter stops on the step. The
  revelation (no blaze, no ghost of paint, lichen in hand-wide rosettes) is staged in the paragraph
  before as content; the last line is the act, not the reveal. Not QUESTION PLANT (Ch.1) and not
  EMOTIONAL GUT PUNCH (Ch.2).
- **Score: 9/10** (Forward pull 3/3, Emotional charge 2/3, Unpredictability 2/2, Promise 2/2)
  - Forward pull 3: the door is real and she is through it. No reader stops on a woman stepping out
    into an unknown season with dung and woodsmoke on the wind and bells just below the lip.
  - Emotional charge 2: the charge is vertigo, held flat. Her watch says ten to two and the sun is
    going down; the plateau that was "almost violent green" in May has gone "paper-bag brown that I
    put down to the light". That clause is the best emotional line in the sequence, because she is
    still explaining it away. The final line refuses to feel anything, which is right for this
    narrator and keeps it off the emotional-report budget.
  - Unpredictability 2: the reader expects "wrong time". The line gives a month, not an era. It names
    the scale she can read (season, from light and air) and leaves the scale she cannot (the lichen
    says years beyond counting) unspoken. That understatement is the surprise.
  - Promise 2: it keeps the chapter's promise (the crossing, rendered as cold, silence and
    embarrassment, then light and a missing paint mark, exactly the outline's beat subversion) and
    opens a new one: what is out there.
- **Ledger check:** final sentence "I stepped out into October." = **5 words**, `punch` ✓, alone in its
  paragraph ✓, last line of the file with nothing after it ✓. It is an action (a step over a
  threshold) whose consequence is the next chapter.
- **No-foreshadowing check on "October":** passes. It is a reading of present evidence: a sun low and
  swollen at what her watch calls ten to two, a brown plain that was green that morning, a chill,
  woodsmoke, fresh dung from flocks still on the high pasture. She is a field archaeologist's
  daughter who has sat through every season's slides; she can date a season from its light. It does
  not name a year, a place in history or anything she has not seen. Canon fit: she arrives in autumn
  AD 70 (outline Ch.4) and Ch.6 is November 70, so October is consistent.
- **Scene-button / aphorism check:** the line is not a device or a click-shut object; it is the
  ledger's required step. "Lichen did not grow like that in an afternoon" (the sentence before) is a
  present-scene deduction from a specific stone, not a generalization about life or time, and it sits
  inside the paragraph, not as the closer. No closing aphorism, no forward gesture, no em-dash in the
  last five sentences.
- **Midnight Test:** turns the page. Ch.4 opens "The goats saw me first." and the ending has already
  put bells and dung under the lip.
- **Rewritten:** no.
- **Alternatives considered:**
  - REVELATION: end on "Lichen did not grow like that in an afternoon." (6). 10 words computes as
    `quiet` (ledger break), and closing on a general truth tilts toward aphorism. Weaker.
  - DECISION POINT: "I went down toward the bells." (6). A clean `punch` and a real choice, but it
    loses the season fact the outline requires and is the obvious choice (she has no other), so it
    carries no moral pull.
  - "I stepped out into autumn." (6). Same sig, but vaguer; "October" is the concrete, readable fact
    and a sharper rhyme against "green in May".
