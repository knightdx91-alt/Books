## Operations NOT Applied (and why)

- **2. Irrelevant Thought:** the Writer already has three Mara chaos moments (two-kinds-of-letter; "Emesa was Homs." and
  the Van Pelt postcard; the misread yawn). Adding a fourth would make her scattered in a chapter that belongs to Kallia.
- **3. Emotional Control Break:** Kallia's double yawn is the chapter's involuntary break, and the brief says to add no
  other. Mara's channels are closed by the bible: hands are rested and her voice is retired until Ch.20. The restraint is
  the design, and "it made a fatter packet than before" already lets an object fail where she does not.
- **4. Precision Deflation:** every number serves the story or the money axis: two/three obols, "Two and a half" years,
  "In four months", "seven letters" (ΑΡΙΣΤΩΝ: Mara's eye measuring the name Kallia cannot read), the twentieth of
  Hyperberetaios (the letter's formula). Deflating them would cost character.
- **5. Ugly Sentence:** it already exists ("The one on top was the cracked one but it fit.") and is untouched.
- **6. Negation Pattern Break:** there are no define-by-negation openers. The chapter's negations are all plain statements of
  absence ("For the house there was no line"), and those are the subject.

## Strongest Disruption

**The horse-seller line.** It is three short lines of speech and the only added material, and it does what the chapter's
subtext needs without anyone saying it. During the practical talk about sending a reply, Kallia goes back to the one fact
in the letter that takes her house out of the next generation: the child will carry the horse-seller's name, not her
husband's. She asks for it, hears it, and says nothing about it. It is untidy the way real conversation is, and it adds
grief without adding incident.

Close second: **the ¶66 cut.** The non-negotiable, that her perfect reading delivers the blow, now comes through the
reading itself ("I read the next part faster", "My voice went on through the oxen without me") and one line, instead of
a paragraph explaining it.

## Risk Assessment

   her hand.") to keep some margin.
3. **The horse-seller line might read as random** to a reader who has forgotten "Here it's for the father's father." It sits
   about 80 lines later. That is a re-read reward, and Kallia's money/labor axis carries it on a first read. If the
   evaluator scores it as unmotivated, the fallback is to cut the three lines. It must not be explained.
4. **The non-negotiable now rests on fewer words.** If the evaluator wants the reading's perfection made more explicit, put
   back one clause, not the paragraph (e.g. "the Greek on the papyrus had been chosen already, in Syria"). Do not restore
   the eye-a-line-ahead mechanism.
