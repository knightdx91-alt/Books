## INFO

- **INFO-01 Timeline: consistent.** Sickness in the olive month (ch-20 l.589, about Oct–Nov AD 73), then "With the heat. Summer" (ch-21 l.72), then "Five. Nearly six." at Rome in mid-March (ch-22 l.28), then about 7 months in late April (Ch.23, derived), then a birth in summer 74. That puts term about late June to July. Ch.24 agrees: "every night that summer", the Vinalia (Rustica, 19 Aug) still ahead, and the dun's feet "done at the Kalends". Ch.25's "about eight months" in early spring 75 fits a July birth. ARCH-8 holds.
- **INFO-02 Roman night hours: accurate names and order.** In Censorinus/Macrobius order: *luminibus accensis*/prima fax (Lamp-lighting), *concubium* (Bedtime), *media nox* (Midnight), *de media nocte* (Past midnight), *gallicinium* (Cockcrow), *conticinium* (After the cocks, the hush when the cocks stop), *diluculum* (First light), *mane* (Morning). *Intempesta nox* is skipped, which is fine. "The ninth hour" is a day-hour stamp (mid-afternoon), correct for the arrival; the tenth and eleventh hours follow inside it in order.
- **INFO-03 Birth history: accurate to the Outlander standard.** The stool with its crescent-cut seat, arms and back, the midwife low in front and an assistant behind (Soranus). Juno Lucina with cakes and a lamp (the light-goddess fits). Hair unpinned and every knot undone (Pliny NH 28.59; Ovid). The eagle-stone with its loose inner stone and the eagle's nest (Pliny NH 36.149–151); tying it to the thigh in labor is Dioscorides's practice and fits. Cretan dittany as the real kind. Oil. The cord tied with wool (Soranus). Fine salt, then oil, then bands with the arms straight down (Soranus II). Colostrum judged "cheese" and refused in favour of honey and a wet-nurse (Soranus; he says about 20 days, Statia says 3, which is her own practice). The child put on the ground and lifted by the father. The ninth-day naming for a boy (*dies lustricus*; girls the eighth). Optional precision points: (a) "cut between the ties" (l.334) implies two ligatures, the modern practice; Soranus ties the infant's side and cuts beyond it ("and cut beyond the tie"). (b) Soranus specifies soft woollen bands; "linen" (l.390) is defensible. (c) Modern scholarship (Shaw) doubts that the floor-lifting *tollere* rite was literal; it is fine as fiction.
- **INFO-04 Information flow, the Ch.25 plant: complete.** The husband has the Chaldean from his brother, the seaman of ch-19 l.108 ("never been off one"), who had his stars cast before sailing. The Puteoli fish quay matches Ch.25's "upper room by the harbor". "mutters gibberish to himself" is the outline's plant. Mara is at the jamb and hears it before the pain (l.124–140), and narration does not react (no foreshadowing). Nothing in Ch.25 is pre-spent: no name "Augurinus", no English, Zippo, basil, Phillies or pie. "Did you get the hour?" / "The cocks." gives the family a reason to want a caster without pointing at him. "not Greek, not Syrian, nothing you ever heard" is a negation run in speech; the disruptor kept it as the plant, so leave it.
- **INFO-05 Felix's knowledge: plausible but not shown.** "Felix said." (l.70): Felix knew of the labor before Valens came off the string, and the page does not show how (the street "since noon", l.48, covers it).
- **INFO-06 The fuller's other half's twins** are new on the page and were not seeded. This is compatible if they were born while Mara was in Rome (Mar–May 74), which also explains why Mara never heard "the street out of bed" (l.420). The Ch.19 lore (they beat each other on market and feast days) is honoured at l.158.
- **INFO-07 Grata:** three children ("You took three out of me", l.90) fits "My first… My second…" (l.22) and her son at the baths (Ch.19). Her not asking questions holds. Note: "the watch round" (l.420, the night watch) shares a noun with the closing Seiko; optional "the aedile's men round".
- **INFO-08 Hoof-trimming realism:** the near fore held between the thighs and the off fore next is right. Four rounds producing "a drift" of horn on a mule with "nothing left to take off" is a little at odds with itself, and "walk tender a day or two" covers the overtrim. This narrows ENTITY CH21-4 (the dun's hoof that Valens's letter mentioned).
- **INFO-09 Valens's hands "big and scored with old cuts"** (l.352) are old scars, not work residue. Allowed.
- **INFO-10 The flies on the ceiling** come 4 times (l.18, 164, 192, 326); the third is optional ("hung under the ceiling with the flies" → "hung under the ceiling"). "There was no air." (l.18) / "There was no air now at all." (l.192) is an in-chapter echo, read as progression.
- **INFO-11 Pliny's** *Natural History* was published c. AD 77. "Pliny had a page on these" is the 1998 narrator's reading, so it is acceptable. If WARN-02's cut is taken, the line still reads as her knowledge.
- **INFO-12 "crown"** means the head (l.464, "on the crown") one shard before the watch crown. If CRIT-005's fix is taken, the watch's "crown" appears once; optional: cut ", on the crown," at l.464. Oscan in Pompeii in AD 74 is unusual but plausible for an old Samnite midwife under her breath. "Mas" and "Noli" are unglossed and correct.
