## Ending (Pull)
- **Type:** EMOTIONAL GUT PUNCH (dramatic irony). Not QUESTION PLANT ✓ (Ch.27 was QUESTION PLANT).
- **Score:** 9/10 (Forward pull 2/3, Emotional charge 3/3, Unpredictability 2/2, Promise 2/2)
- **Rewritten:** no.
- **Closing lines:** *"When they were all up shouting, I looked for you," he said. "You were sitting down."* shape_check reads it as `dialogue` ✓.
- **Subtext check:** the reader has watched for a week as Valens chose to sit. He chose the top ring with the women so that no herald would cry Lupus's name, and so that no crowd would want "Lupus's boy to kill a man". On the night before, he said "I'll watch." In S5 his stillness is the one refusal in a ring calling for the whips. Felix saw none of the reasons. He only saw that his father did not stand. Each word of the line is factually true and says what the son thinks he saw. The reader knows the opposite. That gap is the gut punch.
- **Rule checks:** the line gets no reply, no gesture after it and no narrator gloss. It does not foreshadow, since it says nothing about anything still to come. It is not an aphorism. It is concrete and it is spoken. It echoes nothing earlier: the echo scan and the spoken-line scan both come back with none. It does not repeat Felix's earlier "I'd spend the bout hunting the benches for you". It goes against it, because he hunted the benches anyway and the line is what he found. This irony is earned, not a repeated phrase.
- **Ch.29 pre-emption:** none. Nothing heads toward home, the festival or a reconciliation. Saturnalia starts clean.
- **Midnight test:** passes. The reader cannot leave a misreading this unfair standing, and the page-turn comes from needing to see whether Valens will ever say why.
- **Alternatives considered (not used):** end on "That wants washing." (DECISION/withholding, 6). It leaves Felix's grievance unsaid and goes flat. End on the unfinished palm (OMINOUS, 4). It would be a device used as a scene-button and it leans toward foreshadowing, so it breaks two house rules.
