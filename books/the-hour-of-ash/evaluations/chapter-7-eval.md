**2. Does the registration land as RELIEF?** **Yes.** Three things make it land, and two things blunt it.
- *It lands because:*
  - The wound is set up two scenes earlier and in the same words: "She's nothing to anybody. Ask round this spring." (¶242).
  - Mara is absent from her own humiliation. The splinter "took up my entire mouth" while "Sosandros and Kallia settled what I was" (¶348), so the reader takes the indignity ("in her keeping") while she is chewing a lentisk fiber. That is inhabited chaos doing structural work.
  - The relief is physical and ambiguous: she sits on a cold step and laughs "without a sound, with my shoulders" into another woman's cloak (¶356). The reader can't tell laughter from crying, and doesn't need to.
  - The surprise is earned before it is felt: she gave the name to rescue Kallia, who stood "with a line of householders at her back and not a thing to give him" (¶322).
- *Blunted by:*
  - **¶348's "the men in the line behind us were laughing at something Kallia had said"**, eight lines before the step: the relief laugh is the fifth laugh in the chapter, and it should be the first one Mara completes.
  - **¶356's last clause names the relief's cause** ("it was the only thing in that whole country that had ever said I was in it"). **Ruling: keep it.** This is an essayistic chapter, the narrator's reasoning is its genre, and the irony (a conservator who keeps things "from being wiped smooth" written into "the least lasting surface in Delphi") is the Critic's underline. Without the final clause, the laugh would read as bitter, not relieved. Do not add to it.

**3. Is the closing question real?** **Yes.** "How far was it, at night, on foot, from wherever the women danced to the cave?" (¶398).

- It has a factual answer she lacks, and a plan depends on it. It generalizes nothing, and it does not echo the opening.
- It complicates the chapter instead of buttoning it: the woman relieved to be somebody's is still measuring the road out.
