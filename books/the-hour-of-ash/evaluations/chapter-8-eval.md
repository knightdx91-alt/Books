### The seven points the orchestrator asked me to judge

**1. Is the rite physical and specific, not mystical or lyrical? YES, without reservation.**
- Every element of the rite is a body or a thing: drums that are "round frames of hide struck with the palm" (¶62); a pipe on "two notes" that "never seemed to stop for breath" (¶62); fennel wands leaned on "on the steep parts, which I suspected was not the intention" (¶66); a basket carried up a cliff "without putting a hand to it" (¶68).
- The waking is a farmyard chore, and the text lets the exchange carry the irony rather than the narrator: "It is bread." / "It's bread as well." (¶126–128).
- The dance is "loud and clumsy, hot on the side toward the fire and freezing on the other, and the smoke kept changing its mind" (¶134). Mara joins "mostly for the sake of my feet" (¶144).
- The one place it could have soared, the cry, is grounded in her mouth: "a word I had met in Euripides and never had cause to say out loud" (¶150).
- The only risks are factual (the fennel, the summer barley toss), not tonal.

**2. Does the wet-sandals farce turn into belonging? YES, in two steps, and the second step is the stronger.**
- *Step one, usefulness.* The farce has a real engine: "a whole ring of the same sandal" (¶252) → Timo's hem → "fifty-nine sandals and a stone" → Kallia fishing one out of the fire → Nikaso's red sandal shrunk "on her toes like a hat" (¶324). Mara's warning ("If leather dries too fast it shrinks", ¶274) was ignored, and now her hands are wanted: "Give it her, and hush." (¶334). The payoff is Nikaso's grudging "Now I'll have one eye." (¶350). This is a competence beat, which bestseller-dna warns earns admiration rather than care; it opens the door, it does not take her through.
- *Step two, vulnerability.* "There is no he." (¶372), the pen gone quiet, and the joke she cannot sing (¶418). This is where she belongs: "by the last of them I had the refrain" (¶428). The farce prepared it by making her useful and by putting her among bare feet.
- Verdict: the outline's surprise lands. The farce is where she first belongs in the sense that it is where the women first need her; the song is where they keep her.

**3. Is the stranger's cloak the social revelation? YES. It is the best beat in the chapter.**
- It is planted twice in Ch.7 without a wink (Ch.7 ¶150, ¶376) and once here (¶280: "I had studied that patch at the spring every morning for weeks… and I had given her nothing but the top of my head").

- Two things blunt it, both removable (P3, P4): the cloak's smell borrows the Ch.7 anchor's mechanism and the first-union sample's frame, and ¶484 points at the wrist inversion that ¶446 has already delivered.

**4. Are the four named women distinct by worldview, not tics? YES.**
