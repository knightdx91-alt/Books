## Operations NOT Applied (and why)
- **Simile surgery proper:** there are no analytical similes. The two comparisons ("like a charcoal briquette somebody had tried to cut into wedges"; "no more idea what it was than the cat on Theano's hearth") characterize, and neither explains itself. The fig density is 0.7/1k against a ≤3.0 aim. The explanation-clause cuts above are this operation's equivalent for this chapter.
- **Irrelevant thought:** skipped by rule. The chapter already has a memory insert (the 1988 casts and the pretzel to Trenton), Ch.13 had one, and the outline marks Ch.14 "none forced". Adding one would put the device on a cadence.
- **Precision deflation:** every number here is a story number for the Devoted Reader: 1988, twenty-three, the 1850s, seventeen and nine, ten or eleven years, eight years "and a little over" (already vague), seven years. None is a precision flex.
- **Ugly sentence:** one exists: "The jar was heavy and I got it up there and she said nothing of it at all."
- **Dialogue mess:** dialogue polish has just worked all five exchanges at 5%. They already talk past one another: the father's grievance against the ram, Kallia's correction, the borrowed well story, and "It isn't their knees." Roughing the dawn orders would blunt the run-up to the final paragraph.
- **Shard-end kickers kept on purpose:**
  - "To everybody under that roof it was a town in Italy where they made fish sauce." This is the anchor.
  - "I left him out of the plan. He kept being in it."
  - "and that was everywhere." This is the self-justification chaos.
  - "It was a good plan. … and it held." The irony is the reader's, and nothing in it reaches forward.
  - "Either the cave was shut, or it was open." This is the all-or-nothing distortion.
  - The others were cut or recast. One kicker per shard is still about the most a fragmented chapter can carry before it reads as a device.

## Strongest Disruption
The S8 cut. "There was a thing the plan did that I circled more than once before I would face it." used to stand in front of the stopper and tell the reader a hard thing was coming. Now the paragraph opens on the hard thing itself: "Whoever took me to that town, the plan left him there. Felix had asked that afternoon whether he could come, if it was Italy. It was Italy." With the throat-clearing gone, the moral wrinkle hits before Mara has braced for it, and so it hits before the reader has too.

A close second is the plaster hand. It is the chapter's one quiet failure of control before the loud one, and the laugh lands harder for it.
