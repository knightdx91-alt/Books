### Why each cut

**1. The Ch.11 recall (the cut I would defend hardest).**
- Ch.11 already put the evidence on the page: "Since the mountain he had kept it going, every day, and he had not put a finger to the hands."
- The Ch.18 sentence placed the old fast reading next to today's true one. That is the inference served up for the reader: *it was wrong then, it is right now, so he set it.* The brief says the page must not explain the watch, and the outline says "let her notice".
- What she notices is still all there: it runs (the seconds hand), it is wound full (the crown stands hard), and it reads twenty to one against a near-noon shadow.
- A reader with Ch.11 in mind makes the connection alone. A reader without it still has a watch that has been in a pouch for seventeen months and is running, full, and right by the sun.
- It also unloads the watch. The ending uses the watch, and the recall was the one sentence in the chapter that weighted it.
- The sentence was not inside the hand-over or the wrist business. It was a separate act of reasoning after the hand-over.

**2. The narrator pointers.**
- "on the very ship that was not going to wait for us" nudged the reader to recall Ch.17's "Will they wait?" / "No.". The goose already identifies the ship (Ch.17 named "the one with the goose carved on her stern"). Felix is on its plank and the master is at its rail, so the sail is plainly theirs within a paragraph.
- "no sentence in any language I owned would have come out whole" explained a body the paragraph had just shown, and it ended the paragraph on a negation.
- "It was." confirmed a shout the next sentence shows is true.
- "with no fuss at all" spelled out the black's comic meekness.

**3. The gesture clusters.**
- The run produced five breath and wind beats in the first ~1,000 words: Valens's wind, Valens's breath, Mara's breath for Latin, Mara's rest of her breath, and the "run still in it". Valens keeps one (his hand on the hull, his wind in long hauls). Mara keeps two (breath for Latin, and the "Yes" with no voice under it).
- The wedding exchange is now barer. It reads "Yes." / the broken voice / "Will you?" / "Yes," with no recovering sentence in between.
- The bible's Ch.17 block allows the crown to be handed over "with no speech and no re-description". Felix's own "a crown that's got no leaves on it" still tells the reader it is bare.

**4. The mule chaos.**
- The original ran as a domino chain: kick → rowers stand and rock their boat → cheer → the black bolts *at* the cheer → sprats → thrown fish → **hits a dog**. Each link caused the next and the last link was a punchline. That is the set-piece feel the brief named.
- The pluperfect ("had dragged her knot loose and was off") makes the black's escape something nobody saw start. The rocking boat and the dog are cut.
- The fish-seller's grievance, the sprat scale on the black's nose and every spoken line stay.
- The crew counts (three, four, six, four) in the first four paragraphs were starting to read as a tally. Two are deflated. "Three sailors" stays as the image of the tug-of-war, and "our four mules" stays as canon.
