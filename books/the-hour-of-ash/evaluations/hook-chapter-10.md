## Opening (Hook)
- **Line:** *The letter had been folded down very small.* (8 words; shape_check reads it as
  `short-punch` ✓)
- **Type:** SENSORY IMMERSION, led by an object. It is not CONTRAST, because nothing collides in it. The
  paragraph stays in the hand: a strip of papyrus folded over and over on itself, a thread wound round
  twice, "a thumb of gray clay", and "a packet I could have closed my fist on". It holds the one wrong
  detail the type needs: "One end had stood in wet somewhere and dried with a brown tidemark across
  it." The Elean then adds "it might be bad, a letter that sat so long", so the reader carries the
  tidemark as dread without the narrator pointing at it.
- **Score: 7/10** (Information gap 2/3, Emotional pull 2/3, Voice 2/2, Connection 1/2)
  - Information gap 2: the pluperfect "had been folded" implies a folder, an unseen hand somewhere
    else. That raises three questions: who folded it, from where, and why so small. The chapter answers
    all three by the reading. It is a gentle gap, not a stall, and that is right for a slow, held chapter.
  - Emotional pull 2: the smallness is intimate, and the tidemark carries a time cost. The feeling
    comes in through "Kallia had it out of his fingers and held it shut in her hand", not through the
    hook itself.
  - Voice 2: Mara's eye is plain and exact, with no simile and no credential aside. It matches the
    chapter's 0 em-dashes and 0.4/1k similes.
  - Connection 1: this is deliberately not a bridge. Ch.9 left Valens at the trough under a price.
    Ch.10 is Kallia's chapter and does not pick up the square, per the outline ("a short, quiet punch
    between the money chapter and the Valens chapters"). The arrival with the reopened sailing ("at the
    back end of the sailing, and then the sailing stopped") shares Ch.9's opened roads without echoing
    "first carts since the snow". The echo scan is 0.
- **Meets threshold** (7 for this genre). For a quiet chapter, a hook at 7 that keeps the chapter's
  register beats a louder one that breaks it.
- **Rewritten:** no.
- **Alternatives considered (not applied):**
  - QUESTION: "The letter had sat all winter on a nail." (8 words). This takes the Elean's best
    information away from him and duplicates "on a nail behind his jars" two sentences later. Est. 7, and
    worse for the scene.
  - VOICE: no honest version stays within ≤8 words without Mara commenting on the letter before
    she has read it, and that is a reach toward significance. Est. 6.
  - TICKING CLOCK: not available honestly. A letter that sat all winter is the opposite of urgency. Not

## Ending (Plain Stop — no pull, by ledger)

**Original last three sentences** (in ¶ "Out in the lane somebody was driving a goat…"):
> The sun had moved off the step and onto the foot of the stair. It was a good while yet till the
> lamps. Half the basket was still to clean, and I took it onto my knees again and went on picking out
> the grit.

**Is "picking out the grit" an image-with-meaning? My honest verdict: yes, mildly, through how it is
placed rather than through the word itself.** Three things push it over the HARD line:
1. **It is a verbatim callback.** ¶ "Halfway through the afternoon…" set up "I sat on the step with a
   basket of chickpeas on my knees, picking out the grit." Ending on the same five words bookends the
   chapter: the interruption is closed and the frame clicks shut. That is the Symmetrical Ending reflex
   (anti-pattern #5), and in effect it is a scene-button.
2. **Placement turns the noun into a figure.** Six paragraphs earlier Mara has just thought "I could
   have left out the roof. I could have left out the father with no son… read the wife as so much talk
   about rain." That is a passage about sorting what to keep from what to discard. Coming after it,
   "picking out the grit" invites the reader to read sifting the hard bits as an emblem of her own
   editing of the blow, and "grit" also carries endurance. Neither reading is pointed out, but both are
   there.
3. **"went on" is the cadence of a closing line.** "…and went on picking out the grit" lands as
   *life goes on*, a dying fall that resolves the chapter instead of stopping it. Together with the
   callback, that comes very close to the aphorism the ledger bans, even though nothing is generalized.

There was also a smaller problem in the sentence before. "The sun had moved … onto the foot of the stair"
puts the light on the exact spot where Kallia has just left the obol "where I would have to pass it". That
is a lit object at the close: the unsent reply, quietly spotlighted. It is unpointed, but it is still an
image doing meaning-work.

**New last three sentences:**
> The step was in shade. It was a good while yet till the lamps. Half the basket was still to clean,
> and I pulled it back onto my knees.

- **Plain-stop check: PASS.**
  - Mid-afternoon: the step has gone into shade and the lamps are a good while off, so the time is
    marked by plain facts.
  - Plain moment: an unfinished chore resumed, with no glossing verb.
  - No image-with-meaning: no verbatim return, no "grit", no "went on", and no light falling on the obol.
  - No aphorism and no generalization.
  - No hook: nothing is withheld or about to happen. The leftover half-basket is housework, not a
    thread.
  - No foreshadowing (gate 0).
  - The goat in the lane is left as it was. It is the "afternoon going on" the outline asks for, and it
    is not the last line, so it does not act as a comic button.
- **Signature:** the final sentence is 15 words. shape_check reads it as `quiet` ✓ (it would be `punch`
  at ≤7 words and `long-fade` at ≥25).
- **Plainness score: 9/10** (before: 6/10). I held back one point because "I pulled it back onto my
  knees" still faintly echoes the earlier "on my knees". That is a posture fact, not a five-word refrain.
  Cutting the posture too would leave the chapter ending on a bare inventory ("Half the basket was
  still to clean.", 7 words), which flips the signature to `punch` and breaks the ledger.
- **Midnight test (inverted on purpose):** a reader *can* put the book down here, and that is the
  design. The tension carried forward is Ch.9's Valens danger, which this chapter leaves untouched and
  so leaves unreleased.
- **Alternatives considered (not applied):**
  - End on "It was a good while yet till the lamps." (9 words, `quiet`). This is plainer in content, but
    a held time-marker as the last sentence reads as waiting, a soft ominous note. Plainness 7.
  - "…and I started on the rest." This ends on "the rest", which picks up a second sense. Plainness 7.
  - "…and I pulled it back into my lap." This repeats "in my lap" from the papyrus beat in ¶ "The
    papyrus still lay open in my lap" and from Ch.2 and Ch.5. Rejected for echo risk.
  - "The sun had gone off the step." This was my first recast of the sun line. The echo scan caught it
    ("had gone off the", Ch.6; "gone off the" 3x book-wide: Ch.6, 8, 10), so I replaced it with "The
    step was in shade."
- **Outside my territory (noted, not touched):**
  - ¶ "The gate came to behind her…" ends "Up at the last door the broom still lay over the threshold,
    from post to post." That is the outline's emotional anchor and is required. It sits two paragraphs
    before the close, so the image-with-meaning lives there, where the outline put it, and not in the
    last line.
  - ¶ "Before I folded it…" ("To myself it was a cheerful letter, from a man with a roof to sit on in
    the evenings.") is dry and earned, and it is the emotional surprise. It draws no parallel to Mara's
    own choice.
