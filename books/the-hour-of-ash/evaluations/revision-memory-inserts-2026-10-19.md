# Revision Log: the Philadelphia memory inserts, Ch.6–10 (2026-10-19)

**Author ruling (AUTHOR-CP2-2):** "Wouldn't those memories break immersion? Vary them as well as trimming the ones already there."

**Problem** (feedback/independent-review-ch6-10.md, problem 3): five stand-alone 1990s Philadelphia memories, each shaped the same way:
- two or three sentences;
- a precise Philadelphia place;
- a close on a small loss of knowing.

The pipeline's "irrelevant thought" operation was hardening into a signature move.

**Rule applied:** each insert was either cut or folded into its scene. A fold must be triggered by something on the page, return to the scene at once, sit inside an existing paragraph and never close on a lost name. Any memory kept varies in shape from its neighbours, and no chapter keeps two.

**Result:** four of the five are cut and one is folded. Ch.10's Homs line survives as present-scene knowledge, not a memory. No first sentence, final paragraph or dialogue line was touched. No posture or bend was added.

## The five, before → after

### 1. Ch.6, Larchwood (CUT)
- **Before** (its own paragraph, between the scar on Valens's hand and the mules):
  "In the ice storm of January '94 the man in the ground-floor apartment on Larchwood had appeared at six in the morning in his bathrobe and poured a whole bag of cat litter down the front steps. In March he moved out. Nobody in the building ever learned his name."
- **After:** nothing. "It was an older hand than the rest of him." now runs straight into "He brought the mules up singly."
- **Why:** it was pure detour and closed on a lost name. The ashes Valens throws for the ice already carry the beat it echoed.

### 2. Ch.6, Ray's snow tires (CUT)
- **Before** (the last sentence of the hoof-binding paragraph):
  "...pulling the twine tight with his teeth. Ray put the snow tires on the Buick every Thanksgiving weekend whether it had snowed or not, and took them off on Easter Monday whether it had stopped or not, and drove on them all winter on the theory that the tires knew something."
- **After:** "...pulling the twine tight with his teeth."
- **Why:** the Ch.6 pair could not both stay, and Ch.6 already has two scene-tied memories that are protected: the father winding the watch, and the cliff speech with Gloria's earrings. Keeping this one would have made three 1998 surfacings in one chapter. The paragraph now ends on Valens's work. The kept crouch (the hooves on the ice) is untouched.

### 3. Ch.8, the Vermont brothers' tree lot (CUT)
- **Before** (its own paragraph, between Mara joining the ring and Sostrata's "Stamp"):
  "Every December two brothers from Vermont sold Christmas trees from the corner lot at Twenty-second and Pine and slept in a camper parked beside them, and they burned their offcuts all night in an oil drum with holes punched round the bottom. Coming home from the bus I used to stop beside it for a minute with my hands out to the drum, among strangers, in the smell of burning fir."
- **After:** nothing. "...I joined the ring, mostly for the sake of my feet." now runs into "'Stamp,' Sostrata told me..."
- **Why:** it was pure detour, and it paused the chapter's set piece at the moment Mara joins it. Ch.8 keeps one memory, which is scene-tied and protected: the pay at the bank downtown, which closes the money paragraph on the walk back.

### 4. Ch.9, the hardware store off Fortieth Street (FOLDED)
- **Before:** the filing paragraph, followed by a paragraph of its own:
  "Then the rod. The smith had sent his second-best file with it, I think in the hope that I would ruin it. [...]"
  "There had been a hardware store off Fortieth Street that sold screws singly out of little wooden drawers, by weight, with a cat asleep on the counter scale, and its name had gone, and I filed through most of an afternoon hunting for it."
- **After:** one clause inside the existing paragraph, triggered by the rod and returning to the smith in the same sentence:
  "Then the rod. A hardware store off Fortieth Street sold pins like it singly out of little wooden drawers, with a cat asleep on the counter scale; the smith had sent his second-best file with this one, I think in the hope that I would ruin it."
- **Removed:**
  - the stand-alone paragraph;
  - "by weight";
  - the lost-name close ("its name had gone");
  - the afternoon spent hunting for the name.
- **Why this shape:** it differs from its neighbours.
  - Ch.8's kept memory is a whole sentence that opens "In Philadelphia" and closes its paragraph.
  - Ch.7's Dunphy is a long stand-alone paragraph (protected, planned to recur).
  - This one enters mid-sentence off an object and leaves inside the same sentence.
  - It ends on the cat, not on a loss. "Late in the day the rod went in" still carries the time that passes.

### 5. Ch.10, the Van Pelt bookmark postcard (CUT; Homs kept as a fact, as a same-length rewrite)
- **Before** (between the roof/land paragraph of the letter and Kallia's "A roof you sit on"):
  "Emesa was Homs. There had been a postcard of a crusader castle somewhere near Homs, a great tawny thing on a hill. I had used it as a bookmark in a library book on another subject entirely and returned the book across the desk at Van Pelt with the castle still in it. The oxen and the man to drive them went by while I was at that desk."
- **After:**
  "Emesa was Homs, on the Orontes, and the mountains with snow on them would be the Lebanon. I had river and range laid out as they lay on the map, with the road running west from the town through a gap in the hills to the sea, and the oxen and the man to drive them went by while I was still on that road. Kallia had not left the roof."
- **What it is now:** no Philadelphia and no memory. It is Mara placing the letter's river and snowy mountains as she reads them. The beat where the letter's words slip past her unheard ("the oxen and the man to drive them went by") is kept, and Kallia's next line ("A roof you sit on") follows from "Kallia had not left the roof."
- **Why not shorter (the brief asked for a shorter sentence if a cut would breach the band):**
  - Ch.10 was already at 40.47% dialogue (990 of 2,446 words), which displays as 40% against a 30–40% band.
  - Any net cut of more than 1 narration word pushes the displayed share to 41%. "Emesa was Homs." alone would have made it 41.7%.
  - Holding the band therefore meant a replacement at least as long as the original. It runs 71 words to the original's 69, and the share is now 40.44%, a hair under where it started.
- **Geography checked:** Homs is on the Orontes; the snow-held Lebanon range lies to its west; the Homs Gap carries the road west to the coast. "in my head" was dropped from a draft because it occurs in voice-bank samples 11 and 13.
