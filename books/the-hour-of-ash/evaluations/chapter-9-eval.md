- The disruptor's uneven ladder works: the first dig-out is implied ("On the second try", ¶194), the second is the bee-man's "Out it comes," (¶196), the third is the knife skidding "too fast" (¶254). The compulsion is in the pace, not in a sentence about it.
- It is interrupted three times by other lives: the mistress and her mirror (¶198–¶210), Valens and the pitch (¶214–¶248), the bee-man's asides.
- **Residue:** each fill still closes on a finding sentence ("the line came back as a thin gold thread", ¶192; "my own thumbprint showed in it", ¶194; "the line was there", ¶252; "I had lost it myself", ¶258). Four findings in a row is the report rhythm in miniature. It survives because each finding is a different physical fact (gloss, softness, texture, light). No change ordered; the redo-the-seam scene is now SPENT, as the bible says.

**2. Does each document reveal, and does the curse tablet stay pure texture? Manumission and notice REVEAL; the tablet is texture in plot, not quite in diction.**
- *The manumission reveals* three things the chapter spends: the paramone (freedom with strings), the price scale (three minae against her stater and goat), and Euthydikos (a witness, then the man in the court).
- *The notice reveals* Lupus, Celer, the scar and the two rewards, and its "property of that household" lands on "by beating and by selling" from ¶6 without a word from the narrator.
- *The tablet:* W-08 removed the "Thracian woman" name, so it no longer points at Valens. It names nobody we know, changes nothing, and the boy's "Is it worth anything?" (¶288) folds it into the chapter's price talk as comedy. That is texture.
- *But* its epithet ties it to the first document by diction: "not to be laid hold of" (¶6) → "Hermes the Holder … I hold her under the lead" (¶266). With a runaway notice next, the three documents read as free / bound / hunted. The outline wanted a document that means nothing; this one means a little. Optional fix: Revision #16.
- Its paragraph also spends "the writing surfaced" for the third time (P6).

**3. Does the priest's bargaining carry the subtext unspoken? YES. It is the best-written exchange in the book so far.**
- He prices her by her material, never by her sex: "A smith's price is for a smith's fire. I have wax from my own bees." (¶142). The insult travels underneath, and the bee-man's wax later becomes the fill.
- He talks past her to the slave (¶78, ¶98, ¶116), and "He sought the answer from the slave, and the slave had none for him" (¶120) makes the device comic.
- His only direct addresses are for money and rulings ("Half.", "In what light?", "Daylight,").
- He takes her advice on chilblains and turns it into a slur in the same breath: "Like a slave at the olive picking." (¶162).
- The payoff is a gesture, not a line: coins from above, the fingers wiped (¶312–¶314).
- Two surface leaks, both acceptable: "Where does a woman learn bronze?" (¶112), which is incredulity before the bargaining, and the narrator's "where a husband might have stood" (¶110), an image that states the subtext once. Keep both; do not add a third.

**4. After two chapters at a distance, does Valens's one exchange land, with no romance signal? YES, modestly, and the romance pull comes from the notice rather than the talk.**
- *It lands as character:* he scans the court before he answers ("His eyes went past me … to the stand … and to the old man … who had gone to sleep sitting up", ¶228): who is watching. "That's the god's," (¶230) is a muleteer reading risk, not workmanship. "Somebody will." (¶240) is his whole worldview in two words and is never glossed. "He'll pay." (¶248) is credit to a woman with nothing, and the obol becomes the object at the board (¶416).
- *The body is right:* the mule swings to bite and "he put her nose away with his elbow and went on working" (¶216). The disruptor's removal of the two-fingered caress was correct.
- *Romance signal: none*, in his lines or the narration.
- *Cautions (not holds):* "How long will it stand?" is his fourth "How long…?"; "What for?" is a borrowed question; the exchange is a ping-pong of perfectly responsive turns (#17); and his whole scene is mule care again (Cross-Book #5). The rear-gate staging mirrors voice-bank sample-08 (Valens rubbing a substance into gear while she asks about it; Revision #13).

**5. Does the silent notice scene work as the beat subversion? YES.**
- She reads it twice and the body answers ("The rag had gone damp in my fist.", ¶406); he reads it "while a slow man might read it once, and then as long again" (¶412), which keeps his literacy unconfirmed for Ch.11.
- The look is held across the indifferent traffic of a passing cart (¶416) and neither speaks. "I looked at his face." (¶414) keeps her eyes off the hand the notice describes: loyalty shown, not told.
- Voice under pressure level 1 is exact: no aside from ¶404 to the end.
- The men at the board supply the danger in their own arithmetic ("For three hundred I'd give up…", ¶388) so the narrator never has to.
- Two cautions: the passing traffic as the measure of a held moment is the third use (Ch.1 ¶373 the car, Ch.5 ¶193 the disc buyer; Revision #12, spend it here and retire it); and "I stayed where I was." (¶416) is Ch.5's "She stayed where she was." (Ch.5 ¶237) with the pronoun changed (P-list, Revision #12).

**6. Does the 6-word punch land? YES.**
- "He let the gray drink first." is a plain observed action, no aphorism, no device, 0 echoes.
- It carries three readings without stating any: composure (he does not run, in a square of men who would sell him); care (the animal before himself); and staying ("first" implies he will drink after, here).
- With the caress gone it reads as character, not sentiment, as the disruptor intended.
- The one structural note is that it is Valens rendered through the gray for the fourth chapter running (Cross-Book #5). The line itself is right; Ch.11 must not need the mule.

**7. At 5,840 words against 6,400, is anything thin? The chapter does not read thin. One seam is under-written, and one wound goes missing.**
- *The dawn try (¶262 → ¶268):* her all-or-nothing beat ends off the page. The next section is dawn at the spring with a jar, which implies the dawn went to Kallia's water. That is a good cost, but it is unstated, so it reads as a dropped thread. One clause lands it (Revision #1, +≈15w).
- *The cut thumb* (¶254) never comes back: not at the payment, the basin or the rag. Re-using it in the basin replaces the Ch.5 scrub (P9) and closes the seam (Revision #2).
- The rest is right-sized: Valens's exchange (≈280w) is the length of a practical man's patience; Kallia's scene (≈380w) is brief before Ch.10, which is hers; the notice section is short because pressure level 1 is short.
- **Do not pad.** The PATH nets about −40 words, to ≈5,800, the range floor. STATE has no per-chapter floor.
