## Opening (Hook)
- **Type:** VOICE (a claim that carries the narrator's opinion). Not IN MEDIAS RES ✓ (Ch.28 was IN MEDIAS RES).
- **Score:** 7/10 (Information gap 2/3, Emotional pull 1/3, Voice 2/2, Connection 2/2). The book's threshold is 7, so this passes.
- **Rewritten:** no.
- **First sentence:** *"Of everything the Romans ever thought of, the roads and the arches and the concrete included, Saturnalia was the best."* It is 19 words, inside the 9–27 band. shape_check reads it as `narrative` ✓, which matches the ledger's claim/opinion opening.
- **Why it works:** it makes a bold ranking in Mara's own terms. She puts the festival above the roads, the arches and the concrete, and a conservator would list exactly those things. The past tense "was" reads as a verdict she has reached. The chapter's shape is claim, then evidence, then shame. The opening states the claim plainly, so the end can complicate it without the opening having hinted at it. That keeps the no-foreshadowing rule. The second sentence ("I gathered it the way I gathered other people's broken pots") commits to the essayistic frame in voice. Within the next paragraph the first piece of evidence is already uneasy: "nothing in that early light, at the width of a street, said which of the two men owned the other." So the information gap is real.
- **Emotional pull (1/3):** the opening is cheerful. That is right for the register, but it holds little charge by itself. The charge comes from the white space: the reader arrives from "You were sitting down." and is offered "the best" thing Rome ever thought of. The contrast works on that reader without a word of recap.
- **Connection (2/2):** Ch.28 left an open wound in the household. Ch.29 does not recap the bout and does not answer the wound. Its first move is the household's best season, and that is the bridge. It honours the Ch.28 guard: "Felix alive", with no recap.
- **Alternatives considered (not used):**
  - CONTRAST, "The slave who baked our bread came to the door in the cap of liberty, and so did the man who owned him." (7). It spends the cap image, which the third paragraph earns with the Brutus coin. It would also break the ledger's claim opening and point too early toward the shame beat.
  - QUESTION, "The best thing the Romans ever thought of was a week when you could not tell who owned whom." (6). It states the thesis and its flaw together. That collapses the essay's arc into the first line and comes close to sounding like an aphorism.
  - TICKING CLOCK was not considered. Nothing in the festival runs to a deadline, and forcing one would be dishonest.

## Ending (Pull)
- **Type:** QUESTION PLANT (a quiet image). Not EMOTIONAL GUT PUNCH ✓ (Ch.28 was EMOTIONAL GUT PUNCH).
- **Score:** 8/10 (Forward pull 2/3, Emotional charge 3/3, Unpredictability 2/2, Promise 1/2).
- **Rewritten:** the closing line is not rewritten. Two sentences before it were trimmed (see Edits).
- **Closing lines (final):** *"The tapers inside were down to their last finger of wax, and I blew them dark and left the brazier to itself. Back on the step for the last cup, I found the street unlit both ways as far as the fountain. Under the bakers' shutter the oven was showing a strip of light on the stones again, and somebody was feeding it."* The last sentence is 21 words. shape_check reads it as `quiet` ✓.
- **Why it works:**
  - "Somebody" is the whole pull. Mara knows exactly who is feeding the oven: Cerdo, sent home for the second firing, with the cap pulled off and pushed into his belt halfway across the street. The narration gives him back the anonymity the festival had suspended.
  - "Again" carries the second half. The oven was dark while he sat at her table, because "nobody else of mine knows that oven". The light coming back is the festival ending.
  - Nothing is stated about the shame. The paragraph before keeps both halves unresolved ("I had made my case all week, and I believed it; I still believed it at the bucket"). The image adds no verdict to it.
  - The question planted is felt rather than spoken: what Mara can do with what her hand did. The book does not answer that here.
- **Promise (1/2):** this is a deliberate softness. The pull keeps the chapter's promise, because the claim has met its cost, but it opens nothing the next chapter must pay off. That is correct here. The house rule bans foreshadowing, and the outline says only that Ch.30 opens on "winter turns; the floor moves". A pull that leaned toward the tremor would break the no-foreshadowing law.
- **Rule checks:**
  - It does not foreshadow. It is a present observation of a working oven and does not reach forward.
  - The oven and its light are not used as a scene-button. This is the first and only strip of light in the book (grep: chapter-29 only). The shutter appears earlier only as the place the master's shout comes through.
  - It is not an aphorism and has no gloss. The shame is not resolved.
  - It does not echo the opening, so it avoids the symmetrical-ending reflex.
  - Midnight test: it passes as a quiet pull. The reader is left uneasy, and the feeling sits in one word.
- **Alternatives considered (not used):**
  - DECISION POINT, ending on the pits ("I left the field where Fenn had laid it…") (6). It is warmer, but it resolves toward the friendship. It also ends the chapter on the exiles instead of the shame, against the ledger's "personal turn … a quiet image to end".
  - EMOTIONAL GUT PUNCH, ending on the rinsed cup "going round in the water among the others" (7). It is strong, but it is the same type as Ch.28's pull, which is banned. It is also closer to an object used as a button than the oven is.
  - OMINOUS PROMISE was excluded on principle (no foreshadowing; light before the tremor must not point at the tremor).

## Edits (line 586 only; the closing sentence is unchanged)
- **Before:** "The tapers inside were down to their last finger of wax, and I blew them dark, every one, until only the brazier was left. Back on the step for the last cup, I found the street dark both ways as far as the fountain."
- **After:** "The tapers inside were down to their last finger of wax, and I blew them dark and left the brazier to itself. Back on the step for the last cup, I found the street unlit both ways as far as the fountain."
- **Why:**
  - (1) "dark" appeared in two sentences in a row, two sentences before the last line. "Unlit" is better than a plain synonym. After a festival in which every customer paid in wax, the whole street having its lamps out sets up the oven as the one light in the street, and it does so without stating it.
  - (2) "every one" was the chapter's 17th "every", and "every" is on the bible's WATCH list. Cutting it compresses the paragraph so energy tightens toward the last line instead of slackening.
  - (3) A first trim, "blew them dark until only the brazier was left", tripped the 5c echo scan ("dark until only the", Ch.15). It was re-varied to "and left the brazier to itself", which has no echo. Book-wide uses of "to itself" are all different constructions (Ch.5, 19, 25, 15 "into itself").
- **Counts:** narration −2 words. shape 7,015 → 7,013, style 7,030 → 7,028, show/tell 7,034 → 7,032. Dialogue stays at 35.3% (shape and show/tell), at or above the 35% floor. The header on line 6 has been updated to match.
