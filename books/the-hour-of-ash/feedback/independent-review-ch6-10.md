# Independent read — Chapters 6–10 (2026-10-19)

A full read of Ch.6–10, separate from the pipeline's evaluators, done the same way as the Ch.1–5 read:
length, content, metaphor load, and the habits the gates cannot count. Ch.10's own evaluation was still
running when this was written; nothing here depends on it.

## Verdict

The book is better than it was at Ch.5. The prose is cleaner, the supporting cast is the strongest thing
in it, and the pipeline has driven out nearly every habit the first read flagged. Three things now matter more
than anything at the sentence level: **the romance has had one scene in 54,000 words**, **the plot is idling
while the domestic chapters are excellent**, and **a new device is forming — the Philadelphia memory insert**.

- **Length is on target.** Ch.1–10 were budgeted at 57,800; they came in at 54,371 (94%). Held at that ratio,
  the book lands near 245,000 — exactly the target, comfortably above the 220,000 floor. Nothing is padded;
  every shortfall came from cutting.
- **Metaphor is not the problem.** Two similes per thousand words, against a gate of four. They are concrete and
  they come from Mara's world: Kallia guarding her tree "like a crossing guard who had lost her sign", the
  snow on the cliff rim "like a glass dipped in sugar", a stone-cutter's guidelines "fine as the scratches on a
  long-playing record", warmth under a shepherd's cloak "like a laundromat in February". None explains a feeling.
  The stock-phrase scan found one "whispered" in five chapters, and it describes snow.
- **Shape is varied.** Five chapters, five different openings (mid-action, claim, dialogue, document, object)
  and endings (a spoken line, a question, a quiet image, a six-word punch, a plain stop). Lengths run
  2,566–7,462. Breaks run 0 to 5. The strict shape audit is clean across all ten chapters.
- **The first read's fixes held.** And-chains, which were 23 in Ch.3, now run 1–5 a chapter. Credential asides
  run 0–2. The comprehension problem is handled consistently: she follows slow plain speech and loses fast talk.

## Content: the best of it

- **Kallia is the book's best character, and Ch.10 is the best chapter.** A woman who can't read hands the
  letter that ends her hope to the one person who can, and Mara reads it perfectly — "It was the best Greek I
  had spoken since October, and it was his from beginning to end." The double yawn, "Keep it out of the smoke",
  the broom put back across the threshold, and Nikaso spreading "a grandson" down the lane are all exactly right,
  and nobody draws the parallel to Mara. It earns the plain stop.
- **Ch.6 is a real first meeting.** Valens holding the torch so his shadow falls away from her work; winding the
  watch backward "as if the click were what it was for"; bread carried up the mountain against his skin, "exactly
  the temperature of a person"; "It was the first thing you did up here I understood." That is chemistry without
  a single romance word in the narration.
- **Ch.8 is the set piece the outline promised.** The rite is physical and funny ("It is bread." / "It's bread as
  well."), the sandal farce is genuinely comic, "I lost him on the boat" is the laugh that admits her, and the
  stranger's "Each morning I tell you about the weather, and each morning you look at my feet" is the chapter.
- **Ch.7's registration lands as relief** — written into wax that will be scraped next year, "the only thing in
  that whole country that had ever said I was in it" — and the zip-code scene shows the cost without a word of
  self-pity.
- **Ch.9's ending works.** The priest drops the coins from above so his fingers stay clear of her palm and then
  wipes them; the notice; Valens reading it "while a slow man might read it once, and then as long again"; "He
  let the gray drink first." Nobody says anything.
- **The history holds.** The paramone clause and the "sold to Apollo" form are right; auxiliaries saved at the
  standards; Jerusalem fell in late summer AD 70; giant fennel smoulders; Plutarch's frozen cloaks belonged to the
  rescuers. The pipeline caught a Julia Domna wink, a "Thraitta" one section before a Thracian runaway, and a
  Sicily trip canon forbids.

## Problems, in order of importance

1. **The romance has one scene.** Valens's name or his mule appears in about 1,000 words of Ch.6 and about 1,200
   words across Ch.7–10 combined — his boots and "No." in Ch.7, his back at the last house in Ch.8, ten lines
   over a mule's sore in Ch.9, nothing in Ch.10. At 22% of the book, a romance reader has had one two-hander.
   This is by design (the outline puts the Valens chapters at 11–12) and every chapter has been worth reading,
   but the reader will feel the absence by Ch.9. **Ch.11 has to be a substantial two-hander, not another
   glimpse**, and he should not be framed through the gray mule again; the evaluators have now flagged that for
   four chapters running.

2. **The plot is idling.** The book's question is how to get home. Since Ch.5 the cave has been shut three
   times (Ch.6, Ch.8, and a dawn try in Ch.9 that never happens), and each time the chapter turns to belonging
   instead. Belonging is the theme and the domestic chapters are the strongest writing in the book, but four
   consecutive chapters (7–10) are essentially episodes of Mara's life in Kallia's house. The Oracle goal (Ch.8)
   and the Celer threat (end of Ch.9) are the first forward pressure in 30,000 words. **Ch.11–12 need to move**;
   the outline has them doing so (the yard, Celer's men, a killing).

3. **A new device: the Philadelphia memory insert.** Nearly every chapter now has a short, self-contained paragraph
   of 1990s memory dropped into the scene:
   - Ch.6: the Larchwood neighbour and the cat litter; Ray's snow tires
   - Ch.7: Mrs. Dunphy (planned)
   - Ch.8: the Vermont brothers' tree lot
   - Ch.9: the hardware store off Fortieth Street
   - Ch.10: the Van Pelt bookmark

   Each one is good on its own. But they share a shape: two or three sentences, a precise Philadelphia place, and
   a close on a small loss of knowing ("Nobody in the building ever learned his name", "its name had gone"). It is
   the pipeline's "irrelevant thought" operation hardening into a signature move, and no gate counts it.
   **Recommendation: cap it at one every other chapter, and never end one on a lost name again.**

4. **Kneeling and crouching run high.** 24 times in 54,000 words (0.46 per thousand), above the 0.30 the gate
   allows any other gesture class. Most of it is deliberate: Mara kneeling at the crack is the plot, Kallia
   crouches because she never sits, Mara crouches to read inscriptions. But Ch.8 has six and Ch.5 five. This is
   your call: either it is a characterising habit and stays, or it becomes a gated class and the next chapters
   carry fewer.

5. **Smaller habits, watch only:**
   - Mara noticing what she *could* have done with a broken thing ("I could have had it whole again in a week",
     "I could have left out the roof"): fine twice, a device at three.
   - Paragraphs that close on someone else's dry line ("He's cheap.", "Not that she ever used it much."). The
     first read worried about Mara's own dry buttons; she has mostly handed them to the cast, which is better,
     but the shape is the same.
   - Looking at feet. It is thematic (Kallia's lesson) and paid off beautifully by the stranger, so it has done
     its work; let it rest.

## What the pipeline has been doing

Every first evaluation of Ch.6–10 failed on Prose & Voice for narrator moves the gates could not see, and each
cycle the gate learned one more: negation pivots, the "So/Then/After that" hinge family, "before I had decided",
voice-bank lifts, command echoes, pronoun-swapped echoes, a bending class. That is working; first drafts are now
cleaner than finished chapters were a week ago. The cost is real, though: every chapter has taken one or two polish
cycles plus a continuity repair, and several cycles fixed problems the previous cycle created.

## Suggested actions (author's call; nothing has been changed)

- **Ch.11 brief:** a full two-hander with Valens, away from the mule, and something must change between them.
- **Ch.11–12:** let the plot pull. No more quiet chapters until the Oracle.
- **Cap the Philadelphia memory insert** at one every other chapter, and never end one on a lost name.
- **Decide kneel/crouch:** characterising habit, or gated gesture.
- **Keep the four no-pull chapters.** Ch.10 shows the plain stop working.
