## What KDP distinguishes

KDP has asked about AI content since **September 2023**, whenever you publish a new title or edit and republish an existing one.

- **AI-generated (disclose).** KDP defines this as text, images or translations *created by* an AI-based tool. The quoted policy says it still counts as AI-generated **even if you applied substantial edits afterwards**.
- **AI-assisted (no disclosure needed).** You created the content yourself and used AI to edit, refine, error-check or improve it, or you used AI to brainstorm and then wrote the text yourself. KDP says you don't need to tell it about this.

In both cases you remain responsible for making sure the book follows all KDP content guidelines, including not infringing anyone's copyright.

## Which applies to *Sygl*

**AI-generated, for the text.**

The script, the plot, the characters, the dialogue base and every story decision are yours. The finished novel's prose, though, was written by an AI tool from that script and then revised and edited. Under KDP's definition, AI-written text stays "AI-generated" even after heavy editing. Your script being the source doesn't move the prose into "AI-assisted", because the AI produced the sentences.

Amazon doesn't define an editing threshold that would turn generated text into "assisted". When in doubt, the compliant position is to disclose.

## What to tick (verify against the live form)

The KDP question asks about each content type separately:

| Content type | Answer for *Sygl* |
|---|---|
| **Text** | **Yes, AI-generated.** If the form asks how much, the honest answer is that the entire work's prose was AI-generated from your script and then edited (choose the option that describes the whole book with editing). If the form asks for the tool, name the one used. |
| **Images** | Depends on the cover. **Yes** if any cover or interior art was made with an AI image tool (even if retouched); **No** if a human designer or licensed stock made it. |
| **Translations** | **No** (the book is written in English). |

Reports at launch said the answer is **not shown to readers** on the product page. That could change, so check.

## The general copyright caveat

- **U.S. law requires a human author.** The U.S. Copyright Office's position, set out in its 2025 report on copyrightability and AI and upheld by the courts in *Thaler v. Perlmutter* (D.C. Cir., 2025), is that **material generated by AI is not protected by copyright on its own**.
- **What can be protected** is the human contribution: your original script (`Sygl_Complete.txt`), your characters and story as you expressed them, your own edits and rewrites, and your selection and arrangement of the material.
- **In practice:** your rights in the AI-written sentences themselves may be thin. Someone copying the prose word for word could be harder to stop than if you had written every line. Your rights in the story you created are on firmer ground.
- **If you register the copyright** with the U.S. Copyright Office, the Office requires you to disclose AI-generated material and claim only the human-authored parts.
- **Other countries** treat this differently (the UK, for example, has a separate rule for computer-generated works). This note covers the U.S. position only.
- **Keep records:** your original script, the author rulings in `STATE.yaml` and the revision history all show the human authorship and the human editing. They are useful evidence if the question ever comes up.
