# Chapter Fifteen: Rumors

"I'll be gone all day," Amelia said.

The room was still gray. She sat on his side of the bed, dressed already, with a hand resting on the blanket over his chest. Behind her the card was still propped by the lamp on the nightstand, where she had stood it last night, the name turned toward her pillow.

"Let me know if you need anything," she said.

She bent and kissed him on the mouth, without any hurry about it, and he left his eyes shut afterward to make it last. When he opened them the hand was gone off the blanket. Her side of the bed was smooth.

The card hadn't moved.

---

Every teacher in the castle had come to one conclusion overnight. Yesterday had been a free day, and today was going to pay for it.

Mr. Porter set the class an essay on the duties of a familiar before he had sat down, and watched Bal the whole time they wrote it. Mr. Winters filled his chalkboard, wiped it, and filled it again. In Warfare the instructor ran them through forms in the sand until nobody had any breath left to spend on talking, and Lora, three places down the line, watched him with the point of her rapier dead level from the first form to the last. Even in Crafting the only sounds were files on metal and somebody's stool creaking.

Nobody chatted. Nobody got the chance. Bal passed Viridia once in a corridor between classes, and she pulled a face at him over an armload of books, and that was all either of them said to the other until dinner.

---

He was in his place and halfway through a plate of pot roast when Lora came in.

She paused in the doorway with her books in both arms. Her gaze went first to her own sygl's table, where her stretch of empty bench was waiting as usual, and then down the whole length of the hall to him.

She came the long way, straight down the middle.

The noise did not stop in one go. It died behind her as she passed each table, so that she walked at the front edge of a quiet that kept growing. The Terra boys stopped. The Tempus table stopped. When she reached the last table, a spoon went down on a plate two tables over, and it was loud. Even the teachers had stopped eating.

Lora set her books on the bench. She slid in next to Viridia, facing him, tore a corner from a page of her notes, wrote her order and laid it on her plate. The plate went off empty and returned with a bowl of soup and two rolls.

She buttered the first roll right out to the crust at every edge, while the whole hall watched her do it.

Viridia returned to her spaghetti. Bal ate his pot roast. Somewhere behind him somebody coughed, and the talk came back in pieces, lower than before and nowhere near as busy.

The three of them didn't look around. The easy feeling from yesterday evening had come to dinner with them, and they kept it close and ignored the rest.

"So what was it you gave Amelia?" Lora asked.

Bal finished his milk and waited.

It filled from the bottom, as it always did, white climbing the inside of the cup as if someone under the table were pouring. He had looked under the table, his first night. There was only the floor and his own shoes. He still didn't know how the cup decided it was full.

"I gave her a Dawn card," he said. "A team card, with her name on the back. I think what hit her so hard, though, was that I put her down as Amelia Norheart."

Lora stopped, the roll forgotten in her hand. Then she bit and chewed and went on chewing.

"Why do you think it hit her that hard?" she asked, once she could.

"Look what her family did to her," Viridia said. "They betrayed her. She honestly had nobody. Bal showed her that now she does." She stuck her fork into her spaghetti and wound it, and kept winding long after it was wound. "I would have felt the same way."

She took the bite. When it was gone she said, "I'm happy to be friends with her. With all of you, really. I just hope it stays this easy."

"Stays easy how?" Bal asked.

Viridia shrugged.

"Just some rumors I heard this morning. Word is they're going to start enforcing a rule. Unless you're in class, or off school grounds, you can't mingle with the other sygls."

Bal looked past her. Over at Lora's old table a boy found his food again the moment Bal's eyes reached him. One of the teachers had not bothered to turn his back at all.

"Do you think they will?"

"Probably," Viridia said. "They weren't happy when it was only me hanging around with you. Now we've got Lora too, and her a Waves sygl? Yeah. It's a good possibility."

"What could they do to you if you refused?"

Viridia winced. It was the question she had been hoping he would skip, and she made him wait for the answer.

"It depends on whether it comes as a school rule," she said, "or just something all the Lead Mages try to do. If it's a school rule, they could expel us. If it's only the Lead Mages, it's demerits."

At *expel*, Lora's butter knife stayed in her fist, upright on the tablecloth with its point to the ceiling, and Bal doubted she knew it was there.

So the school would make it a rule. Probably.

Bal drew in a long breath and eased it out. If the teachers did this, there was just one way he could still be around the two of them, and it was not the kind of thing you said over soup.

"There's something we could do," he told them. "But I'd rather wait to tell you what it is until we need it."

Viridia leaned in. Lora's eyes went very wide behind her glasses. They both said all right, and they both meant it, and they were both plainly dying to know how anybody got around the school's own rules.

Lora noticed the knife in her fist, and used it on her second roll.

---

After dinner the three of them walked to the Blood dorm together, past every open door in the corridor.

The new door was in the wall beside the library. Bal had put it there that morning, between waking up and finding his socks. He had stood barefoot at the hearth and pictured Lora with the green book, reading the poultice chapter aloud until somebody stopped her, and then he had pictured where she would actually do it. A long bench scrubbed pale. A stone sink with a pump. Beams overhead with herbs hung upside down to dry. A window, so whatever she boiled had somewhere to go.

Lora opened the door and smelled dried mint and did not come out again.

Bal and Viridia kept to the couches. Viridia spread her runes homework over the low table and chewed her pencil over it. Bal took the old couch with the history of Raizen's wars.

From the herb room came the squeal of the pump. Much later Lora's voice said "No, no, no, no" to something that hissed.

Viridia finished her last rune and sat back. She watched him turn pages for a while.

"How come you're reading that?"

Bal kept his place with a finger. "Because they've started training again. And they aren't going after Naisura."

Viridia was quiet.

"You mean they're going after Amelia's followers," she said. "Don't you."

Bal turned the page.

Any other night he would have waited until the girls had gone home to open this book at all. Raizen was Viridia's originator. But if he ever did have to offer to convert them, he wanted Viridia to understand exactly what they were up against first. He left the book open in his lap and explained it as he saw it. None of the originators were happy that Amelia was free. They had decided Raizen ranked lowest of them, so dealing with her was his job. They knew perfectly well they could not trap her again as they had before. This was all they had left, short of actually fighting her, and none of them wanted that.

Viridia listened to all of it. She didn't argue. She didn't ask anything else either. Her pencil lay across her finished runes and did not move again, and when Lora came out smelling of scorched ginger to report that the second recipe had worked, it was Bal who had to ask her how.

Amelia came back a little before curfew. One minute the arm of the far couch was empty, and the next she was perched on it, asking Lora what had burned.

---

Later, with the girls gone and the lamp turned low, Amelia lay on her side facing him.

"Why was Viridia so quiet when they left?"

He explained the rumor and the rule that might be coming, and what he had said to Viridia over Raizen's book.

"Honestly," Amelia said, "that rule sounds like something Kenmei would suggest. When the originators pulled the teachers, they probably told them Viridia was spending time with you."

"Yeah. More than likely." Bal laced his fingers behind his head. "I did tell Lora and Viridia I had a way around the rule. I didn't tell them what it was. Hopefully it doesn't come to that yet. With Raizen already getting ready for a war, if I convert Viridia now, he's going to lose it."

Amelia had never in her life cared how Raizen felt about anything, and she did not start now.

"He's going to lose it whenever it happens."

"Yeah, but it's still early," Bal argued. "I hope I don't have to offer it yet. If it comes to that, I will. I honestly don't want to lose either of them."

The blanket moved. Amelia came across the bed until she was against his side.

"Just do what you have to do," she said. "We will deal with whatever happens. Together."

She laid her head on his chest. Her hair was cool and heavy and smelled faintly of somewhere that was not the castle, wind, maybe, or pine. Bal unlaced his fingers and put one hand on her back.

On the nightstand the card leaned on the lamp's brass foot. In the next room, down in his pack, the job paper was still folded in half where he had tucked it. The weekend was a few days off. Saturday, then. Saturday they would go find Lora's flowers.
