# Chapter Seven: The Blood Dorm

The pedestal bit him.

It stood at the end of the corridor of paintings, a squat column of grey stone about as high as his waist, with the blood sygl carved deep into its flat top. Amelia stopped in front of it.

"Put your left hand on it," she said.

Bal put his palm flat over the carving. There was a sharp little prick in the middle of his hand, like a pin left in a new shirt, and before he could pull back the stone was already drinking. His blood ran out along the grooves of the carving, filling them line by line until the whole sygl stood out red on the grey, the same shape as the one under his hand. Then it sank into the stone and was gone, and the top of the pedestal was dry and grey again.

Beside them, a section of the wall slid aside with a low grinding sound. Behind it a short flight of stone steps climbed up into warm light.

Bal turned his hand up. There was a single red dot in the center of the sygl. He had barely felt it.

"Every time?" he asked.

"Every time," Amelia said. "It only opens for blood it knows."

She went up first. Bal hitched his pack higher on his shoulder. It had been dragging at him since the buggy. He followed her. Behind him the stone slid back into place and the wall was a wall again.

The steps ended in a long, low room with a fire already burning on the right-hand side. In front of the fire stood a couch upholstered in deep blood red, and next to it a wing-backed leather chair, and a coffee table set before them both. Across the room were two doors. Otherwise it was fairly plain, and smaller than he had expected for a whole dormitory.

"Each dorm is enchanted in different ways," Amelia said, wandering ahead of him to the fire. "One of the enchantments on mine is that there are no set rooms. Say we get another student. Another bedroom appears. Need a dining room? One will appear. If you want it to be permanent, normally you have to ask the Lead Mage." She threw him a look over her shoulder. "Which is you. Or a High Priest. Which is also you."

She laughed and dropped onto the red couch, and her hair fanned out over the cushion behind her.

Bal let his pack slide off his shoulder by the end of the couch and sat beside her. The couch was soft enough that he sank farther than he meant to.

"Will we get more students?" he asked.

"Oh, yes," Amelia said. "Especially once word gets out that I'm back."

She lay there a little longer with her eyes on the ceiling. Then she sighed and sat up, pulling her legs under her.

"Okay. Official stuff, before I have to go and deal with the family." She held up one finger. "Do you remember how you got down here?"

Bal ran it back in his head. The little door behind the stage, the narrow corridor, the stairs going down, the paintings. "Yes."

"Good. I may not always be with you, so you need to know your way around." Another finger. "You've only got four classes as a first year. I'll get you the list once I'm through dealing with them. There's a curfew, but as Lead Mage you're exempt from it." "The library's on the second floor, because I'm sure you're going to go hunting for it."

"I was going to ask."

"I know you were. Make friends with the other Heirs if you can. If you can't, don't worry about it. They'll come around." She leaned in. "And lastly. You like secrets, right?"

Bal gave her a sideways look. She had asked it much too sweetly. "Yes," he said, carefully.

Amelia's smile spread. "Me too. If you figure out any more enchantments in here, or any other secrets in the school, I'll reward you."

Bal couldn't help smiling back. A whole castle full of secrets, and someone to bring them to. "I can't wait."

"Me either." She uncurled from the couch and stood. "Now I really do have to go. If you need me, push your magyk into your sygl, and I'll be there."

She hugged him, quick and cool around the neck, and before he had properly hugged her back she straightened, snapped her fingers and was gone. The cushion beside him slowly puffed back up.

The fire popped. Bal sat in the quiet for a while.

---

He spent the next few hours exploring his dormitory.

There was not much of it, at first. The fireplace, the couch, the chair, the coffee table. One of the two doors across the room opened on a bathroom, white tile and a deep tub on clawed feet, and he closed that one again. The other opened on a bedroom, and he stood in the doorway with his mouth open.

It was easily twice the size of the one he'd had at the orphanage. That one had been his along with twenty other boys. This room had a four-poster bed with a canopy, and the bed was so big it looked as though five people could have slept in it without anybody kicking anybody. Across it lay a soft blanket, black, with a single rose embroidered on it in red so dark it was nearly black itself. A chest stood across the end of it, every bit as large as the bed called for.

Bal fetched his pack from the main room and lifted the lid of the chest. It was empty and smelled of cedar. He lowered the pack in, and it sat at the bottom looking very small, the whole of what he owned with room to spare all around it. He let the lid down.

He pressed a hand into the blanket. It was as soft as it looked.

Then he went out and closed the bedroom door behind him, satisfied.

A dorm with no set rooms. Bal planted himself in the center of the main room and thought about what that meant. If a room appeared when you needed it, then what did he need? A library, obviously. That was the first thing. And if he was going to live here, a kitchen. And somewhere to eat whatever got cooked in it.

As he thought it, the wall facing the fire shifted. Three new doors stood in it, side by side, as though they had always stood there and he had only now thought to look.

Bal stared at them. Then he went straight to the first one.

It was a kitchen. There was a wood-burning stove against one wall with a stack of split logs beside it, and two big chests off to one side, and above the chests a row of shelves crowded with little jars of spices. He unstoppered a jar of something brown, sniffed it and sneezed. He opened the chests. One held meat, wrapped in paper and cold to the touch. The other was full of vegetables, carrots still with dirt on them and a sack of onions. The kitchen had a stove in it and food in it, which was what kitchens were supposed to have, and he stood there pleased with it anyway.

The second door was the library.

It was smaller than the common room, with tall bookshelves lining the walls and five chairs drawn up in front of another fireplace. The fire was laid but not lit. The shelves were bare from top to bottom. Bal ran his hand along the nearest. Not even dust.

He was a little disappointed. Then he looked again at the five chairs, and the empty hearth, and the long bare shelves, and pictured them full. It would be a good room to read in, once he had something to put on them. Fredrick's books would fill about a foot of one shelf. That was a start.

The third door was the dining room. A large mahogany table took up most of it, dark and glossy enough that the ceiling showed in it, with polished wooden chairs set all around. Bal walked the length of the table and back. Passing the salt would take a long arm.

Standing between the two rooms, he had a thought. Why walk your dinner across a hallway? He pictured the kitchen and the dining room as one room, the stove and the long table together.

The wall between them went soft at the edges and was simply not there anymore. Now there was one large room, warm from the stove at one end, with the mahogany table at the other.

Bal laughed out loud, alone, in his own kitchen.

He returned to the fire, stood in front of it and looked at the doors in turn. The bedroom. The bathroom. The library. The big new kitchen. He thought, as plainly as he could, *stay*.

He couldn't have said what changed. Nothing moved. But when he looked at the new doors afterward they had a settled look, the look of doors that were going to be there tomorrow.

His stomach growled.

---

He could have cooked. There was a whole chest of meat in the kitchen. But somewhere in this castle there was a dining hall, and he was curious to find it.

He took the stone steps down, and the wall slid aside to let him out. He retraced his way past the paintings, up the narrow stairs, and along the corridor, and when he got near the main part of the school he began to hear people talking and laughing.

The corridor let out into a large courtyard in the middle of the school. Students filled it, sitting around in groups on benches and the edges of planters, talking. It didn't take long to work out how they were sitting. Most of them were with others of their own sygl, Waves with Waves, Terra with Terra. A few of the braver ones had drifted over to chat with somebody from another group.

Bal glanced down at his sweater. The blood sygl had come up on the front of it at some point during the claiming, dark red on the dark wool, exactly where Verin had said it would. He hadn't even noticed.

Some of the students had noticed. A few heads turned as he stepped out of the corridor. A few conversations stopped and started up again more quietly.

"Hey, you're that Blood guy, right?"

The voice came from behind him. Bal turned quickly.

A girl stood there with her hands in her pockets. She was a little taller than he was. Her hair was pale blue, a cold, bright blue like the sky at the edge of a storm, and her eyes were the same color exactly. She had a small smile on her face.

It was the Tempus girl who had ridden ahead of them on the road that morning. So it had been her doing the crackling, after all, and not the sweater.

While he was looking at her hair, it did it again. Down near her shoulder a strand stirred on its own, and then the whole of it bristled, and a faint snapping ran through it like someone shaking out a wool blanket in winter. Then it lay flat.

The girl rolled her eyes.

"It's an inheritance thing," she said. "After Raizen did our question-and-answer thing, he told us any inheritance traits we had could start showing up today. From what I understand it's one of the rarest ones, if not the rarest. It's supposed to give me more control over storms." She shrugged. "But who knows."

Her eyes went over his shoulder to the courtyard, to the groups and the half-stopped conversations, and then back at Bal.

"No one rolling out the red carpet for you yet?"

Bal only blinked at her.

"I mean, has nobody tried to talk to you yet?" she said.

"No," Bal said. "Nobody."

"Oh. Well." She took a hand out of her pocket and stuck it out. "I'm Viridia Vizer. Heir of Raizen."

Bal shook it. Her grip was quick and firm.

"Bal," he said. He tipped his chin at the courtyard. "They probably don't know what to think about me. It's not something that happens every day."

"Yeah, that makes sense." Viridia glanced around at the groups herself. "Honestly, though, once people get over the shock, they'll probably start coming up to you. Just remember not everybody's going to be friendly. You've got a lot of years of hate and stuff to get past."

"Yeah, I know," Bal said. "Thanks for the warning, though." He looked at her more closely. "Aren't you afraid of what they'll think, you talking to me?"

Viridia snorted.

"They wouldn't say anything to me about it if they did. Kids with an inheritance are considered the elite of a class. This year I'm the only one who's manifested one so far. That could change. Even if it did, I wouldn't care. The petty politics most of these kids try to play are stupid."

"So you don't think Raizen is the greatest thing ever," Bal said, "like the rest of them?"

Viridia made a face.

"Nah. They're people just like us. They've got faults just like us. They just live longer than the rest of us."

"That's not an opinion you hear often," he said. "Most people don't criticize them. Not that I've heard, anyway."

"I'm not like most people," Viridia said.

"I can see that." Bal let his eyes go up to her hair, pointedly. "The electric blue kind of gives it away."

Viridia stuck out her tongue.

He was still looking pleased with himself when she grabbed him by the sleeve.

"Come on," she said, already towing him off between the groups. "Let's go get lunch."

---

Other students were drifting toward it too, and they followed the stream through a pair of tall doors into the dining hall.

It was a huge room, with high windows down both sides and five long tables running the length of it, each with a bench on either side. A sygl was inlaid in the wood at the head of every table. The Waves students were filling up the table with the Waves sygl, the Terra students the Terra table, and so on down the hall.

The Blood table was the last of the five, nearest the back wall. It was empty.

Bal walked down to it, sat on the bench, and looked up to find Viridia sliding onto the opposite bench.

He raised an eyebrow at her.

"Did you want to eat alone?" Viridia asked.

"Not particularly," Bal said.

"Okay, then." She settled in and planted her elbows on the table.

On each silver plate lay a piece of paper. Bal read his. It was instructions for ordering a meal. Write what you want on the back, it said, then lay the paper on your plate. After today any piece of paper would do.

Who washed the plates, if they went away on their own? Was there a room somewhere in the castle full of dirty plates, piling up? Somebody had to do it. Somebody always did.

There was a pencil beside the plate. On the back Bal wrote, in large letters, *LARGE PEPPERONI PIZZA*.

He laid it on his plate. The plate vanished. In its place sat a big silver dish, and on the dish a whole pepperoni pizza, steaming, the cheese still bubbling at the edges.

Bal reached for a slice. Across the table Viridia already had a burger in both hands and was taking a large bite out of it.

They sat in silence for a while, both of them busy with their food. The pizza was very good. It was so much better than anything the orphanage cook had ever made that Bal ate the first slice too fast and burned the roof of his mouth, and then ate a second one, and a third.

"You nervous about your classes?" he asked, once he'd finished the third.

Viridia had finished her burger. She reached across the table without asking and helped herself to a slice of his pizza, and took a bite before she answered.

"A little, yeah." She wiped a smear of grease off her chin with a napkin. "I heard you can't leave the forest until you bond a familiar."

"Why wouldn't you be able to bond one?" Bal asked. "As long as you know the incantation, it's not that hard."

Viridia lowered the pizza. Her eyes narrowed.

"You've already got a familiar," she said. It came out like an accusation. "They told all of us not to even try it until we had the class. How'd you get one already?"

Bal couldn't quite keep the smirk off his face. "Mine's a special case. Honestly, I hope I get to see your face when you meet my familiar."

"Why wouldn't you get to?" Viridia took another bite. "What is it, a wolf or something?"

"No, it's me," said Amelia.

She was right there next to Bal, elbow to elbow with him. She had not been there a second ago.

Viridia inhaled her bite of pizza and choked on it.

She coughed, and coughed harder, one hand flat on the wood. Bal half rose to go around and pound her on the back, but she waved him off. At last she grabbed her cup and gulped water until she could breathe again. Her eyes were streaming.

"Wait," she croaked. "What? I don't understand. Lady Amelia, you're his familiar? What does that even mean?"

In answer, Amelia lifted her black bangs off her forehead with two fingers.

The brand was there, a small blood sygl matching the one in Bal's hand, glowing a soft red against her pale skin. Viridia stared at it.

"But how is that possible?"

Amelia let her bangs fall. She helped herself to a slice of Bal's pizza, and explained between bites.

"As an Immortal, and a vampire, I'm technically a magical creature," she said. "That's why the bonding was able to work."

"Yeah, but bonded creatures always want something in return." Viridia was still staring. "What could he have that you'd want?"

Amelia stopped chewing.

She left the slice on the dish, and when she looked at Viridia her red eyes were perfectly level.

"Don't ruin the good impression I've gotten of you so far," Amelia said. "Insulting him is the one thing that's liable to set me off."

Viridia opened her mouth, and shut it again. Color came up in her cheeks.

"No, no," she said, both hands up and waving. "I didn't mean it like that. I wasn't being disrespectful. I only meant, you're Immortal. What could a mortal offer you that you couldn't get for yourself?"

Amelia studied the pizza.

"You'd be surprised," she murmured.

Bal felt it was about time somebody changed the subject.

"This is our big secret until class," he told Viridia. "You've just gotten a preview. Imagine how the rest of the class is going to react."

Viridia burst out laughing, and some of the stiffness went out of her shoulders.

"Yeah, that's going to be hilarious," she said. "They're all going to have these lame little familiars, and you're going to have a beautiful vampire familiar."

Amelia's head came up.

"You think I'm beautiful?" she asked.

"Of course," Viridia said, perfectly straight-faced. "I'd die to have skin like yours."

Amelia only looked at her. Viridia looked back and didn't blink. A Waves boy at the next table over had stopped with his fork in the air and was watching them, and nobody at the Blood table paid him any attention.

Amelia's mouth twitched first. Then she put her head back and laughed, loud enough that half the hall turned around.

"Okay," she told Bal. "You can keep her."

She swallowed the last bite of her pizza and slid out from the bench.

"I'll be in the dorm when you're done hanging out, and we can go over the stuff we didn't get to earlier. Have fun, you two!"

There was a soft pop, and Amelia's place on the bench was empty.

Bal and Viridia looked at one another across the pizza.

"What did she mean," Viridia asked, "you can keep me?"

Bal shrugged.

"Honestly?" he said. "I have no idea."

---

They stayed and talked a while longer, until the hall had mostly emptied around them and the silver dish held only crusts. Then Viridia climbed off the bench and stood.

"I should get back to my dorm," she said. She waved goodbye from the end of the table and headed off down the hall, and out through the tall doors.

Bal sat on at the empty table. Then he headed back to his dorm.

There were still students milling around in the courtyard, and more of them in the corridors, standing in their groups. Bal walked through them with his hands in his pockets. A boy in a Terra sweater looked at the sygl on his chest and then up at his face, and kept on looking. Farther on, an older boy with his arms folded watched him the whole length of the corridor, flat and unfriendly.

No one said anything. No one bothered him.

He went down the narrow stairs, and the noise of the school fell away behind him. The paintings went by in their heavy frames. At the end of the corridor the pedestal stood waiting, the blood sygl carved into its top.

Amelia was behind that wall. So was whatever she had put off telling him about the High Priest thing.

Bal pressed his hand to the stone and felt it prick.
