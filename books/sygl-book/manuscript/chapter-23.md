# Chapter Twenty-Three: Road to Londra

Amelia saw them off at noon. Fern's grooms had the horses waiting at the canyon gate, saddled and loaded, and she hugged Lora first and then Viridia and said something to each of them too low for Bal to catch. Then it was his turn. She held on to him longer than she had held either of them, and when she let go her fingers stayed on his collar a moment more, and straightened it, though it had been straight.

Hopper stayed behind with her. A bloodwolf at Viridia's heel would have given the whole disguise away at the first gate.

Amelia stayed out on the road while they rode off. When Bal twisted around in the saddle at the bend, she was still there.

"Everyone else is beneath you," Lora said. "Start there. It's how most nobles feel, so it's how you have to feel. Everyone."

They had been riding an hour, three abreast where the road allowed it. Viridia sat in the middle in the clothes the priests had found for her, a gray riding dress with a high collar and more buttons than any one person needed. She had unfastened two of them before the canyon had dropped behind them.

"Everyone," she said. "The guards."

"The guards. The innkeeper. The man who sells you a ribbon. Us. Especially us. A lady doesn't thank her servants and she doesn't explain herself to them. She doesn't look at them at all unless she wants something."

"Not looking at Bal? I'm a natural."

"You're not. You lean in when people talk. Don't. Let them lean in to you." Lora's voice had none of its usual hedging. "And don't say please. You can say thank you to someone important, once, and then never again. Short sentences. If you ask a question, ask it like you already know the answer and you're checking whether they do."

Viridia raised her chin and peered down her nose at Bal from the height of a horse that was a hand shorter than his.

"Water."

Bal handed her his water skin. She drank and handed it back in silence.

"Better," Lora said.

Viridia ruined it by grinning at him.

"And do your buttons up," Lora said.

Bal rode with slack reins and listened to them. Lora went on to forks, and which ones, and Viridia asked why anyone needed three. Now and then he squinted up at the sun. They would have to push the horses soon if they meant to make Londra in time. He gave them another mile. Lora was enjoying herself, and Viridia was learning more than she let on.

The goodbye at the gate was still with him. It had gone straight through him. Since the mausoleum he had been fond of her, and amazed by her, and on one or two occasions a little afraid of her. Somewhere in these past weeks it had grown into more, and he had missed it happening until she was standing in the road getting smaller. He was falling in love with the Vampire Goddess.

Then there were these two. Lora was demonstrating how to hold a cup, with her pinky out, on a cup that wasn't there. Viridia was copying her badly on purpose. What he felt for them grew every day too. He was amazed at his luck, constantly. One beautiful woman who wanted to be with him would have been plenty for an orphan from Saint Andre's. He had found three.

He only hoped he could keep them safe. The plan was dangerous, and he had no illusions about everything going off perfectly. He had not seen any other way. And he was not about to trust their safety to anyone else.

---

The plan was to stay clear of every town until Londra. Most of the fighting was in the towns along the border, so they would be living on what Commander Fern had ordered packed for them: hard biscuit, dried meat, a wheel of cheese wrapped in waxed cloth, and oats for the horses.

When he judged that Lora had had long enough, Bal told them to quicken the pace, and he kicked his own horse into a gallop.

They held it until the horses had had enough. Then they slowed to a walk to let them blow, and then they galloped again. The country rolled past in long yellow stretches of grass, with a farm far off now and then and a line of trees along a stream. It was hot and the cheese went soft. At night they made no fire.

---

They met no one on the road until the second day.

Up ahead, two people on horses were coming toward them, with two more on foot. When they were close enough, Bal saw that the riders were children: a boy on one horse and a girl on the other, too small for the saddles, their feet nowhere near the stirrups. A man and a woman walked beside them, one at each bridle. Their parents, Bal guessed.

The parents went stiff at the sight of Viridia's hair. Bal raised a hand and called out to them, and they stopped anyway. They stopped the way people do when it would be worse not to.

Flies were bad on that stretch. The girl was swatting at them, and then the boy, and each time a small hand came up Bal saw the mark on its palm. Blood sygls. Both of them.

Bal's own left hand had been born with that mark on it. Somebody had taken the hand off for it.

He could guess the rest.

"Any news from up ahead?" Bal asked. "What are things like?"

The father spat on the ground.

It landed in the dust between his boots and the hooves of Bal's horse. His eyes never left Viridia.

"Same as everywhere else," he said. "We got run out of our town a few days back. Just trying to make it to Kysmer. That's the closest safe place for blood mages, around here."

He took his son's reins and started the horse walking. The mother went after him with the girl, and none of them said another word. The boy swatted at another fly.

Bal sat his horse until they were well down the road behind him. Kysmer was days off for children that size. Viridia kept her face to the front. Lora kept quiet.

He could not help them. Bal clicked his tongue to his horse and rode on.

---

It went on like that for the next two days. They kept meeting people on the road, fleeing the fighting, looking for some kind of peace in the other territories. Families with carts. A man on his own with a pack on his back. Some had been hurt. He passed a woman with her arm bound up in a sling made of a shirt, and a boy not much younger than him with a bandage around his head that had bled through and dried brown.

Normally, when one originator fought another, ordinary people were left out of it. Soldiers fought soldiers, and the farms went on farming. Bal had known that Raizen was doing something different. He had read Thad's reports. He had not understood until now how many people that something reached. Nobody had written down the woman in the sling.

They all saw Viridia's hair first. Then they found something to study in the road.

---

They reached Londra on the fifth day.

It sat behind a high stone wall, with a stone gate set in the middle of it. A guard stood at either side of the gate, waving people through. There was a short line of carts and walkers, and the dust they raised hung in the heat.

Lora and Bal dropped back together and let Viridia take the lead. Bal rounded his shoulders and tried to take up less of the road. He hoped he looked enough like a servant. He had seen plenty of servants. He had never paid attention to how they stood.

The guard on the left put out an arm to stop them. He was a big man, bigger around the middle than in the arms, but he held a heavy black truncheon in one hand, and from his grip on it he had used it before.

"What's your business in Londra?" he said. It was a nasal voice, pitched high for a man that size.

Viridia gave him the kind of look Lora had been drilling into her for five days.

"I am on my way to my family in Pashar," she said. "We shall rest here a few days before we go on. I am to meet my father in Pashar this coming week, and I should like to buy him and my mother a gift while I am here."

The other guard had not been listening. He was younger, and he had been gazing at her hair since they rode up, with something close to awe. Now he stepped over to her horse to inspect her eyes.

Her hair began to crackle.

It lifted off her shoulders at the ends, a strand at a time. Small blue sparks ran along it and snapped out. It did that when she was nervous.

"I've never seen that gift," the young guard said. "It's really rare."

Viridia threw her head back.

"Of course it is. Now, may we pass? I am tired and I would like to rest." She flicked her fingers back at the road they had come by. "After passing filthy blood mages on the roads, I'll be glad to be among my own people."

Bal held his tongue, as a servant would.

"Of course," the guard with the black truncheon said, and waved them on.

Viridia started forward, then pulled up short. Something else had occurred to her, or she wanted it to look that way.

"Could you give me directions to the Three Boars Inn? I usually stay at the Winespring when I'm here, but I hear their wine is exceptional. I should like to try it."

"Certainly, my lady," the big guard said, quick and eager now. "Head up to North Street, then take the third street on your right. You'll see their sign hanging off the building."

Viridia thanked him, the once Lora had allowed her, and slapped the reins on her horse's neck.

---

Bal and Lora followed her through the gate in silence.

The streets inside were crowded. They threaded their way through people on foot and people with handcarts, past a woman selling onions off a blanket and a boy chasing a goose that had other plans. People nudged each other wherever Viridia passed, a good many of them. A man on a doorstep forgot the pipe in his hand until it went out. She rode through it with her chin up and her buttons done.

Bal brought his horse over beside Lora's and spoke under the street noise.

"Exactly how rare is Viridia's gift?"

Lora had noticed the stares too.

"Honestly? No idea. She doesn't really talk about it." She frowned. "From what I've read, her powers are a lesser version of Raizen's. How much weaker, I couldn't tell you. The only time I've seen her use it was at that lake. When she shot lightning out of her hand, straight down beside that big snake." She paused. "That poor snake never stood a chance."

Bal remembered. He could still smell it.

"And she's still got her lightning," Lora went on, "and now she's got her blood magic on top of it. She's one scary lady."

Ahead of them, Viridia turned her horse off the main street. North Street, Bal assumed, until she went straight past the third turning on the right. Soon after that she drew up at a stable yard attached to a long two-story building with its shutters thrown open. The painted board over the door said WINESPRING INN.

Bal glanced at Lora. So that was why Viridia had asked the guard the way to the Three Boars: Xavier had put the Winespring just past it. Lora lifted her eyebrows back at him and offered nothing.

The inn's stable boy had been leaning in the shade of the yard wall. He came over at a run, and stood ready at Viridia's stirrup while she got herself down out of the saddle in all that gray skirt.

"M'Lady," he said. "We're happy to see you. I hope your travels have been pleasant."

He reached for her reins.

"We were told to expect you."
