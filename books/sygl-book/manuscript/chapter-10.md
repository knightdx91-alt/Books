Bal hurried down to lunch with the rune kit tucked against his ribs, and he spotted Viridia before he was properly through the doors.

She had their table to herself and was writing on a scrap of paper. She finished, laid the paper on her plate, and the plate went away and returned loaded. Chicken nuggets, a heap of them, and a pile of french fries, with a paper cup of ketchup tucked in at the edge.

So she had made it back. All ten fingers, by the look of her from the door, and no sign that anything had chewed on her.

Bal swung his legs over the bench across from her and parked the kit on the bench beside him. This time he had come with paper. He tore the blank bottom off his page of rune notes and borrowed her pencil. Chicken nuggets and french fries looked like the right idea, so he wrote that and put the strip down in front of him. His plate went. It returned with the same as hers, cup of ketchup and all.

The first nugget was too hot and he ate it anyway.

"Lady Amelia really shook up the class," Viridia said, dragging a fry through her ketchup. "No one expected that."

Bal chuckled. "I'd have been surprised if they had. What were they saying?"

"Some of the guys were making the usual comments." Viridia rolled her eyes. "How it's hot, you having a beautiful woman at your beck and call who calls you Master." She bit the fry in half. "But quite a few of them think it's kind of cool, you having that kind of connection with your originator. None of us are ever going to have that."

She gave that a second to settle.

"I do think Lady Amelia is the coolest, though."

"I agree," Bal said. "What makes you say that?"

Viridia reached for the salt shaker and salted her remaining nuggets. She was generous about it. When she was done she went back over the ones at the bottom of the pile that she had missed, until the whole pile glittered.

"You know what a bloodwolf is, right?"

Bal had to think. He had read about them once, in a bestiary, long enough ago that he needed a minute to find the page again.

It returned to him a piece at a time. Bloodwolves were rare. They had very few pups, and their pelts were tough enough to turn the edge of a longsword, so anyone who had seen one at all had something to brag about for years. Bonding one was something else altogether. Their fur was a light silver, with blood red laid across the top of it, along the back and over the shoulders. They were also quite a bit larger than an ordinary wolf.

"Yeah," he said slowly. "Are you saying you saw one?"

Viridia pushed the shaker back to the middle of the table and dropped her voice.

"So after Porter let us out," she said, "everybody went charging off into the trees in one big clump, and I hung back. I was standing there trying to decide which way to go. And then Lady Amelia was there. Not right next to me. A little way behind. She had to say my name twice before I turned around."

"I thought she had errands."

"Apparently I was one." Viridia waved the next nugget at him. "Anyway. We walked, and we talked for a while. Once I'd gotten used to her a bit, she asked me whether I wanted to show up everybody else."

"What did you say?"

"I laughed. I told her I didn't think anybody was going to top your performance."

Bal thought about Mr. Porter's face going from purple to nothing at all, and decided that was fair.

"She said that was fair too," Viridia said. "And then she asked me again. Would I like to show everyone else up." "By then I was curious where it was going. So I said yes, I would. She told me to give her my ring and follow her."

"Where to?"

"Uphill, mostly. It was a good thirty minutes. We came out where the forest runs up against the side of the mountain, and there was a cave there. A big one. You could have driven a buggy into it." She tapped the table in front of her plate. "She told me to wait right there. Then she walked into the cave by herself, with my ring."

"And you waited."

"I waited. Not long. Long enough that I started working out what I was going to tell Porter." Viridia's eyes had gone somewhere past his shoulder, and she was smiling at whatever was there. "Then she came walking out again, and there was a bloodwolf walking behind her."

Bal forgot all about the nugget in his hand.

"It's not full grown," Viridia said. "Look at the paws, though. She's going to be huge. Her paws are already bigger than my hands." She held one up, fingers spread. "She sat down a ways off from me. Lady Amelia came over on her own, and she said, 'The bloodwolf's name is Hopper. She'll agree to a bond with you. All she requires is bloody meat to eat, and the possibility of being around me.'"

"Hopper," Bal said.

"Hopper."

"Around Amelia?"

"That's what I said. The meat part I understood. The other part, I just gaped at her, so she explained it." Viridia talked with her hands now, building it in the air over the nuggets. "The bloodwolves are partly hers. She made them. She took a bunch of big plains wolves and gave them her blood, and that's where the breed came from. They don't need blood like a vampire does. It helps them grow, though. And they look at her as a mother. Sort of." She dropped her hands. "So being near Lady Amelia now and then is a big deal to Hopper. It's part of what makes the bond balanced."

Bal leaned away from his plate.

Amelia wanted him to convert Viridia. She had said so last night, by the fire. Now Viridia had a familiar who would want to be near Amelia while the bond lasted, and Amelia would be wherever Bal was. Which meant Viridia would be too, for the time being, and she had walked back through those trees without the faintest idea of it.

He was impressed. A whole morning of errands, and somewhere in the middle of them Amelia had gone up a mountain and found Viridia the best present in the forest. It happened to come with a reason to keep visiting.

He had a feeling Viridia wouldn't mind, when she worked it out.

"You should have seen them," Viridia said, "when I walked out of the trees with her behind me. The whole class went dead quiet. One of the other Tempus kids said Raizen wasn't going to like it. Me having a bloodwolf for a familiar."

"What did you tell him?"

"Nothing. I laughed. Raizen doesn't pay any attention to his followers one way or the other. That's what the rest of them don't get. They all think if they do well enough, he'll notice them."

She pushed her plate away. A few salted nuggets were still on it.

"At least your originator talks to you," Viridia said. "She really is nice. I honestly didn't expect that. Unless somebody disrespects you, anyway." She laughed.

Bal shrugged, a little sheepishly. "I think she's protective. It's sweet."

"Oh, it is," Viridia agreed.

---

Their next class was Warfare. They left the hall, turned right, and went out through the big front doors into the afternoon. From there they walked around the outside of the castle to the back, where the training grounds were.

The grounds were wide and flat, with six training areas marked out on them in packed sand, each one roped off from the next. Racks of weapons stood along the edges of every one. Swords, mostly, and spears and axes, and a few things nobody had ever taught Bal the names of. The sun had been on the sand all morning, and the heat came up off it through the soles of his shoes.

In the middle of the nearest area a young man was working through sword forms by himself, waiting on the class to arrive. He was broad through the shoulders, with the same build and the same easy, settled stance as Naisura, and Bal would have believed it if someone had told him they were father and son. He had his shirt on, at least. That was one difference.

They arrived last of anyone. Everyone's eyes followed them across the sand as they went and joined the end of the line.

The young man finished his form, lowered his sword, and came to stand in front of them.

"In this class," he said, "you will learn to protect yourselves. My name is Zoran."

He didn't waste anything after that. He swept his free hand around at the racks.

"Take your time choosing a weapon. Pick each one up. Imagine using it. Take the one that speaks to you. Once you have one, come back to this line, and I'll pair you off to begin practicing. Then I'll take each of you through some exercises to get used to your new weapons."

He stepped back, and that was all the dismissal they got. The whole line broke at once and scrambled for the racks.

---

They took the last rack in the row, because nobody else was headed for it. Bal leaned his kit against the end post, where nothing would step on it.

A minute later a girl joined them there, a few pegs down.

She was shy-looking, with brown hair pulled back in a tail, and glasses that kept sliding down her nose no matter how many times she pushed them back up. She had freckles across the bridge of her nose, under the glasses. Bal noticed, idly, that the sygl on her sweater was Kenmei's. Nobody else in the class had come within a weapon's length of him. By now there had to be stories going around the castle about him, and whatever the stories were, she had heard them and come anyway.

"I'm Bal," he told her, reaching for a great axe while he talked. "This is Viridia."

The axe was massive. He got it free of its pegs and tested the weight of it at arm's length, and the head of it tipped toward the sand on its own. Bal was strong for his size. He was not strong enough to see himself swinging this thing at anyone. He hung it back up.

"L-Lora," the girl said. "Lora Aegis."

She had gone straight past the axes without looking at them. She took a rapier from its pegs, stepped out onto the sand, set her feet, and started an exercise with it.

It was brief and plain. Point out, a step forward, the blade turning over, a step back. Then the same again, mirrored. She did it without hurrying and without stopping, her glasses slipping a little further down with each lunge, and they did not trouble her.

Bal watched her go through it twice. He liked how she moved. Then he took the next rapier along and copied her.

The rapier was four and a half feet long, made of good steel, with a guard that curled around the back of his hand. It was much lighter than it looked. He set his feet where hers were and pushed the point out when she did. He drew it back when she did. While he copied her he imagined himself using it, as Zoran had said, and that came easily. The blade went where he sent it the moment he sent it there. By the third time through, the left hand Amelia had given him had quit gripping the hilt and started holding it, and nobody could have told him when that happened.

He liked it a lot.

At the end of her exercise, Lora noticed him. She studied his feet, which were where hers had been. Then his point, which was nearly there too.

"I, um. I've had practice," she said. "My father trained me. He was with Kenmei's army, when he was younger."

"That's amazing," Bal said, and meant it. "Would you mind partnering with me?"

Lora blinked at him.

Viridia, meanwhile, had pulled a spear off the next rack over and was holding it upright beside her, looking it up and down. She thumped the butt of it on the sand once, and kept it.

"If you're not put off by all the rumors about my originator," Bal added, as the three of them started back toward the line in front of Zoran.

"No, it doesn't bother me," Lora said, very quickly. "I'm just glad to have someone to practice with."

Bal was about to ask why on earth anyone wouldn't have practiced with her. Zoran got there first, sorting the line into pairs as fast as he could point. He took one look at Lora's grip and one look at Bal's, and put them together. Viridia went off to the neighboring sand with a Terra boy.

"You," Zoran said to Bal. "Guard and parry. You'll learn the forms." He turned to Lora. "You've used a rapier before."

"Yes, sir."

"Then you attack him. Slowly."

Zoran showed Bal the guards. There were only a few, high and low and out to the sides, and all of them returned to the same middle position, point up, elbow in. Bal learned them standing still. Then Zoran went on to the next pair, and Lora came at him.

Slowly, as she'd been told. The point came toward his chest. Bal moved his blade across and met it, and steel slid on steel, and her point went past him to the left.

Again. High. He was late. She stopped her point a hand's width from his collar and waited until he had it, then drew back.

Again. Low. He had that one.

Again.

Again.

Fifteen minutes in, his forearm was burning. Bal kept the point up anyway, and after a while it shook, and he kept it up shaking. Lora did not speed up and did not slow down. Each time she came in, she came in the same, until his blade knew where to meet hers before he had finished thinking about it.

They didn't talk much. There wasn't much room for it. Between rounds, while Zoran was busy with someone else, Bal learned a little. Lora was usually quiet. She mostly kept to herself.

"I like books better than people," she said, during one of the breaks, and then went red. "Mostly. Not you. I mean, I don't know you. I mean..." She stopped and started over. "Books don't mind if you don't say anything."

"Then we'll get along fine," Bal said. "Quiet's okay with me."

She peered at him above the rims of her glasses for a second. Then she raised her rapier, and he raised his.

---

When Zoran finally called time, Bal's arm felt about twice its proper weight. He hung the rapier back on its pegs, third from the left, so he would know which one it was next time. Lora's went up beside it. Then he collected his rune kit from the foot of the post.

"You can sit with Viridia and me," Bal said, "whenever we eat. If you want to."

Lora wiped her palms down the front of her sweater.

"I'll think about it," she said.

She went off around the opposite corner of the castle, by herself, walking fast. She didn't look back. Bal watched her go until the corner of the wall took her.

Viridia came up beside him, swinging her arms. The spear was back on its rack. She had sand on one knee, which bothered her not at all.

"That Terra boy," she said, "kept trying to knock my spear out of my hands. The whole time. He's built like a door. I hit him in the shins twice." She considered. "Three times. He said the third shouldn't count."

Bal let her tell him all of it while they walked. They walked around the castle wall, headed for their last class of the day, and stepped out into the courtyard by the stage.

Amelia was there, waiting for them.

She sat on the stage's edge with her palms flat on the stone, kicking her legs back and forth over the drop. The long dress was gone.

She was wearing a school uniform.
