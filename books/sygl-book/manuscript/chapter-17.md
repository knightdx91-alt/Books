# Chapter Seventeen: Saegen Woods

The pass was full of gravel.

They reached it about an hour after they broke camp. High gray stone rose on both sides, and over the years it had shed itself onto the floor of the gap, loose rubble with sharp pieces mixed through it that had sheared off the cliffs clean and kept their edges. They had to pick their way. Viridia used the butt of her spear as a walking stick. Hopper ranged ahead and trotted back to them, over and over, and did not put a paw wrong from one end to the other.

It was not a long pass, half a mile or so. The gravel crunched and slid under their boots until the walls fell back, and they stood at the crest of a hill. A worn path ran down it to the woods below.

The woods were very green and very thick. From where they stood, the treetops looked solid enough to walk on.

Bal checked the map.

"Well, this is it," he said. "Where did Ms. Summers say the flowers were, in there?"

Lora unbuttoned her breast pocket and unfolded the receipt. It had gone soft at the folds from riding against her overnight. She read it over, then raised her head to study the forest.

"There." She pointed east. "Do you see the river? She told me about five hundred feet in, on the bank."

He found it, a bright thread through the green. They could go around and cut straight in from the east, which looked shorter. Or they could follow the river right to the spot. They needed to fill the water skins anyway.

"River," Bal decided.

---

Going down the hill, he asked Lora about the flowers. She had the alchemy book, after all, and he wanted her guess at what Ms. Summers might use them for.

Lora had read the entry more than once. She didn't stop to think.

"Aurora felis is rare," she said. "It goes into healing potions, mostly. The flowers are blood red and yellow, and they only bloom every three years, so you have to get them soon after, or you've missed it. That's part of why she wanted to hurry and send somebody, instead of waiting until she felt better." She stepped over a root without breaking stride. "And once they're cut, you have seventy-two hours. They have to be prepared and used inside that, or they start losing their potency."

"So anything made with them costs a fortune," Viridia said.

"Pretty much. The flowers are rare, and what goes in with them isn't cheap either." Lora hesitated. "She offered to help me make two of the potions. As part of the payment."

None of them needed the money for anything in particular, except the guild dues. They had agreed to it back in New Spring without much of a discussion.

She was already halfway into that shop in her head. She had bought plants and supplies to stock the herb room Bal had put in for her, and now she was going to watch a real master work from start to finish.

---

The river was quick and clear and not very deep. Brightly colored fish held against the current in it, with the sun bouncing off their scales.

They stopped first to fill the water skins. It made Bal's teeth ache and tasted faintly of stone. Viridia drank straight from her cupped hands, and Hopper waded in up to her ankles and drank more than all of them together. Then they turned upstream and followed the river in under the branches.

"I'm surprised they're normal size," Viridia said as they walked. "I figured, with the ley line, they'd be bigger."

"It's over that way." Amelia pointed off to the west. "So the fish wouldn't have been touched by it. With beasts, and even with vampires, you can take some of another creature's magykal energy by consuming it. If a creature kept killing and eating its prey, it could get quite large indeed."

Somewhere out there in the west, then, something had eaten something that had eaten something, for generations.

"Fortunately," Amelia added, "there's nothing in these woods that can't be killed. The closest thing would be Hopper here, with that pelt of hers."

Hopper heard her name and trotted over in case she was wanted.

---

It was not long before they found the spot. On the bank of the river, among the tall grass, grew six of the red and yellow flowers.

They were smaller than Bal had pictured. Each sat at the top of a single stem, red at the heart and yellow at the edges.

Six meant Lora could keep three for herself, once the other three went to Ms. Summers.

"Why didn't Ms. Summers tell us to bring back all of them?" Amelia asked.

Lora tucked the receipt into her pocket and got down on her heels in front of the flowers with her knife. She cut each stem low, carefully, and eased each flower into its own glass vial. The vials had ridden from the castle in her pack, wrapped in cloth.

"She only has the ingredients to make three. So it was no use being greedy." Lora pushed the cork into the last vial and stood up. "One potion is worth what she'd make in a month selling the others. I think the only reason she offered us two is that I'm interested in alchemy. Her face lit up when I started asking her questions."

She held the vials, all six, in the crook of her arm.

"She's lonely, I think," Lora said.

He gave her his back so she could stow the padded vials in his pack. He felt her working at the buckles, and the small weight settling in.

"I'm surprised she doesn't have an apprentice or something," he said, and led them out toward the tree line, still following the river. "An alchemist is important. If something happened to her, the whole town would suffer."

Nobody answered that. The water went on talking to itself beside them. Bal noticed, after a while, that it was the only voice left. The birds had stopped, and he had missed them going.

He drew a breath to ask if the others had noticed.

---

Something big and furry hit him from the side.

He went off his feet and clear over the water. The water went by underneath him. A tree stopped him, all in one blow, and his head snapped back against the bark.

For a while the world was green and full of bells.

He got one knee under him. Then the other foot. The tree helped. Across the water somebody was shouting his name, Lora, he thought, and the ground kept tipping to throw him down again, and his hand went hunting for his rapier and could not lay hands on the hilt.

He was not really hurt. The thing that had hit him was.

When his eyes would focus again, it was over, and he had to put it together from what was in front of him. Amelia had gone after it the moment he was hit. Its claws had done nothing to her. It had tried to run, and she had not allowed it. Now she had both arms locked around its massive neck, and she was squeezing, and it was not moving anymore. Above its head her face was white and set with fury. She looked as if she would have liked to kill it a second time.

It was a bear. He saw that once she let it drop into the grass. It was the biggest bear he had ever heard of, let alone seen.

Amelia came straight to him. She got an arm around him and lifted, and carried him back to the other bank, and his boots never touched the water. He was glad. He was still woozy, and he did not much want to try swimming.

---

On their own bank the girls went over him. Viridia tipped his chin up and found the knot coming up under his hair. Lora felt down his arms and asked how many fingers she was holding up, and accepted three, though he suspected it had been two. The bandages from yesterday had held. His back had something to say about the tree, but it had been complaining since the road.

Next they checked the vials.

They were fine, all six. Lora had put them in the side pocket of his pack. If she had put them in the middle, between him and that tree, no amount of padding would have saved them.

"Side pocket," Lora said, a little faintly, and pressed her fingers to her mouth.

Amelia kept sending glares over the water at the body in the grass.

"Uh. Amelia," Bal said.

She turned. "Yes?"

Her voice had lost its playfulness. Her head was in the fight, he figured.

"Um. I know the bear couldn't hurt you," he said. "But it did manage to hurt your clothes."

Amelia's face went blank.

He waved a hand at his own chest.

She checked her front, and spun her back on all of them in one motion. The bear had torn her dress open high on the bodice. Both girls had seen it already, while they were fussing over him, and had kept quiet about it, which Bal found interesting.

Amelia fixed her dress. Her hands worked fast at it, and when she had it held together she walked straight past him, heading out of the woods. Her cheeks were flaming red.

They followed her out. She walked by herself, well ahead, and none of them tried to catch up.

---

At the pass, Bal left Lora and Viridia and lengthened his stride until he was walking beside Amelia.

"Thanks for taking care of the bear," he said, as the walls rose around them. "I hate to think what would have happened if Viridia and Lora had had to fight it."

"I honestly never heard it," Amelia told him. "I was listening to Lora when you got hit. He must have been very old to grow that big."

Bal bent and scooped up some stones. They were good ones, round and smooth, egg-sized.

"At least it couldn't hurt you," he said lightly. "You looked pretty angry."

He tossed the first stone up, and the second after it, and then the third, and caught them coming down, and threw them again. He had learned it as a boy. His hands had not forgotten how. The stones rose and fell in a little wheel in front of his face.

"I think you'd have killed it again if you could," he said.

"You have no idea. Seeing you hit that tree, I snapped. It had tried to run." She smiled, grimly. "But it didn't get far."

They walked. The stones climbed and dropped into his palms, slap, slap, slap, and climbed again.

Amelia watched him juggle for a stretch of the pass.

"So you like to juggle, huh?" she asked.

Her glance flicked past his shoulder, to the girls behind them, and returned to him.

Bal was sure she wasn't talking about the rocks.

He let the stones fall. They clattered onto the gravel and lay among all the others, and in a moment he could no longer tell which had been his.

"It's not something I planned on doing," he said. "And if the ones being juggled had a problem with it, I'd hope they would tell me."

He walked on a little. Amelia waited.

"I would also hope," he said, "that each one knows how much they matter to me. And that I do not feel more strongly for one over the other."

Gravel crunched behind them, close.

When he turned, Lora and Viridia were nearer than he had thought, only a couple of paces off. Viridia said something to Lora, too low to hear, and the two of them slowed and let the distance open up again.

Amelia said nothing. She walked with her arms folded, thinking about what he had told her, and he let her think.

Bal ran through it again in his own head. They left the pass and went on along the road, and a few miles went by in quiet. Behind them Lora and Viridia were talking in low voices, with Hopper trotting happily at their side.

---

They had decided to walk straight back instead of camping a second night. It was an uneventful walk. They came through Whitehaven in the late afternoon, and at its town limit Viridia touched Hopper's collar, and Hopper shrank back down to fit the streets.

It was full dark when they reached New Spring. They went straight up the square to the guild hall to turn in the flowers.

The windows were lit. Dawn members came and went at all hours, so the Hall never closed. Soren was on duty, which was a relief. She had helped them on their first visit.

Lora unbuckled the side pocket of Bal's pack, and three of the vials went onto the counter in a row. Soren drew them over to her side and began counting out the reward.

"Oh. Um. The reward's changed a little," Lora told her. "Ms. Summers is paying us partly in potions."

"Thank you for letting me know." Soren made a few notes in the ledger on her desk, in that even hand, and counted again. This time the amount came out right, minus what she held back for their guild dues.

---

Dinner at the castle had been over for hours. They bought steaming bowls of stew at a restaurant on the square and ate at a table by the window, and Bal had a second bowl, because the first had disappeared without his noticing.

It was well after curfew when they reached the castle, and every hall was quiet. In the entrance hall Lora hugged Amelia, and then Bal, holding her own three vials out to one side in their cloth to keep them safe. Viridia hugged them both after her, quick and hard. The two of them went off toward their dorms together, talking under their breath, and their footsteps faded down the corridor.

Curfew had no hold on a Lead Mage.

Bal walked Amelia home to the Blood dorm, and he walked slowly. The castle was dark around them. They did not talk. Back in the pass the girls had been closer behind him than he'd thought, and he had no way of knowing how much of it they had heard.
