# Chapter Twelve: I Like You Too

Lora was the first to see him come in.

She sat about halfway along the Waves table with a good length of empty bench to her left and her right. The rest of her table had bunched up at the window end, all talking over one another, and the gap between them and Lora was wide enough for three more people. When Bal came in, her head came up. She found him through the crowd and smiled, quick and shy, a smile that was gone nearly as soon as it started. Then she pushed her glasses back up her nose and returned to her food.

Bal smiled back. She didn't see it.

*That's someone else who needs a little time,* he thought.

He crossed to the last table, his own, as empty as ever except for one place across from his, where Viridia was working a knife through a steak.

He'd brought paper this time, a folded sheet in his pocket. He wrote out eggs, toast, bacon and oatmeal, the same breakfast he'd had that morning, and dropped the sheet onto the silver. It went, and returned full and steaming.

Viridia eyed it. Then the window, where the sky had gone orange, then his plate again. She let it go.

The bench on his side was empty from end to end. Yesterday Amelia had appeared from nowhere in the place at his elbow and eaten off his plate. Tonight she was back in the dorm, or off in the mountains moving people into a canyon, and the place at his elbow stayed a plain stretch of wood.

Viridia let him get started on his eggs first. Then she jerked her chin at Lora's table.

"She saw you," she said. "I went over and asked her again, before you got here. Whether she wanted to sit with us."

"And?"

"She said no." Viridia sawed off a corner of steak. "She was nice about it."

"She's really shy," Bal said. "She'll need a while to open up, that's all."

And there was the table itself. Nobody ever sat at it but Bal and one Tempus girl choosing to sit with him, and the whole school could see it from every bench in the hall. Anyone who sat down here would be choosing a side. It would make her a target.

Bal stopped with a forkful of eggs in the air.

"The others haven't tried anything with you," he said, "have they?"

Viridia shrugged.

"Not really. A lot of snide comments, so far. Nothing physical." She put a bite in her mouth and talked around it. "Some of the Tempus kids won't talk to me anymore." Her mouth curled up at one side and stayed that way. "It's funny they think I care. Some people have been coming up and asking about you, though. Some from other sygls, even."

Bal got his oatmeal down before he answered.

"Asking about me? Why?"

Viridia looked at him like he'd lost his mind.

"Bal, not only are you a High Priest, you've got your originator for a familiar. You can talk to her every day, any time you want. Nobody else has anything close to that. The most the rest of us get is once a year, when school starts." She pointed her knife at him. "They want to know what it's like."

"Then why don't they come and ask me?"

Viridia laughed at him.

"Because Amelia's made it pretty obvious she favors you, and nobody wants to piss her off. And if they got too interested in you, other people might start treating them how my fellow Tempus students treat me." She returned to her steak. "You'll just have to give them time."

It was hard to argue with her when she was right.

Over at the Terra table a big boy had balanced a bread roll on top of his water cup and was trying to balance a second roll on top of the first. Two of his friends were holding their breath for him. The second roll went into a girl's soup, and the friends cheered anyway.

"I'm trying to get Amelia to start eating with us," Bal said. "It'd be good for her. And if people saw her in here having dinner with everybody else, it might take some of the mystery away. Oh, and so you're not caught off guard. Amelia may or may not have read your diary this afternoon."

Viridia had her cup up, in the middle of a long drink.

It went down wrong. She jerked the cup down so fast that half of it slopped over the rim and across her steak, and then she was coughing into her elbow, folded sideways on the bench with her free hand groping at the air. Bal passed her his napkin. She snatched it out of his fingers and coughed into that instead.

"She did *what*?" she managed, once the coughing let go of her. Her voice came out thin and wet.

Her face showed nerves and nothing else. Amelia hadn't been teasing him on that bed. She had been telling the truth.

Bal's face would not stay straight.

"Yeah," he said. "I was off doing some priest stuff, and when I got back, she was lying there reading your diary."

"How'd you know it was mine?"

The color was already rising in her cheeks.

"You're really cute when you blush." He meant every word of it, and it only turned her pinker. "Don't worry. All I saw was your name on the front, and she told me what it was. Your secrets are safe with her."

Viridia let out a small noise in her throat and went to work on the steak. She cut the piece in front of her in half, and then cut the halves smaller, and then smaller again, and didn't eat any of it. The knife went on squeaking against the silver. She had made it down to crumbs and was still keeping her eyes on them.

He could have let it go there. He didn't.

"And Viridia?"

He waited. It took her a while. Her eyes lifted off the plate, slowly, and found his.

"I like you too."

Her fork dropped.

It hit the edge of the plate, rang off the silver, and went over the side of the table. Bal heard it land on the stone under the bench, two little bounces and then nothing. Viridia's hand stayed in the air where the fork had been.

"But you," she began. "But you *said* she only told you it was my diary!" She said it so loud the Waves boy two tables down glanced over, and her whole face had gone red to the hairline.

Bal took the hand that had dropped the fork.

"Hey," he said. "It's okay. That honestly is all she told me. I promise. And I'm glad she did, because otherwise I might never have worked up the courage to tell you I like you."

Viridia wiped her eyes with her other hand.

"That was still mean."

"I'll find some way to make it up to you," he promised.

"You better," she muttered.

She kept her hand where it was, inside his, on the table between the oatmeal and the ruined steak, and neither of them went under the bench after the fork.

---

The hall cleared a table at a time. Outside the high windows the orange drained from the sky, and the lamps along the walls were lit. Benches scraped. Plates vanished in front of people as they stood. The Terra boys left in a crowd, one of them with a bread roll in each pocket. At some point Lora's bench emptied too, and Bal never saw Lora go. Viridia talked about nothing much, her spear partner's shins, the steak, and he answered her, and afterward not one word of it stayed with him.

He walked back to the dorm with his right hand still warm. He could have taken the stairs three at a time. Maybe he did. He didn't remember them. He remembered lying down in the dark, and not much after that.

It was barely light when something started pounding on the wall of his dorm.
