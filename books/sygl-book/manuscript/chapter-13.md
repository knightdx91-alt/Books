# Chapter Thirteen: New Spring

The whole wall shook with it. In the next room the fire irons chattered in their stand, and grit pattered down on the canopy.

Bal sat up with his heart going.

Amelia hadn't moved. She was curled on her side next to him with her black hair spread over two pillows and one hand tucked under her cheek. When she had come in, he could not say. The embroidered rose was bunched up under her chin.

The pounding came again. It was not a polite sort of knock. Somebody had made a fist and meant it.

*Please don't wake up,* Bal thought at her, and eased out from under the blanket by inches. The floor was cold. He got the door shut behind him without a click and hurried down the steps and put his hand flat to the wall at the bottom. It slid aside.

Viridia stood in the corridor with her fist still up. Lora was behind her, half a step back.

They were not in uniform. Viridia had on a blue skirt that had been let down at the hem at least once, and a laced vest over a white blouse. Lora wore brown trousers and a brown jacket with patches at both elbows, and her hair was loose for once, down past her shoulders. Neither said a thing. They stared at him, the pair of them, and their faces were going a slow and steady pink.

Bal followed their eyes down.

Boxers. He was in his boxers. Only his boxers, and the corridor was cold too.

"Come in," he said, very fast, and waved them up the steps past him. "Sit down. Anywhere. I'll be right back."

He made it into the bedroom before either of them reached the top. Amelia was still asleep. He found his uniform where he had left it over the chest and got into it the quickest he had ever gotten into anything, the sweater on backward the first time, and reappeared with his hair still flattened from the pillow.

They were standing at the hearth, still pink.

"Sorry," Bal said. "About how I answered the door. I wasn't really awake yet."

They all started in at once. Bal put up a hand and stopped, and waved for them to go first.

"Pretty sure nobody's allowed in other people's dorms," Viridia said, turning slowly to take in the room. "It must get lonely in here. With it only being you."

"Yeah. It's pretty big," Bal said. He caught her eye. "It'd be nice to have other people around."

Viridia found something on the mantel that needed her full attention.

"So what's all this?" he asked, and pointed at their clothes.

---

"Well, the thing is," Lora said, "all the teachers got pulled by the originators today."

She tucked her hair behind her ear, and then her hand stayed up by her ear, as if it was not sure where else to go. He guessed she was not used to being one of a group of anything.

"Apparently it happens sometimes," she went on. "When it does, they give us a free day. Viridia asked me at breakfast whether I wanted to walk down to the village with her, and I said yes." Her eyes flicked to Bal, there and gone, asking whether that was all right.

He smiled at her. Her hand came down from her ear.

"And then she said we should drag you along," Lora finished.

Bal glanced toward his room. One originator, at least, had not pulled anybody anywhere this morning. When he left her she had been asleep with her mouth a little open.

"I wouldn't mind going," he said, "as long as the two of you are with me. Let me go get changed."

Viridia looked pointedly at the uniform he had barely finished putting on. He went anyway.

In the bedroom he got out of the uniform a good deal more slowly than he had gotten into it, and into his own trousers and an old shirt from Saint Andre's, washed thin. He dug his backpack out of the chest, the same one he had carried off the orphanage roof, and emptied it.

Amelia could find him anywhere she liked. A note was pointless, really.

He tore a strip off the back of yesterday's rune notes and wrote on it with the stub of pencil from his kit.

*Gone to the village with Viridia and Lora. Back before curfew. Bal*

He read it over. It did not say much. He wanted her to hear it from him anyway, and not have to go looking. He folded the strip once and stood it up on the pillow beside her hand, and let himself out, easing the door shut on her.

---

They were not the only ones on the road. Students in their own clothes were strung out along it in twos and threes, and once a knot of them went past at a jog, as though New Spring might close if they did not hurry.

"Were either of you going to the village for something in particular?" Bal asked. "Or was it to get out of the castle?"

"Shops." Viridia patted a pocket of her vest, which clinked. "I brought some money in case I see something I want."

"What about you?"

Lora jumped. She had been walking on his other side, silent, for most of a mile, and her face said she had planned to go on that way the whole five.

"Oh. Um." She pushed her hands into her jacket pockets. "I'm hoping to look at the job and bounty boards. There's a faction I've been reading about. It's more of a guild, really. It only takes you once you've finished a certain number of requests. It's a good way to make money, but that isn't really the point." She was speeding up. "The point is, the guild has spellbooks you can't find anywhere else. Some of them you're not even allowed to see until you reach a certain rank. Can you imagine?"

Her hands had come out of her pockets partway through. She had not said so many words in a row since he met her, and she had not stumbled over a single one of them.

He caught Viridia's eye over Lora's head. Viridia's eyebrows went up and stayed up.

"Would you have to take the requests on your own?" Bal asked.

"No. You can register with the local Dawn faction as a team. They give every member a wooden card with the team's name on it. Each time you turn in a job or a bounty, the number on your cards goes up."

Bal's eyes went to Viridia again. She mouthed *yes* at him and did not bother to make it subtle.

"Okay," Bal said. "We'll stop by there, so you can join if you want."

"Oh! No, I'm not sure I'm ready," Lora said quickly. "I need to study more."

Bal laughed. "It's fine. We'll stop by anyway."

The road was long and then it kept on being long. A farmer's cart passed them with a dog asleep on a load of turnips. When the first roofs showed through the trees ahead, the sun was high, and breakfast felt like yesterday.

---

The village was called New Spring. The road brought them straight into its square. Shops stood on every side of it, with stalls set up in front of them on the cobbles. Most of the shops had signs hanging out over their doors that showed what was sold inside, a boot or a loaf or a pair of scissors. The ones without a sign had big glass windows instead. The square smelled of woodsmoke and horses and frying onions.

Bal followed the onions.

A man at a cart was selling hot rolls with meat baked inside, pulling them off a griddle with his bare fingers and shaking his hand after every one. Bal bought three. He handed one to Viridia and held one out to Lora.

"Oh. I can buy my own," Lora said.

"I already paid for it." Bal kept holding it out. "Why waste it?"

Lora looked at the roll, and then at him, and could not find the hole in that. She took it.

Viridia had no such scruples. She had already eaten a third of hers and was walking on.

The roll was the best thing Bal had eaten in his life, or at least that morning. The meat inside was peppery and too hot, and juice ran to his wrist.

In the far corner of the square a crowd had gathered, and the crowd was going *ooh*.

A vendor stood on an upturned crate behind a table of weapons laid out on a red cloth. He held a longsword above his head. As they watched, he gave the hilt a twist, and the blade burst into flame from guard to point. The fire ran up the steel with a soft, hungry sound, and the heat of it reached Bal's face from three rows back.

"Enchanted!" the vendor told them. "Never needs a match! And for those of you who prefer a little more snap, I've got some here that throw lightning!"

Bal edged up to the table and found the price tag tied to the nearest hilt. He read it and winced.

The flame climbed the blade once more. A sword that made fire was only a sword with something written on it. He was fairly sure he knew how to write. It would take some experimenting.

"Bal," Viridia said. "Come on."

---

They wandered.

Viridia stopped at nearly every window with anything bright in it. At one she lingered over a silver pin shaped like a leaf, and then left it. Lora walked beside Bal with her hands back in her pockets.

Partway around the square Viridia halted at a shop with a painted sign of a cat curled up inside a bird cage.

"Wait here," she said. "I'll only be a minute." She ducked in under the sign. A bell went somewhere inside.

Bal leaned on the post outside. Lora stood next to him.

"Are you having fun?" he asked.

"Yes," Lora said, and sounded surprised to hear it. "I am. You and Viridia are surprisingly easy to be around." She addressed the next part to the shop window, where a ferret in a basket was asleep on its back. "If you couldn't tell, I don't really do well with other people."

"That's okay," Bal said. "Not everyone does. I grew up in an orphanage. You get used to meeting new people there."

Lora gave him a sideways look over her glasses.

"So it doesn't bother you? That I'm shy?"

"Not at all," Bal said. "You are who you are, and I accept that."

Lora smiled. It was a real one, slow and wide, and it went up into her freckles.

"You really are beautiful when you smile," Bal told her.

Lora spun around and put her face into the corner where the post met the wall, and kept it hidden there. The tops of her ears had gone red.

Viridia had come out of the shop in time to hear it. She stood on the step with a cloth bag in her hand and smirked at Bal.

He shrugged. "I only tell the truth," he said. "What'd you buy?"

Viridia opened the bag and drew out a collar.

It was wide dark leather, big enough to fit a pony. She turned it inside out. A rune had been stitched into the lining in silver thread, small and tight and very neat.

"It's a size adjustment rune. The man said it'll shrink Hopper down to about the size of a dog. If I want to change her back, I push my magyk into the collar, and she's big again." She ran her thumb along the stitches. "That way she can come into the castle with me. If she wants to."

Bal leaned in to see the rune. Every corner of it was where it ought to be.

"That's a really thoughtful gift," he said.

Viridia tucked the collar away in its bag and swung the bag on its string.

---

Two shops along, while the girls were bent over a tray of hair combs in another window, Bal spotted the ring.

It sat on its own in a little velvet box at the front of a jeweler's display, a plain band of bright silver. A hand-lettered card beside it said the ring let its wearer move and shape the earth, briefly. The one drawback was that it had to be recharged after every use, and recharging it took a good deal of magykal energy.

Bal read the card twice. Still. One use was enough, if nobody saw it coming.

He checked the price. Then he checked where the girls were. Lora was holding a comb up to the light and Viridia was explaining to her why it was ugly.

He was in and out of the jeweler's in under a minute. The ring went into the right-hand pocket of his trousers, and when Viridia turned around he was back on the same cobble.

The bookstore was the last shop before the road out. A metal bell on a spring dinged over the door as they came in, and a stout woman behind the counter looked up from her ledger and told them to let her know if they needed anything.

Bal stood in the first aisle and argued with himself. He had been planning to look for books on strategy, and on the wars the originators' armies had fought. But the school had a whole library full of exactly that, two stories of it, and a librarian who was already setting things aside for him. Then he thought of the library in his dorm, and its long bare shelves. That room was going to waste.

He went to find the history section.

Lora and Viridia drifted off together toward the back, talking low. Bal took his time. After fifteen minutes he had four books, big ones, one each on the battles of Aren's, Kenmei's, Naisura's and Raizen's armies. On his way to the counter he passed a shelf of alchemy, and a book on it nearly as thick as it was wide, bound in green cloth with brass corners. He thought of Lora, and took it down.

At the counter he paid for all five. The alchemy book went straight into his backpack before anyone saw. He carried the four histories stacked in his arms and went to find the girls.

---

He heard them before he reached them. They were one aisle over, behind a tall shelf of advanced spellbooks, and they were talking about him.

Bal stopped where he was.

"You don't think anything's going on between Amelia and him?" Lora asked.

"Who knows? But from that 'you can keep her' thing she said, when we all ate together, I'd say she's not the jealous type."

"I guess."

A page turned. Then another. Bal stood very still, with the four books getting heavier in his arms.

"Do you mind, though?" Lora asked.

It was a while before Viridia answered. Long enough that Bal thought she might not.

"Honestly?" she said. "No. Where I grew up, your whole life was pretty much planned out for you the day you were born. Who you'd marry, even. Then I got my Tempus sygl." Bal heard her breathe out. "It was like I'd finally been set free. Hardly anyone in my village ever got a sygl. It had been decided, way back, that if anyone ever did, they'd be allowed to leave. No matter what." She was quiet again. "Joking around with Bal. Playing around. I can't really describe how that makes me feel."

Nobody spoke. Bal's eyes were on the spine of a book in front of him. *Wards and Their Failings.* He did not take in a word of it.

"What about you, though? He did call you beautiful."

A book shut with a snap, and slid back into its place on the shelf.

"I was teased a lot, growing up," Lora said quietly.

Bal shifted his grip on the histories and held his breath.

"My father and our weapons master both think I'm a prodigy with a rapier. When I've got one in my hand, it's honestly an extension of who I am." Her voice was steadier than it ever was when she talked to people. "Most girls wanted to play with dolls, or dress up. I loved my books and my rapier. Our town held tournaments now and then, to show off everybody's martial skills. Once I was old enough, my father begged me to enter. Girls weren't forbidden to. It was more of an oversight than anything. No girl had ever tried."

"So you did."

"So I did. Not a single other boy had ever seen me train. Neither had their fathers. They'd only heard about me secondhand. That was part of why they mocked me. They couldn't believe a girl could be that good. Especially a girl of thirteen." The page-turning had stopped on both sides. "War was a man's game, they kept telling me."

"So what happened?" Viridia asked.

Lora was smiling. He heard it in her voice, plain as anything.

"I beat them," she said. "Every single one."

A small, delighted noise came from Viridia.

"After that, the comments got worse. And the petty things. So I mostly stayed home. By day I trained, and by night I read. That's been my life for the last five years." Lora paused. "So it's taking some getting used to. Someone actually wanting to be around me."

---

Bal backed up.

He went quietly back down the aisle and around its end. When he judged he was far enough off, he let the top history slide off the stack.

It hit the floorboards flat, with a bang that rattled the stout woman's teacup in its saucer.

Lora's head came around the end of the spellbook shelf. She saw him crouched there reaching for the book, and smiled.

Bal looked up and gave her his most sheepish face.

"Sorry if I startled you."

"It's okay," Lora said. "We were only talking. Find everything you needed?"

"Yep." Bal stood with the stack. "Also, there's a surprise for you at the dorm."

Lora's eyes went over his four books, spine by spine. None of them was the surprise. Bal was very glad, right then, that the green book was sitting in the bottom of his backpack.

Viridia came around the shelf after her.

"Get everything?" she asked, eyeing the histories.

"Yep. But let's head over to the Dawn faction as soon as Lora's bought her book." He hitched the stack up. "I'm getting hungry again."

Lora had chosen a thick brown volume with a broken column stamped on the cover. She paid for it, and while the two of them headed for the door Bal swung the backpack off his shoulder and fed the histories in on top of the green one.

Just inside the door he stopped Lora.

"Here," he said. "Let me put your book in my bag." He turned to Viridia. "Your collar too. There's no point in both of you carrying things when I've got this."

Viridia handed the collar over gladly. Lora hesitated, with her new book held against her front.

Viridia gave her a light elbow.

"Come on," she whispered, loud enough for the whole shop. "It makes him feel useful."

Lora laughed. It was a bright, sudden sound, and it bounced off the shelves and made the stout woman look up. She held out the book.

Bal took it. As he did, he looked her in the eye.

"You don't have to be afraid of me," he said. "I promise. I'm nothing like anyone else you've known."

Then he pushed the door open, and the bell dinged, and he held it until the girls were through before he followed.

---

The Dawn Hall had its name in gold letters on a large and very ornate sign over a three-story wooden building at the top of the square. A deep covered porch kept the rain off anyone waiting. Inside were tables, where people sat talking over mugs and maps, and a long counter down the whole right-hand side.

Two women stood behind the counter in matching gray jackets with a sunrise stitched on the breast. Bal guessed it was a uniform.

"Welcome to the Dawn Hall," said the woman on the right as they came up. "My name is Soren, and this is Kim. What can we do for you today?"

Kim lifted a hand to them and went on sorting a box of papers.

Lora stepped forward before Bal could.

"Do you have any jobs posted?"

"We do." Soren took her time looking at the three of them. "But I haven't seen you in here before, so I have to ask. Are you a Dawn member?"

Lora's face fell.

"I thought you didn't have to be a member to take certain jobs," she said. "Like going out to collect herbs. That sort of thing."

"That's true," Soren said. "But why would you not join first, if that's your aim? I can promise you there are benefits to being a member."

Lora's head snapped up.

"Everyone I've talked to says you have requirements before you can join."

"As an individual member, that's true. We want our members to understand what they're getting into when they join the guild." Soren folded her hands on the counter. "For a team it's different. All we ask of a team is one job, taken right away when you join. Then the usual, one job or one bounty every week. That's not counting the guild dues every month, but we can take those out of your reward money if you'd prefer."

At the word *team*, Lora's head went down again, and stayed down.

Bal glanced at Viridia. She set her jaw and jerked her head at the counter. *Go.*

"I would like to enroll as a team." Bal stepped up to the counter.

Lora started to speak. Viridia put a hand on her arm and shushed her.

Soren's gaze went from one girl to the other and back, and stayed a second longer than it needed to. Then she gave Bal her attention.

"Very well," she said. "I take it these two will be the members of your party?"

Bal turned around. Lora and Viridia had their heads together and were arguing in furious whispers. Lora kept making small exasperated noises through her nose.

"Yep," Bal said to Soren. "They are."

Soren's mouth twitched. She reached under the counter and came up with three thin wooden cards, pale, each no bigger than Bal's palm.

"And the name you'd like for your team?" she asked.

---

Bal went over to the girls.

"It's okay, Lora. You want this," he said. "This way we can have your back when you go out on a job. Joining the Dawn is something you've wanted. Why are you fighting it?"

"Because I don't want you both to have to join," Lora said, "only because I can't get in on my own right now."

Viridia groaned at the rafters.

"That's what friends are for," she said, and raised a finger before Lora could start again. "You *are* our friend, right?"

"Yes," Lora said to the floor, in a very small voice.

"Then we're doing this." She swung toward Bal. "What did she say?"

Bal stayed with Lora.

"It really is okay," he told her. "I think it'll be fun. Besides, it gives me an excuse to hang out with both of you more."

Lora blushed to the roots of her hair. Bal tried Viridia instead.

"She wants a name for our team. I figured that was something we should decide together."

"Agreed." Viridia faced Lora. "Well? Any ideas?"

Lora saw she was not going to change either of their minds, and gave in.

"I like 'The Heirs,'" she said. "But that seems kind of boring. I only thought of it because we're all different sygls."

"It's not bad, though." Viridia tapped her chin. "What about..." She stopped, and the tapping stopped. "Sygls Best."

Lora and Bal both stared.

"I mean, if we're going to do this, we might as well aim for the top."

Lora's face changed. "I like it," she said, and meant it.

"Bal?" Viridia prompted.

"I think we've got our new team name."

On his way past her to the counter he reached out and gave Viridia's hand a quick squeeze.

"You did good," he said, low, and kept walking.

Soren was waiting with the cards.

"Have you decided?"

"We have," Bal said. "The consensus is Sygls Best."

Soren took up what Bal took for an ordinary pencil and wrote *Sygls Best* on the face of the first card, in an even, flowing hand. Where the point passed, the letters came up white and sank into the wood, clean and bright. She did the second card, and the third.

Bal glanced back. The girls had taken one of the tables and were sitting with their heads close, talking quietly.

"Soren, can I ask you something?" he said. "There's someone else I want to invite to the group. She isn't here right now. Could you make a card for her too?"

"Of course. I'll need her name." Soren reached under the counter for a fourth card. "Once you give it to her, tell her to push her magyk into it. That will attune it to her."

She lettered the team name on it. Then she flipped the card face down on the counter, and waited with the pencil over the bare wood.

"What's her name?"

Bal froze.

If he said Amelia, Soren would put it together in about a second. There was exactly one Amelia anyone had been talking about this week, and she was his familiar and a goddess. But Amelia was all he had. He had never heard her last name.

He hoped very hard that she would not mind.

"Amelia Norheart," he said.

Soren's eyebrow went up. Norheart was his own last name, and plainly she knew it. She considered him over the pencil. Bal held her gaze.

Not a word. She wrote it.

*Amelia Norheart.* The letters went white and settled into the grain, one after another, as if the wood had been waiting for her.

Bal slid the card into the left-hand pocket of his trousers, away from the ring, and carried the other three to the table.

---

He did his own card first, to show them how it worked. Viridia pushed her magyk into hers and it warmed in her palm, and her name came up white on the back, in the same flowing hand.

Lora's took a second longer. When *Lora Aegis* showed white on the back, she glowed.

"I can't believe it," she said. "I've wanted this for *so* long."

"Well, if you want to keep it," Bal told her, "you'd better go pick us a job. Remember, Soren said we have to take one the day we become official."

"Right!"

Lora was gone, hurrying across to the counter with her card held in both hands. Soren drew a thick stack of papers out from underneath, and the two of them bent their heads over it.

Bal dropped into the chair Lora had left. It was still warm.

"I like seeing her excited like this," he said. "It must have been tough where she grew up."

"It was." Viridia let it rest at that, and he did not ask. "I'm glad we did this. We're going to have to finish it fast, though. Free day or not, there's still a curfew."

"I'll get you both back in time," Bal said. "If we have to, I'll walk you back myself and come down and turn the job in on my own."

Viridia laughed out loud.

"You do realize I said *we* have a curfew? That includes you."

"Actually," Bal said, "I don't have one. I'm Lead Mage of the Blood sygl."

"But how can you be..." Viridia started, and stopped, and he watched her get there. "Of course. You're Lead Mage because you're literally the *only* mage."

She put her forehead down on the table.

"I wonder if anyone else has figured that out," she said to the tabletop. "All the other Lead Mages are fourth years. And here you are, the first Lead Mage in history that's a first year." She sat up again. "Ours is a jerk. He doesn't allow loud talking in the common room. No kissing. And he actually tries to make us keep a study time."

Bal heard very little after the second one.

"No kissing? What does he do if he catches you?"

"Five demerits. And five automatically means he gets to make you do any chore he wants. Clean the bathrooms. Muck out the stables. Anything, really."

Bal raised his right palm, solemn as an oath.

"I promise you can kiss anytime you want in my dorm," he told her. Then, with a smirk, he added, "As long as it's me."

Viridia's chin came up. Something mischievous got into her face and made itself at home.

"What if I wanted to kiss Amelia instead?"

Bal had no answer.

Lora saved him, bounding over with a sheet of paper. She saw Bal's face and looked to Viridia for an explanation.

"Later." Viridia pointed at the paper.

---

"Okay," Lora said, perching on the third chair. "They know we go to the school, so we don't actually have to do this today. But it has to be done and turned in by the end of the weekend."

Bal got his mouth working again. "What's the job?"

"There are some rare flowers growing about a day's walk from here. The local alchemist needs three of them." Lora turned the paper his way. *Aurora felis (3)*, it said at the top, in a cramped hand. "To get to them you have to go through some deep woods, and the alchemist is an older lady. She doesn't feel well enough to go out and get them herself. Soren says there might be wolves, and bears, and that sort of thing. But I told her we could handle it."

All her fluency drained out of her at once.

"Is it okay?" she asked. "What do you two say?"

"You picked a good one. I'm glad we can help her out."

Lora folded the paper in half and handed it to him.

Bal took it, surprised. Lora saw his face.

"You're the leader," she said. "Without you, I'd be on my own."

Bal checked with Viridia.

"She's right. If it wasn't for you, I'd never have thought about joining the Dawn. I might not have met Lora." She smiled at Lora, and Lora, shyly, smiled back. "So I agree. You're the leader."

Bal tucked the job into the backpack, in between Lora's present and Viridia's collar.

"If you say so," he said. "But like Viridia said, we've got to be home before curfew. Is there anything else either of you wants to do before we head back?"

Neither of them did.

"I think we're good," Viridia told him.

They went out past Kim and her box of papers, into the square. The afternoon had gone gold while they were inside, and the man with the meat rolls was scraping his empty griddle.

The road home was the same five miles it had been that morning, and the pack was heavier now, with six books and a collar in it.

Bal walked a little behind the two of them. His hand had found its way into his left pocket. It closed on the corner of the card, and the whole way out of New Spring the letters were there under his thumb, smooth in the grain. He did not know what she would say. He did not know whether she had ever wanted a last name, or what she would do with his.
