"It's going to happen tomorrow."

Bal's pencil skidded off the end of a word. He had been half listening to the fire for so long that a voice coming out of it startled him.

Amelia hadn't left the hearthrug. She was still facing the flames.

"What's going to happen tomorrow?" He let the pencil roll into the crease of the pad.

"That school rule you were talking about a while back."

On the couch, two heads lifted from the rune homework at once. Lora's pencil stopped mid-stroke. Viridia's book slid an inch down her lap before she caught it.

"I heard Mr. Porter talking to Mr. Winters about it earlier," Amelia said. "Quite excitedly. Apparently, when the originators called the teachers in this morning, he told them that neither Lora nor Viridia spends any time with her own sygl anymore." She turned her head at last, enough to find Viridia. "Plus you have a bloodwolf familiar. That's what did it, I think. Raizen wouldn't want anyone who carries his rarest inheritance gift walking around with something so clearly connected to me."

Viridia said nothing.

"The others left the decision to Raizen," she added. "Kenmei didn't care either way."

Kenmei was Lora's originator. Bal checked Lora's face, ready for it to hurt.

Lora had the end of her pencil against her lip. She considered the news about Kenmei for perhaps a second, and found nothing in it worth keeping, and dropped it.

That surprised him more than anything Amelia had said yet.

"So Raizen asked Mr. Porter if he had any suggestions," Amelia said, "and he did. Ban all students from socializing with students of other sygls, except in class, or when they're off the grounds." She paused. "He also said they should make you release Hopper."

That last was to Viridia.

Hopper was asleep across both girls' feet, collar-small, and she went on sleeping. One hind leg ran a little in a dream.

"Can they do that?" Viridia's voice went high on the last word.

"As long as your originator doesn't care, yes, they can," Amelia said. "Raizen gave him permission. So that will be something they'll talk to you about tomorrow."

---

The rumors had reached Bal weeks ago. He had hoped they would stay rumors.

Neither girl spoke. Lora's pencil had dropped to the page and dragged a long grey line straight through a rune she had spent ten minutes on. Viridia's hand had found Hopper's back and rested there, flat, in the thick red fur.

Tomorrow they could be told to choose: stop coming around Bal, or be expelled. And Viridia would have to send Hopper away besides.

Six weeks had done something to the four of them. Saturdays on the road, the bear, the snake, the stew at midnight on the square. Bal couldn't have named the day it happened, but somewhere in there nobody in this room had been willing to lose anybody else in it.

He reached over and closed the history book. It had been full of ideas about Toma, and Toma could wait. He squared it with the table's edge, though nobody had asked him to, and swung around on the cushion toward the girls.

He had known for weeks what he would say, if it came to this. How Viridia would react, he still couldn't guess.

He was surer about Lora, and that was the problem. Every single member of Lora's family bore the Waves sygl, and had since before she was born. Her father had served in Kenmei's army. Viridia's village was different. Sygls were rare enough there that one more strange one might not matter as much. Hopefully.

"There is a way to circumvent the rule," Bal said. "But it is not something to take lightly."

His voice was less sure than he wanted. He heard it himself. So did they; Lora's face went still, and Viridia sat forward and lifted her palm from Hopper's fur.

"What is it?" Lora asked.

Bal looked to Amelia.

Her face was toward the fire again. She was going to let him handle this on his own. He thought he understood why. If they said no, it would hurt less if she had never been part of the asking.

"They can only enforce the rule," he said, "because we all belong to different sygls. But you could become blood sygls."

Lora and Viridia checked each other's faces first, and then his.

"What exactly do you mean?" Viridia asked. "How would something like that even be possible?"

"As High Priest of the Blood sygl, I can convert someone. Change their sygl to blood." He made himself go slowly. "But only if they choose to. It can't be done to anyone who doesn't want it. This isn't something many people know."

"Would I lose my inheritance?" Viridia asked.

"No. You wouldn't." He hesitated, then gave her the rest of it, since it was true. "Actually, it's possible to gain another inheritance gift."

They were both quiet, weighing it. It scared him a little how badly he wanted to hurry them. He said the hard parts instead.

"Look, you need to think carefully about this. There's no way to reverse it. And you have to think about your families too. You both know how a lot of blood sygls get treated." He was in the villages in his head, in Thad's narrow room with the bad news. He pulled himself back out of it. "This isn't something to do lightly. I care about both of you, and I would absolutely hate to lose you. But I don't want either of you to be unhappy."

---

Viridia twisted the earth ring on her finger, around and back, thinking.

"Would anything change?" she asked.

"It's a bit different for every mage, from what Amelia has told me. Our sygl mainly gives us access to other magyks, ones our normal magyk doesn't reach. It's easier for me to control blood, even to use it as a weapon. You might lose some of your storm magyk." He remembered the lake glowing to the bottom, and the snake floating up belly first. "Honestly, I doubt it. Not with your inheritance. Lora will lose her waves magyk, though."

Lora waved that away with the pencil.

"I've hardly ever had a reason to call on that side of my magyk. So that isn't important." Then, more carefully: "The teachers can't retaliate, can they? If we do this?"

"Nope. I mean, what else could they do? You won't be in their sygl anymore." Bal leaned back. "And if a Lead Mage tries anything, I've got an in with the Blood Lead Mage."

He was extremely pleased with that one.

Viridia gave the ceiling a long-suffering stare. Lora laughed, a short helpless burst she tried to catch in her sleeve and couldn't.

When it had gone, nobody filled the quiet. The fire settled. Hopper stretched in her sleep until her claws ticked against the floor.

It was Viridia who broke it.

"We heard what you told Amelia in the pass," she said.

Bal's ears went hot to the tips. Amelia kept her face to the fire. Her shoulders did something small under her dress that might have been a laugh she was keeping to herself.

Nobody asked him to say anything about it. He did not try.

"I can't be sure how my father will feel," Lora said. She was looking straight at Bal, and her chin was up, and her voice did not hedge anywhere. "But I know he loves me. My family will stay by me. I'll do it."

"Yeah, I'll do it too. I can't have them trying to take Hopper from me," Viridia said.

Nobody said a thing. Lora blinked at her. Even Amelia came around from the fire for that one.

"Hey. I thought it was obvious I didn't want them taking you from me either." Viridia elbowed Lora, lightly. "Especially Miss Book Smart here."

Lora went pink to the edges of her freckles and did not mind it one bit. Bal was glad to see it. The girl he had met in Warfare class would have hidden in a book until the conversation was over. This one sat up a little straighter.

"Okay. So, last chance," Bal said. "Is this something both of you want to do?"

"Yes," said Lora.

"Yes," said Viridia, at the same moment.

Over at the fire, Amelia was smiling a little.

"Okay, then," Bal said. "Let's do it."

---

He told them to come and sit on the tabletop facing him, and went to fetch his dagger.

The bedroom was dark and smelled of his own clean laundry. He found the dagger by feel, next to the lamp, and stood with it a minute in the dark.

He had never done this before. Amelia had taught him, months ago, with her fingernail drawing a line across his palm. He went over it again, step by step, from the cut to the end.

If any of it dripped on that table, who would get it out of the wood? Did the dorm clean up blood by itself, the way it put rooms where you wanted them, or would it be him on his knees with a rag and cold water?

He went back out.

The girls sat shoulder to shoulder on the tabletop, close, their knees almost touching his once he sat. Hopper was awake now, her muzzle resting across Viridia's thigh, watching the dagger.

"Hold out your left hands," he said. "Palm up."

They did. Lora's Waves sygl, Viridia's Tempus sygl, in the middle of each palm. He had seen those hands across a hundred tables and had not thought twice about what was on them.

He meant to change one thing from her instructions. He would do them both at once.

He cut his own left palm first, and deep. It stung and then mostly stopped stinging. Blood welled up and began to pool in the cup of his hand, dark against the sygl.

He offered the dagger hilt first. His hand was not quite steady, and both girls saw. Nobody mentioned it.

Viridia took it, and drew it across her palm without a sound, and passed it on. Lora cut herself with a neat, quick stroke. Then she wiped the blade on her trouser leg and laid it aside, out of everybody's way.

Three left palms, open, bleeding.

Bal reached for his blood.

It rose from his palm when he called it, and he split it into two thin streams, red threads in the firelight, and sent them over to the girls' waiting palms. Where his blood touched theirs, theirs gave way. It retreated into the cuts. It went back so far that in a breath both palms were bare, no blood and no sygl. Their hands were clear and white from the heel to the fingers. The only red on them was his, sinking into the cuts.

He pushed it deeper.

The cut in his palm was nothing. He hardly noticed it. The pushing was the work. It had to be steady and it had to be gentle, both at once, in two people at the same time, and those two people were Lora and Viridia. Sweat ran down behind his ear. He did not wipe it. He did not dare take his mind off either thread for long enough to lift a hand.

It went in. Through it, he knew how far there still was to go. He followed it down until something in each of them pushed back, warm and dense and theirs, the place where their magyk lived.

Bal grunted and leaned on it, and his blood rolled over theirs and took it.

The cuts closed. They shut together, both of them, and pinched off his blood in midair. The two red threads fell back into his hand.

On the two white palms, something was coming up from underneath. It was faint at first, a shadow of a line, then another line beside it. It darkened while the three of them watched, without anybody saying a word, Viridia bent so close over her own hand that her nose nearly touched it. Lora's mouth was open. Her glasses had slid to the end of her nose and she let them stay.

Some minutes later the marks on their palms were the blood sygl, as dark as Bal's own.

---

Amelia had watched it all from the hearthrug, her knuckles white in her lap. When the cuts closed, he had seen her lean toward them, hoping. Now her eyelids came down. Her mouth curved.

"I can feel you now."

Bal was on his feet. He hauled both girls into his arms and kissed them.

"Thank you," he said. His voice cracked halfway, and he had to start it again. "If I'd lost you, I wouldn't have known what to do."

Viridia muttered something into his shirt that he did not catch. Lora's forehead was pressed hard against his collarbone.

The three of them squeezed onto the couch with Amelia. She had left the rug to claim the end of it, and for a while it was enough to sit there. The fire popped. Lora kept her left hand open in her lap, palm up, and could not stop reading it.

Then Amelia did something Bal had not expected. She rose and stood before the girls.

"I'm making you priests," she said. "Now. It will give you more authority over my followers. And it will be good for Bal to have two priests he can trust completely."

Lora sat bolt upright. Viridia, for once in her life, had no comeback.

"Bal." Her attention had moved to him. "I'll call a meeting of the originators and formally declare war on Raizen. It will limit some of the things he might try. Making another High Priest, for one. And it has to be now, before word of this spreads in the morning."

She gathered all three into a hug and held on hard. When she stepped back, she was gone.

Hopper padded over from the table, circled once on the spot where Amelia had stood, and lay down on it with a groan of deep contentment.

---

The room was much quieter.

In the morning, Bal thought, Mr. Porter would carry his new rule into the hall at breakfast, and he would bring it to their table, and he would find out it was too late. Bal discovered he was looking forward to breakfast.

Both girls were watching him. Hopper had gone back to sleep.

"You know," he said, "I've only got one bedroom."

He did not mention that he could have had another before he reached the door. The dorm would have made it for him gladly.

The girls traded a glance. They grinned, both of them, and then they were facing him again.

"Then let's go to bed," they said together.
