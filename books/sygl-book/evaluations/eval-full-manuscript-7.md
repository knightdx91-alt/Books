## 1. Did the four story fixes work?

### Fix 1: the climax conversion (Ch.28 plant, Ch.29 choice). **WORKS.**

**The plant (Ch.28 L203–213) is the best of the twenty insertions.** "It pulled." stands as a one-line paragraph. "His blood leaned into hers. It wanted in." The detail that "the wanting ran back up the lash to his hand before he had decided a thing" is physical and specific. It reuses the existing Ch.19 mechanism ("his blood rolled over theirs and took hold"), so it adds no new rule. Bal then pulls back and "kept the edge of the whip off her skin". That is a character beat, not a lecture.

**The recall (Ch.29 L155–157) makes the core moment a choice:** "He knew that pull… He let it pull, and he went with it." The conversion of Akora is no longer luck. Toma's conversion is still an accident, because Bal does not know about the life bond. The premise ("converts the enemy High Priest by accident") holds.

**Against Ch.30.** "Bal admitted the elemental was a mystery to him, and Amelia confessed it was one to her too" is about the *elemental*. Neither of them knew blood could make one. The pull is something Bal felt. Amelia comments only on the swing ("seen that swing coming a mile off"). **No contradiction.**

**Against Ch.29 L215, "He acted on instinct, against Toma's elemental."** The choice is made in a split second, with no understanding and no plan to convert anyone, which is instinct. Amelia also cannot see inside his head. **No contradiction.**

**One ambiguity (R7, recommended).** "She might have let it." (Ch.28 L203) invites the Hostile reader's question: did Amelia stage the cut to show him the pull? If she did:
- she knew more than Ch.29/30 admit;
- the discovery becomes hers, which undercuts Fix 2.

Cut the sentence. Her "seen that swing coming a mile off" then reads as bravado over a hit he really landed, which is better for both fixes.

### Fix 2: Bal's wins credited. **WORKS. One new frame repeat (F4).**

- **S2a (Ch.23):** the girls wearing the charged shirts unasked is shown, not told. It is good. It also created **F2** (below).
- **S2b (Ch.25):** "The cup never crossed my mind." is credit in Lora's own book-bound terms, and the best of the credit beats. "Anything he said back would have sounded like bragging… it stayed warm in him for miles" is warm and in voice. "From Lora, that said plenty." is a small gloss; it is optional to cut.
- **S2c (Ch.27):** "The plan had been his." It is placed just before the boom, which gives it dramatic irony: "he had walked them both deep into Raizen's country to do it." It works. Hostile footnote: "Viridia had bet her life on a cup of brandy" is slightly inexact, since she drank expecting to die rather than gambling. Optional.
- **S2d (Ch.28):** the cost of non-interference. "He turned his face to the curtains so she would not have to watch him want it." This is an underline-grade sentence and lands the cost without a word of complaint. It sits just after the existing "He saw what it cost her… She could see what it cost him to say it, too." The two now double up slightly on "cost". It is acceptable, and the new paragraph is the stronger of the two.
- **S2e (Ch.29):** the rules argument is credited, and Amelia's teaching is kept. **Two problems:**
  - "That part had been his" rebuilds S2c's "The plan had been his." two chapters earlier. That is the same self-crediting frame in the same position, and the Critic will hear it.
  - "and Toma had taken it" can read as Toma *stealing* the credit. **F4** fixes both.

**Net effect.** Across Ch.23–29 Bal now makes the shirts, the cup, the plan and the argument, and he pays a felt cost. He reads as a protagonist who acts, not one who is rescued.

### Fix 3: Lora and Viridia overrule Bal on practical points. **WORKS. Two seams (F1, F3).**

- **P22, Lora's maid argument:** "I don't have an answer that lasts past the soup." This is pure Lora: hedged with people, fluent and bossy on a plan. It also retroactively explains the maid/lady staging. **But** the narration that follows contradicts Bal's own plan a few lines above. L177 has him already cast as "the lowly manservant", yet L199 says "Bal had pictured the three of them riding in as near enough equals." → **F1.**
- **P23a, Viridia and the fire:** this is the best of the four. The tinker who "thought we were just friendly" is a specific village frame of reference, dry and funny, and it sets up Ch.26's "We can't risk a fire" as a lesson learned.
- **P23b, Lora on posture:** "You look like you stole that horse and you're sorry about it… He isn't ashamed. He's bored." It is strong. **But** Ch.25 L247 still has Bal leaving through the same gate with "Bal hunched in the saddle and studied his horse's ears", which is the exact posture Lora corrected. A Hostile or Devoted reader catches it. → **F3.**
- **P24, Viridia's "Watch for the cart":** good comic timing. It uses her lady role as tradecraft and changes no beat.

**Do they read sharper? Yes.** The two are now differentiated by kind of competence, not only by temperament:
- Lora knows how noble houses work.
- Viridia knows how a village watches the road.

There is no relationship friction, as ruled.

**Watch item (not a fault yet):** four "correct Bal → Bal complies in one line" beats fall in three chapters: "Maid it is." / "He let the wood fall where he stood." / "Bal straightened in the saddle." / "Bal watched for the cart." Each is in a different register and the last is a deliberate comic button, so this is not a device today. A fifth would make it one. I recommend adding the shape to the at-risk list as spent.
