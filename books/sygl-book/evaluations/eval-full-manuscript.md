## 4. Voice consistency, arcs, relationships

**Voice, early vs. late:** one narrator throughout: plain, warm, joking under pressure. Ch.1–8 carry more stage business (grins, towing, a hand flexed twice) and more explanation ("That made sense, Bal thought. A lot of sense."). Ch.21–29 are tighter and more physical. The drift is improvement, not inconsistency. Two shared verbal habits cross the cast: "honestly" (31 uses, spread over Amelia, Viridia, Lora, Bal and the narrator; the source's own word, 28 uses) and "plainly" (narrator). Take the narrator's two "honestly" out (Ch.15 L80, Ch.24 L264) so the word stays with the characters.

**Bal:** from the boy nobody claimed to the one who claims and protects. The arc reads cleanly: "I like to read" → "What can I do to help?" → "We will not respond in kind." → "I just want to live my life with my girls." His violence is justified, carries no guilt, and the Sean beat is noted once ("That surge of anger had surprised him."). As ruled.
**Amelia:** confident, playful, protective, manipulates no one. The Hopper bond is framed as a kindness Bal admires. "Just give me a little time" → the card → "I feel useless" → "Damn their war" → the chalice. Strong.
**Viridia:** needling to sincere in clean steps: the diary and fork, the bookstore confession, Bran, the poison, the still hair. Her best chapter is Ch.25–26.
**Lora:** hedging to exact is the best-executed voice split in the book. Her *romantic* thread is the quietest of the three. She never says she wants Bal; it's carried by the juggling-talk "We heard" (Ch.19), the kiss over the poison idea (Ch.24) and her hand on his cheek (Ch.26). That is enough for the genre and needs no new beat.
**The four-way:** earned and warm. Amelia and the girls get their own bond: "What if I wanted to kiss Amelia instead?", the swim, the shared letter "*My loves*", the hug at the temple door. Nothing reads as coerced. Every choice is voiced ("Yes," said Lora. "Yes," said Viridia, at the same moment.).

## 8. The ending and the closing line
