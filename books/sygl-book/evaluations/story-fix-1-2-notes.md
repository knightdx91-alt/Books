## FIX 1: the climax shouldn't read as luck

### S1a, Ch.28, roof training (after "...and they kept at it till it held.")
> Once, with the sun straight overhead, his whip got through her guard. She might have let it. The edge opened her forearm a hand's length above the wrist, and for one breath his blood lay in the cut with hers.
>
> It pulled.
>
> His blood leaned into hers. It wanted in. Every inch of the whip wanted to close around what it touched and keep it, and the wanting ran back up the lash to his hand before he had decided a thing.
>
> He tore the whip loose and called it home. His palm shut on it.
>
> Amelia's arm had closed already. She shook it out and told him she had seen that swing coming a mile off.
>
> He didn't ask her about the rest. He couldn't have shaped the question yet. He went at her again, and for what was left of that day he kept the edge of the whip off her skin.

Why: this plants the mechanism the climax uses, his blood taking hold of other blood (the Ch.19 conversion
reads "his blood rolled over theirs and took hold"). It only uses what the scene already has: Amelia, the
whip and "nothing he did could hurt her". She notices only the swing, so Ch.30's "a mystery to her too"
still holds. No spell name and no rule is stated. The paragraph that follows ("On the second evening the
stone...") now stands as its own paragraph.

### S1b, Ch.29, at the core (between "...and he pressed." and "He pushed it in.")
> The core pulled at it.
>
> He knew that pull. He had felt it on Amelia's roof, in the cut on her arm, and he had torn his blood free of it without knowing what it was. He understood it no better here, and he was past saving anything. He let it pull, and he went with it.

Why: Bal recognises the pull and chooses to give in to it, so the turn is his choice and not a reflex. He
still doesn't understand it, and he chooses only about the elemental, so Toma's conversion still surprises
everyone. Amelia's "he acted on instinct" to Aren is still fair, because Bal chose in a split second with
no plan to convert anyone. Every original sentence of the climax is kept.

## FIX 2: Bal's own contributions, and one cost of Amelia's non-interference

### S2a, Ch.23, road to Londra (after "She had unfastened two of them before the canyon had dropped behind them.")
> Under all those buttons she had on the green shirt, with every one of his runes in it charged. Lora had hers on under her maid's dress. He hadn't had to ask either of them.

Why: the girls trust the arrow-rune shirts (Ch.18), shown through what they do. The shirts are still never
tested on the page (author ruling); Viridia's is still the green one.

### S2b, Ch.25, leaving Londra (after "They kept to a walk until the walls had dropped behind a rise.")
> Lora let her horse drift back beside the black.
>
> "I spent that whole hour at my table going through the book in my head," she said, low. "Every page. Hunting for an antidote I already knew wasn't in it." She was watching Viridia's back up ahead. "The cup never crossed my mind."
>
> She left it at that. From Lora, that said plenty.
>
> Anything he said back would have sounded like bragging. He rode beside her and kept it to himself, and it stayed warm in him for miles.

Why: the purification rune saved Viridia, and the one expert in the party credits it, in her own
book-bound terms (the chapter already says there was no antidote). It is short and specific to Lora,
not general praise. It sits in the walk before the gallop and changes no beat.

### S2c, Ch.27, at six o'clock (after "...would be dying about now, if Lora's book was right.")
> The plan had been his. He had found it over a map at Xavier's table, while Amelia sat next door with a book so that no one could accuse her of helping. Lora had brewed the poison and Viridia had bet her life on a cup of brandy, and he had walked them both deep into Raizen's country to do it.

Why: the Londra plan reads as Bal's (Ch.22). It also credits the girls' parts and recalls Amelia keeping
out of it ("I'll find something to read"). It is placed just before the thunder, so the ambush lands on
something he made.

### S2d, Ch.28, after "Should I find them, and bring them home?" / "No" (after "The quiet went on, and he let it.")
> Amelia could have had them home before supper. She could have managed it in less time than it had taken him to drink the water. He had told her no, and if she asked again tomorrow he would tell her no again, and every part of him that was not busy with the war wanted the word back. He turned his face to the curtains so she would not have to watch him want it.

Why: the cost of non-interference, felt on the page by Bal, through the water he has just drunk and the
curtains already in the room. His no stands, and she manipulates nothing.

### S2e, Ch.29, the rules exchange (after "...walked him through each rule till he had them by heart.")
> But she had stood at his shoulder through that whole exchange with her mouth shut, because she had to. She could teach him the rules. She could not argue them for him. That part had been his, and Toma had taken it.

Why: the source credits Amelia's teaching ("Bal had Amelia to thank for it"), and that stays. This adds
that he made the argument himself, against a High Priest, while she couldn't speak. The no-interference
rule is the one Toma has just stated, so no new rule is added.
