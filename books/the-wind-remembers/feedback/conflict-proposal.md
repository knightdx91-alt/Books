# Conflict proposal — The Wind Remembers (v2)

**Status: PROPOSED, awaiting author sign-off.** Nothing here is canon. The
architect must not build an outline against this until the author approves it.
`research/source-notes.md` remains the only binding canon.

**Settled:** standalone.

**v1 is superseded.** It proposed that the man who put Elizabeth on the wheel and
the man who can sever souls from it were the same person. The author rejected the
coincidence, and the rejection is right for a second reason: v1 also insisted the
wheel's administration must never be staged, and then gave the wheel an
administrator. A person who *drafts* souls is an officer of the wheel. That is the
thing a standalone cannot have.

---

## The correction: there is no binder

Nobody put Elizabeth on the wheel. **The wheel took her**, impersonally — which is
the author's own note, verbatim: *"possibly her ability grew to the point where it
qualified her as exceptional and the wheel determined her as important."*

The wheel does not waste what is rare. Something exceptional surfaced in a girl in
Victorian England, and it was kept in circulation. No decision, no decider, no
crime. Weather, not a government.

That leaves **exactly one antagonist**, which is what a standalone can carry.

## The antagonist

A man who can cut a soul free of the wheel. **He is not a threat — he is a mercy.**

Some old souls are tired. A soul on its fortieth life that burned to death forty
times will burn again, and will remember it every cycle. He offers rest, and he
has a real argument.

**His goal is mercy. His sin is that he does not ask.** He severs people who never
chose it — which is the wheel's sin exactly. Elizabeth was not asked. Knight was
not asked at three years old. Two forces that both decide for you, and Mandy is
the first person in the story handed a real choice. **The theme is consent.**

## How he reaches this story — no coincidence required

**He comes for Knight.**

Knight is precisely the soul his work exists for: centuries deep, every life
remembered, every battlefield still in him. By the severer's lights, a man that
worn should have been let go a long time ago. He is not wrong.

Knight refuses — because of Mandy.

So the severer turns to her: *free her, and he has no reason to stay.* The
leverage **is** the romance. There is no contrivance in it; he wants Knight for a
legible reason and reaches Mandy through him, the way pressure actually travels.

And it forces the climax the notes were always pointing at: **implacable will
against a man offering rest**, with Mandy as the reason Knight keeps breathing —
and Mandy's own choice, made for herself, as the thing that settles it.

## What v1 got right and this keeps

- **Severance as mercy, not menace.** The threat "you will never be reborn" cannot
  land on a woman who does not know she has past lives. The offer *"you could stop
  hurting"* lands on Knight immediately, in chapter one of his presence.
- **Knight's gift is the climax mechanism.** Once he commits, nothing on earth
  stops him while he still breathes. A loaded gun, not a superpower; the book is
  which way it points.
- **"Balance" means something.** Souls must be able to leave, or the wheel is a
  prison; if leaving becomes easy, whatever the wheel is for collapses. The climax
  restores *the ability to choose* rather than winning a fight. Mandy — newest
  soul, not yet hurt — argues for staying; Knight argues for leaving; they trade
  positions by the end.

## The author's three side-note questions, answered without a binder

- **Why is a soul reborn?** Because the wheel keeps what is rare. Impersonal, and
  chilling for being impersonal — nobody chose her, she simply was not wasted.
- **How often?** Irregularly, and nobody knows why. Knight has had many lives;
  Mandy is on her first return. Left unanswered on purpose, under discipline 2.
- **The overarching conflict?** The above.

## Standalone disciplines

1. **Never stage the wheel's administration.** No council, no clerks, no officers,
   no audience with management — and now, no binder either. The moment the
   institution has staff, it has a sequel.
2. **Answer "why me," not "why the wheel."** Mandy never learns why the wheel
   exists. She learns what being kept by it costs, and she chooses. Characters get
   closure; the cosmos does not.
3. **Deliver every reveal through the mechanism already in the notes.** Memories
   trickle back through dreams, faster the more she embraces her past. No
   investigation subplot, no archive, no research montage — the past resolves
   inside the romance instead of running beside it.

## Two craft calls that follow

**POV / dramatic irony.** Opening on Elizabeth's death means the reader already
knows more than Mandy. Ch.1 is a promise that this is a dramatic-irony book, not a
whodunit — so give Knight enough POV that the reader knows what he is early, and
let the tension be *when she finds out and how she takes it*. Running a mystery
after that opening wastes the opening.

**Flashback budget.** Present day is the spine. Victorian returns a handful of
times after Ch.1; Knight's medieval life fewer and shorter. Equal flashback real
estate for him is two books inside one cover.

## A possible ending (author's call)

Hands are assigned only for a soul's **first** reincarnation — so Knight's job
ends. The wheel releases him and points him at the next stranger.

**He stays anyway.** That is what implacable will is for: assignment becomes
choice, and the man conscripted at three years old finally does something because
he picked it.
