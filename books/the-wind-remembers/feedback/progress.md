## The seed, in one line

Elizabeth, a Victorian Englishwoman who can call the wind, dies calling it
harder than she ever has — and is placed on the wheel of reincarnation. Her
first reincarnation is Mandy, present day, who knows only that she is drawn to a
period she has no reason to love. Knight, an old soul out of the Middle Ages
whose distinguishing trait is a will nothing can stop, was assigned as her
"hand" — a watcher for a soul's first time around — and has been three years
ahead of her since the day she was born.

## Blocking the architect

Three of the author's own side-note questions are unanswered, and the last of
them is the book's spine:

1. **What is the overarching conflict?** The author's working idea is someone
   who can *sever a soul's connection to the wheel*, with **Balance** at stake.
   Until this is settled there is no antagonist and no outline.
2. **Why is a soul reborn?** This is the metaphysics the ending has to pay off.
3. **What determines how often a soul is reborn?**

Plus one smaller, non-blocking choice: Mandy works at **a hotel** (as first
written) or is **a museum curator** (the author's own later preference — it puts
her unexplained pull toward the period in her daily life). The notes lean
museum. Everything else in the notes reads as settled.
